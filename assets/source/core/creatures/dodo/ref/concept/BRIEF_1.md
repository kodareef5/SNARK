You are painting the concept design of a game creature. Work only in the current folder.

## What the pictures are for

A survival game needs its dodo: a slow, dim, flightless bird that waddles about beaches and grassland, gets knocked out and tamed, is picked up and carried in a player's arms, lays eggs and runs away when hit. It is the first creature made for the game, so every later creature will be held to it.

Your pictures are the design. After you, a modeller builds the 3D mesh straight from your orthographic views, three animators work on a skeleton fitted to those views, and a texture painter paints from your colours and markings. So the views must be exact enough to model from, consistent enough that front, side, back and top describe one bird, and beautiful enough to put on a studio wall.

It is a caricatured dodo, not the real extinct bird, and it must be our own design. It must not copy any existing game's dodo. Do not search the web, and do not look at any pictures of other dodos. Work from this description. In your image prompts, describe the bird in your own words and do not name any game, studio or franchise.

## The bird, in words

**What makes it a dodo at a glance (keep all of these):**

1. **The beak.** Oversized, deep and heavy, laterally flattened, with a strong hook at the tip that curls down past the end of the lower beak. It dominates the head.
2. **A bare face set in a feathered hood.** Bare skin runs from the base of the beak back past the eye. The crown, the back of the head and the nape stay feathered, so the face looks set into a hood with a ragged edge.
3. **The silhouette.** A fat pear or egg of a body, widest at the belly and rump, tilted chest-up. A thick neck rises from the top front of the body in a gentle S and holds the big head up above the chest. Short, stout legs. Small stubby wings folded on the flanks. A tuft of tail feathers at the top of the rump.
4. **The dim look.** A small eye set high at the back of the bare face, under a heavy brow. It stares blankly and sleepily, mildly grumpy. Nothing about the head looks alert or clever.
5. **Overlapping feathers in rows** over the body, readable as a texture up close.

**Our own choices (paint these exactly; they are what make it ours):**

- **Beak.** Deep and heavy, with a raised ridge along the top that arcs into a defined, slightly pointed hook overhanging the lower beak by about 2 cm. Near its base the upper beak has three shallow grooves running across it, like rings. The nostril is a long narrow slit low on the side of the upper beak, about a third of the way from the face. The line of the mouth curves down at the back into a fleshy corner, so the bird always looks faintly put out. The beak is one colour; its grooves are a little darker and the hook a little paler and worn smooth.
- **Face.** Bare, pebbled skin (fine rounded bumps, like a turkey's or vulture's head skin), with a few loose folds below the eye and at the corner of the mouth. Not deeply wrinkled.
- **Brow.** A heavy, smooth, leathery ridge of bare skin that runs from the base of the beak back over the eye and overhangs it like a shelf. It is a different colour from the face.
- **Eye.** Small, about 2.2 cm across. Pale straw-yellow iris, small black pupil. The upper lid hangs half closed; the lower lid is fleshy. Vacant and sleepy.
- **Hood.** Dense, short, velvety feathers over the crown, the back of the head and the nape. At the front the hood comes down to a blunt point on the forehead, between the brows, just above the beak. Around the face its edge is ragged with small tufts. At the back it lengthens into a short shaggy mane of loose feathers that falls over the top of the neck. No thin plumes, no spot-tipped feathers, no crest standing up anywhere on the head or back.
- **Throat.** Under the lower beak, a patch of bare, finely pleated skin from the chin back to the top of the neck, like a soft pouch. Same colour as the brow.
- **Body feathers.** Medium, rounded feathers in loose overlapping rows: neat on the breast and back, but softer and less regular than scales, like shingles on an old roof. On the flanks, the thighs and under the tail they grow longer and shaggier, forming loose feather "trousers" over the thighs that hang to the top of the scaly leg, and a ragged lower edge to the belly. The outline of the body is soft and slightly ragged, never smooth.
- **Back pattern.** Narrow pale bars run across the back, the rump and the tail, slightly chevron-shaped, fading out down the flanks. The breast and belly are plain.
- **Wing.** A small rounded stub folded on the middle of the flank, covered in body-coloured feathers, with three or four longer stiff flight feathers that stick out behind it as short pale "fingers", curving slightly upward. The fingers are the pale accent of the wing.
- **Tail.** A short loose curl of six to eight broad soft feathers at the top of the rump, curling up and forward over the back. Body-coloured with the pale bars. Not a tall plume, not a fan, not purple or lavender.
- **Legs and feet.** Stout, short, scaly legs coming out of the feather trousers: large plates down the front, small pebbly scales behind. Three thick toes forward and one behind, with long, strong, dark horn claws.

## What must hold

- **The views agree.** Measure them: crown top, eye centre, beak tip height, top of the back, lowest belly edge, wing top and bottom, tail curl top and the ground must sit at the same rows in side, front and back (within 2% of the standing height, about 20 pixels). Widths in the front view must match widths in the top view, and lengths in the side view must match lengths in the top view (within 3%). Every marking, feather row, bar, tuft and colour must be the same in every view where it shows. Draw horizontal guide lines across the four views side by side and look at them. If a view disagrees, fix it or make it again; do not stretch a drawing to fit.
- **Flat neutral light.** Even, soft, frontal light with only gentle form shading. No strong key light, no rim light, no cast shadows, no ground shadow, no reflections. A texture painter must be able to read true colours from it.
- **A plain background.** One flat even colour, light warm grey (about RGB 214, 212, 206), the same in every view. No ground plane, no props, no scenery, no vignette.
- **No text** in any of the pictures: no labels, no numbers, no signatures, no watermarks. The region maps and check sheets are the only exceptions, and even they need no text.
- **Symmetric.** The bird is the same on both sides.
- **No added parts.** No plumes, crests, horns, spurs, wattles, combs, armour or accessories beyond what is described.

## What excellent looks like

Concept art a studio would put on its wall. A crisp, confident silhouette that says "dodo" in one glance even as a black shape 16 pixels tall: a round body, the thick neck at the front, the big forward head with its hook, the stubby stance. Up close, a design with its own clear language: the ringed, ridged beak with its sharp hook, the pebbled rose face under a heavy ochre brow, the half-lidded straw eye, the dark velvet hood with its shaggy nape mane, the shingled slate feathers going shaggy at the flanks, the pale bars on the back, the pale wing fingers and the barred tail curl. Realistic, matte surfaces on a slightly caricatured shape. Feathers drawn as a believable texture that a painter could follow, not noise. Someone who knows game dodos should recognise the creature immediately, and on a second look see that it is its own bird.

Look at every picture you make, compare the views against one another and against this description, and keep going until they agree and you would stand behind them.
