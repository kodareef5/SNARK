# A second opinion on the dodo's form

You are shown `form_beside_concept.png`: on the left, the painted concept of a game creature (a dodo, three-quarter, side and front); on the right, the 3D model built to it, rendered in plain grey with no paint yet. Its feather cards (the hood's edge, the mane, tufts over the body, the trousers, tail and wing feathers) are cut out by a plain test picture, not the final one.

The model is meant to be the concept in the round: a stranger should say "a dodo" at once, and the model should look like the painting from every angle. Paint will come later and will carry the feathering, the colours and the skin's detail; judge the form.

Look as a stranger would, then as a sculptor would, and write, in plain sentences:

1. What a stranger sees in the grey model, in a sentence or two.
2. Where the model's form differs from the painting, most important first: which part, which view, how (bigger, smaller, higher, rounder, flatter, missing), and about how much against the bird's size. Only form and silhouette, not colour or surface detail.
3. Anything that looks broken or wrong as a 3D object: an odd crease, a part that floats or sinks, a shape that reads as something else.

Keep to what is there: do not propose new features the painting does not have. Write your answer to `review_1.md` in this folder, under 600 words. Do not change any other file.
