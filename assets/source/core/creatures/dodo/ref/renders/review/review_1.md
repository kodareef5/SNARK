1. What a stranger sees

The grey bird reads as a dodo most clearly in side and three-quarter views: a heavy round body, small wings and a large hooked bill. From the front, the swollen cheeks and long central breast make it look less like the painting's sleepy, compact bird.

2. Where the form differs, most important first

These are approximate comparisons after allowing for the grey birds being shown larger. “Bird height” means crown to ground.

The face is too lumpy beneath the eyes, especially in three-quarter and front views. The painting has relatively flat cheek areas tapering towards the bill and throat; the model has rounded knobs beside the bill, almost like a muzzle. Their excess projection looks roughly 1–2% of bird height. The brows also read as straight projecting shelves above tiny exposed eyes, whereas the painting's brow and eyelid form a softer, closely fitted droop. This changes the expression before paint enters the comparison.

The tail loses much of the painting's rounded fan. In the side view, its visible envelope is roughly a third smaller relative to the bird, a difference of about 5–7% of bird height in the fan's dimensions. The concept's feathers sweep backwards, curl upwards and overlap into a substantial rounded tuft. The grey feathers read as a few straighter, widely separated blades, particularly in three-quarter view. The test cutout makes their exact edges provisional, but the overall spread and curvature still differ.

The wings are too deep from top to bottom. In side and three-quarter views, the grey wing forms a large oval plate; the painting's wing is a shallower, compact overlapping mass. The difference looks around 20–25% of wing depth, or 3–4% of bird height. The grey tips also spread farther down and away from that mass instead of gathering into the concept's short rounded end.

From the front, the model's lower neck continues into a pronounced, narrow breast bulge over the belly. The painting broadens more evenly into one rounded chest. The grey bulge remains distinct through much of the torso, making the body read as a central pouch with masses beside it. Lighting exaggerates this, so its projection cannot be reliably measured here. The overall body width and side-view belly silhouette are otherwise fairly close.

The bill's forward reach looks slightly short in the side and three-quarter views: approximately 10% of its own length, or 2% of bird height. Its hook and overall depth are already close; the main difference is reach rather than a need for a larger bill everywhere.

The toes are much flatter and more needle-like, especially in three-quarter view. The painting has rounded toes that taper into distinct claws. The grey toes lose perhaps half that local thickness, although this is less than 1% of bird height.

3. What looks wrong as a 3D object

The long dark gap between the bill halves makes the mouth look held open; the painting shows a close-fitting seam. The wing's hard lower boundary makes it look attached as a separate plate. The front breast can read as a hanging sack, and the feet as thin spikes attached to blunt ankles.

The scattered jagged gaps and ragged feather-card edges are not reliable evidence of broken geometry with this test cutout. No clearly floating part is evident in these views.
