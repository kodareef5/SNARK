# Body painting, round 1: close; three things

Built and drawn by the game's renderer, the bird now reads as the concept's from every side: a round slate body in soft rows, five chevron bars across the back and rump where the design puts them (your top view beside the concept shows it well), the plain breast. Three things are not yet right (attached: the back close up, the side, and the bird from above):

- **The bars are white stitching.** Close up each bar is a thin, regular sawtooth line of bright strands, the same depth everywhere, like zigzag stitching. In the concept (`ref/concept/back.png`, `top.png`, and your study's comparison) each bar is the row's pale feather tips: at each feather's centre a soft brush of pale strands 8 to 10 mm deep pointing down the body, thinning between feathers, the strands' inner ends fading into the grey at different lengths. Make them deeper at each shaft and irregular in their strands. Two more: near the rump two bars run close together on both sides (row 3 has a second line just below it, and rows 4 and 5 bend into an M round the tail); give the five rows even spacing, about 3 cm, and let them thin and break up as they run down the flanks rather than stopping clean.
- **A stepped seam across the neck.** From the side, halfway up the neck, the rows break in a stair-step line across the neck's whole width. Find it (probably where two placement zones or the direction field meet) and make the rows run on through it.
- **Fish scales on the neck and breast.** There every feather is outlined by a thin dark rim, with a pale centre, so the neck reads as fish scales or a pinecone, the look the design moves away from. In the concept the free end of each feather is soft and a little paler, its edge a fringe of barb tips, and the dark is only the soft shadow in the row's overlap, not a line round each feather. Take the outline out of the base and the occlusion, soften the free edges into fringe, and keep the rows.
