# Body painting, round 3: the bars as feather tips, and then it is done

This is close to the bird. The five rows are even and lie where the design puts them, they are filled and soft, and the neck and breast now read as soft, overlapping feathers. Keep all of that.

One thing left, and it is the design's signature marking. Each row now reads as one continuous zigzag ribbon of bright cream (attached: from above and behind). In the concept (`ref/concept/top.png`, `back.png`, and `concept_bars.png` attached) each row is a **chain of separate feather tips**: one soft pale V per feather, as wide as the feather's free end (about 3.5 to 4.5 cm), pointing down the body at its shaft, with a small notch of grey between neighbouring tips where their sides meet. So a row across the back is eight to ten pale tips side by side, not one ribbon, and its lower edge is scalloped feather by feather.

- **One V per feather.** Put each pale tip on a feather of the row, centred on that feather's shaft and as wide as that feather; let the row's grey show in a narrow notch between neighbours. The rows of pale tips should be rows of the plumage itself: the feathers of a barred row are the feathers whose free ends carry the cream.
- **Muted, not bright.** The strands now reach nearly white even in the default cream. Hold the pale tips' value within the cream (their base a little above the region's mean grey, not far above it), with only the very ends of a few strands lighter, so the bars read as cream feather tips rather than white paint.
- **They thin down the flanks.** Toward the flanks the tips get smaller and further apart and stop, rather than the ribbon running on to the wing.
