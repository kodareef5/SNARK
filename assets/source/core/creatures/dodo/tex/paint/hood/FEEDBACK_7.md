# Hood painting, round 7: plumage, not hair

The art director's review of the whole bird, and a stranger's before it, say the same thing: the hood reads as hair. From the side it reads as a dark helmet, and from the back as a black bob wig with a ragged hem. The default colour is part of it, and the coordinator is warming and lightening the default Head dye toward the concept's umber. The other part is the drawing: fine strand-like fibres read as hair at every size, on the cap, in the mane and in the hem.

It must read as plumage, as the concept's hood does (`ref/concept/head_side.png`, `back.png`, `top.png`, `head_front.png`; look at them at full size):

- **The cap:** small, rounded feather tips overlapping in loose rows that follow the skull back from the forehead, each tip a little paler than the shadowed root of the one in front, so the eye sees feathers, scale-like but soft, not fibres. A few shafts catch the light. The surface between the tips is the feathers' own soft vanes, not hair.
- **The mane:** the same feathers grown longer and broader down the nape, overlapping in rows, each with a visible rounded or slightly pointed tip and a shaft; not strands.
- **The hem** (the edge strip, the mane's lower tips): made of feather ends, each a rounded tip, ending unevenly; not a fringe of strands.
- Look at it dyed in the concept's umber (#3B302A, and a warmer #6A4532) as well as near-white: in the concept's umber it should read as the concept's brown feathered hood.
