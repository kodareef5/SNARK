# Study: the olive horn of the beak

Read `tex/studies/COMMON.md` first and follow it. Your folder is `tex/studies/beak/`.

## What it shows

The beak is region 2 (blue), olive horn by default, and the thing a stranger looks at first. In the design's words: olive horn, deep and heavy and laterally flattened, 0.12 m long. A ridge runs along its top and arcs down into a full, heavy hook that overhangs the lower beak by about 2 cm, paler where it is worn. Three shallow grooves ring the upper beak near the face. The nostril is a long narrow slit low on the side, a third of the way along. See `ref/concept/head_side.png`, `head_front.png` and `top.png`.

- **The horn.** Keratin: a fine grain running along the beak toward the tip, faint growth lines, shallow pits and pores scattered unevenly, a few fine scratches and nicks. Satin, not glossy and not chalky.
- **The wear.** Toward the hook the horn is worn smoother and paler, with chips and scuffs at the very tip and along the cutting edge. The paleness is value in the base colour (lighter) and smoothness in roughness, so it reads in any dye.
- **The grooves.** Three shallow grooves ring the upper beak near the face, a few millimetres apart, each a soft rounded channel, a little dirtier (darker) in its floor.
- **The nostril.** A long, narrow slit with a slight soft rim.

## Sets

1. `horn`: the plain horn surface. 2,048 pixels for 0.256 m, tiling both ways, the grain running across the picture (left to right is along the beak).
2. `beak_side`: one picture that is not a tile: the side of the beak laid flat, face on the left and hook on the right, in the proportions of `ref/concept/head_side.png`, both halves of the beak (the upper with its ridge, grooves, nostril and worn hook; the lower with its own wear along the edge). 2,048 pixels for 0.14 m, so the beak fills the width. Region map blue on the beak, black around it. It shows where everything sits along the beak; the bird's painter will lay the same things onto the modeller's beak.

## What excellent looks like

Up close it is old, used horn you could tap with a fingernail: grain, pits, scratches, a hook rubbed pale and smooth by use. Shrunk to the bird, the grooves and the pale hook still read, as in `ref/concept/side.png` at 128 pixels. It looks right olive, slate, charcoal or umber, and dyed lime it is a lime horn beak, not a lime plastic one.
