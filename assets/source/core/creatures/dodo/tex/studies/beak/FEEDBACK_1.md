# Beak study, round 1: sent back

The numbers are right (mean grey 0.500, neutral, normal against height 0.99 and 1.00, horn seams under 1.2, clean regions), the side layout is well placed (the beak's outline, the three grooves near the face, the low slit nostril and the gape all sit where the concept has them) and the wear masks kept as separate fields are exactly what the bird's painter needs. But the surface is not yet horn, and it is not yet the concept's beak, so please do both sets again. The attached sheets are my review: the horn and the side relit, dyed, at the bird's size and beside the concept's beak at the concept's scale.

- **It reads as brushed metal or painted siding.** The grain is a strong, even, continuous horizontal streaking across the whole surface, the same everywhere. Horn's grain is subtle and broken: soft longitudinal fibres that come and go, here and there a faint growth line, and mostly a smooth satin surface. Turn the streaking well down and break it up.
- **The base colour is flat.** Dyed and unlit it is one even tone. Read the new section "Where the drawing lives" in `tex/studies/COMMON.md`. Look at the concept's beak at full size (`ref/concept/head_side.png`): the horn is mottled, with soft paler and darker patches like old horn; it is peppered with small dark pits and pores, scattered unevenly and clustered in places (more toward the hook and the base); fine scratches cross it at angles; the worn hook is pale, almost ivory, with tiny dark speckles and faint staining. All of that is value, and it belongs in the base, as well as the pits and scratches in the height. Your generated original may have had some of this; bring it through.
- **The worn hook barely shows.** In the concept the hook's paleness is one of the things that reads first, even at 128 pixels. Make the worn part clearly paler and smoother, its edge soft and following the curve of the hook and the cutting edge, so that at the bird's 200-pixel size it still reads as a pale worn tip in every colour of the set (olive, pale horn, slate, charcoal, umber, moss, oxblood).
- Keep the grooves, the nostril and the layout; they are right. The grooves could be a little crisper as raised rounded bands with narrow channels between, as in the concept.

Same sets, names and scales; update the report for this round. Before you stop, set your side study dyed olive and relit beside the concept's beak at the same size: a stranger should see the same old, pitted, worn horn.
