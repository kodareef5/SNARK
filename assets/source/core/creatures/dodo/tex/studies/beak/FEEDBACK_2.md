# Beak study, round 2: closer, sent back once more

Better: the base now carries value, the pale worn hook reads, the streaking is gone, and your same-size comparison (`checks/concept_comparison.png`) is exactly the right check. Set it beside the concept again and look at these:

- **The mottling overshoots.** It is now round, cloudy blotches of strong contrast, so the horn reads as camouflage, lichen or dirty plastic, worst of all dyed lime (see the attached sheet, "worst, whole"). Old horn's mottling is gentle and long: soft tonal drift running along the beak with its growth, a little darker toward the base, never round clouds. About half the contrast, and shaped along the grain.
- **Dark clouds that read as shading.** On the side panel, unlit, there are dark patches along the inner curve of the hook and along the gape that look like the form's own shadow. The base holds no shading of the form; take them out.
- **The pits are soft.** In the concept they are crisp little craters, 0.3 to 1 mm, with dark floors and sometimes a faint rim, of varied size, scattered unevenly with clusters on the hook and near the base. Yours are blurred dots. Make them crisp in both the base and the height.
- **The grooves are faint lines.** In the concept the three grooves near the face are bold: rounded raised bands with narrow, crisp channels between them, clearly read at 128 pixels. Yours are thin pencil lines. Make them as bold as the concept's.
- **The relief is too slight.** The horn's normal map is almost flat and its occlusion averages 0.999. Satin horn is smooth, but its pits, scratches, growth lines and grooves are real relief and should catch the light.
