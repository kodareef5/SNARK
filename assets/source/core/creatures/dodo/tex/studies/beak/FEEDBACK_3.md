# Beak study, round 3: very close, one last pass on two details

This is horn now. The mottling runs gently along the grain, the dark clouds are gone, the worn hook is pale and smooth, and relit on your proxy the beak stands beside the concept's. Keep all of that. The beak is the first thing a stranger looks at, so two details are worth one more pass:

- **The grooves are drawn as lines.** In the unlit base the three grooves are dark pencil lines, so even relit they read as three lines scored into the horn. In the concept (left panel of your comparison) they read as rings: three rounded raised bands with pale crests, separated by narrow, dark, crisp channels, like the growth rings at the base of a horn. Model the bands in the height (rounded crests a little proud of the surface, narrow channels between) and let the base keep the channels' floors darker and the crests a touch paler, so the bands read at the bird's size as ringed, not as scored.
- **The pits are a uniform pepper.** They are many and all tiny and of one size, so the surface reads peppered. The concept's are fewer and more varied: mostly small, a scattering of larger ones (1 to 2 mm), some in loose clusters, a few shallow and soft, a few crisp; more on the hook and toward the base. Two or three clear scratches crossing at angles, as in the concept, rather than one faint one.
