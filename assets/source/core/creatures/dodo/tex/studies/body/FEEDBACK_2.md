**The bars are now the wrong shape.** They are thin, hard-edged crescents, each shorter than its feather's width and separated from its neighbours by gaps, so a row of them reads as fingernail clippings or eyebrows. `concept_bars.png` (attached) is the concept's back and rump enlarged. There:

- **The bar is the feather's fluffy tip itself.** The pale part includes the free edge and its fringe: the barb tips at the very edge are cream. So the bar's outer edge is the feather's own soft fringe, not a line inside it.
- **Its inner edge is brushy.** Toward the root the cream does not stop on a smooth curve: pale barbs reach up between grey ones to different lengths, so the boundary is a soft comb of strands. - **It is deepest at the feather's centre**, about a third of the exposed end (roughly 8 to 10 mm at the tip), thinning toward the sides, so each feather's pale tip is a soft V or scallop pointing down the body.
- **It is continuous across the row.** Each feather's pale tip runs the full width of its exposed end, and neighbours' tips meet and overlap, so the row of tips makes one continuous scalloped band. Cover will rise to perhaps 15 to 20% of the tile; that is right.
