# Study: the straw eye and its lid

Read `tex/studies/COMMON.md` first and follow it. Your folder is `tex/studies/eye/`.

## What it shows

In the design's words: under the brow sits a small straw-yellow eye with a black pupil and a half-closed upper lid: a vacant, sleepy, mildly grumpy stare. The eye is 22 mm across. See `ref/concept/head_side.png` and `head_front.png`.

- **The eye** is never dyed: painted in its own colours, black in the region map. A pale straw-yellow iris with fine radial fibres and flecks, a slightly darker rim at its edge, and a round black pupil about a third of its width; the bird's eye shows almost no white. The surface is a smooth, wet cornea over the iris: the smoothest, wettest thing on the bird. The modeller's eyeball is a sphere half covered by the lid; this picture is laid on it from the front.
- **The lid** is the face's skin (region 1, green), finer: tiny pebbles and soft fine wrinkles running along the lid, parallel to its edge, heavier and deeper toward the brow, with a smooth thin rim at the edge. It is the skin that makes the stare sleepy, so the wrinkles matter.

## Sets

1. `eye`: the eye seen from the front, centred, the iris filling 22 mm of a picture that covers 26 mm. 1,024 pixels.
2. `lid`: the upper lid unrolled: across the picture is along the lid from the front corner to the back (about 3 cm), down the picture is from the brow (top) to the rim (bottom), with the rim along the bottom edge. 1,024 pixels for 0.03 m, tiling across only.

## What excellent looks like

Up close the eye is alive and wet, with an iris you could look into, and dull: the stare comes from the lid and the plain straw colour, not from anything fierce. Shrunk to the bird, where the eye is about 100 pixels across at the closest, the pupil and iris read cleanly and the lid's wrinkles still make it heavy. The lid dyed any face colour, from ivory to dusky taupe, is still a soft wrinkled lid.
