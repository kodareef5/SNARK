# Eye study, round 1: sent back

Thank you: the set is complete, aligned, rebuilt byte for byte, and the lid's numbers are right (mean grey 0.500, neutral, normal against height 1.00 and 0.99, seam 1.19 across). The engineering is not the problem. The picture is. Both sets fall short of the concept and of "alive and wet", so please do them again. The attached sheets are my review of what you delivered: the eye and the lid relit, dyed, at the bird's size and beside the concept.

## The eye

- **The pupil is far too big, and that is my brief's fault.** I wrote "about a third of its width"; the concept says otherwise, and the concept wins. In `ref/concept/head_side.png` and `head_front.png` the pupil is a small black bead, about an eighth of the iris across (about 3 mm in a 22 mm iris). That small pupil in a plain field of straw is exactly what makes the stare vacant and a little dim. A third-wide pupil makes it an alert, dark-eyed bird. Make it about 3 mm, round, crisp-edged.
- **The iris is a pale paper disc.** Its straw is washed almost to cream, its fibres are so faint they vanish at any size below full, and nothing in it has depth. Look at the concept's eye: a warmer, more olive straw-yellow, clearly coloured, with a slightly darker ring at its edge and a quiet radial texture. Give it real structure: fine radial fibres and a few shallow crypts and furrows, a slightly different tone in a narrow zone round the pupil, a darker limbal ring that defines the edge, and fine flecks. Quiet, not fierce, but rich enough that at 100 pixels across it is an eye with an iris, and up close you could look into it.
- Keep the smooth cornea, the flat normal and the low roughness: that is right.

## The lid

- **The wrinkles read as sand ripples or bark, not as a lid.** You drew many thin, wavy, broken lines crossing the whole tile at random. A heavy upper lid has few folds: three or four long, soft, rounded folds that run along the lid roughly parallel to its rim, following its curve, each a fleshy roll with a soft crease above it, deeper and heavier toward the brow, fading out toward the corners. Look at the folds above and below the eye in `head_side.png`: big soft rolls, not ripples.
- **The pebbles are too coarse and too strong for a lid.** Lid skin is the finest skin on the face: make its pebbling finer and lower than the face's, smoothing out toward the rim.
- **The rim** should be a distinct smooth, thick, rounded edge, about 1 mm, a little heavier than you have it, because the heavy rim is what makes the lid look half-shut and sleepy.
- **The base colour is perfectly flat**, so every bit of the drawing lives in the normal and occlusion and vanishes under soft light. The brief asks for the drawing to live in value: skin pigment is a little paler on the tops of folds and pebbles and a little darker in the floors of creases. Draw that in the base, restrained and with no light direction, keeping the mean at 0.50 linear and the hue at none.

Deliver the same two sets, same names and scales, with the report updated for this round (say what changed and why). Look at the result beside the concept's eye at the concept's size before you stop.
