# Face study, round 1: sent back

The numbers are right (mean grey 0.500, neutral, normal against height 1.00, seams about 1.0), and the pebbled skin relit up close is the closest any study has come in this round to the real thing. But the four sets are not yet the concept's head, so please do them again. The attached sheets are my review, and `concept_face.png` is the concept's eye, cheek, brow and pouch enlarged.

- **The base colours are flat.** Read the new section "Where the drawing lives" in `tex/studies/COMMON.md`. In the concept the face reads as pebbled even at a small size because the narrow creases between pebbles are darker and the pebbles' tops paler; the pouch's pleats have darker floors; the brow's fine grain has darker lines. That is value in the base, restrained and with no light direction, mean 0.50 and no hue.
- **The skin's pebbles are too soft and faint.** Relit they read a little like hammered metal, and at the concept's scale they vanish. The concept's pebbles are distinct rounded polygons, about 1.5 to 2.5 mm, packed like fine cobbles, each separated from its neighbours by a crisp, narrow crease, finer toward the eye and the beak. Clearer cells, crisper creases.
- **The folds are thin wires.** The concept's under-eye folds are big, soft, fleshy rolls: three or four of them stacked in concentric arcs under the eye, each 3 to 4 mm thick with a soft deep crease above it, the whole set about 25 to 30 mm wide; the mouth-corner fold is a thick crescent roll that turns down. The pebbles continue over the rolls. Yours are 1 mm lines that disappear at the bird's size. Make them the size and weight of the concept's, fading softly into plain skin at their ends.
- **The brow's grain vanishes.** Smooth is right, but at the concept's scale the concept's brow still shows a fine reticulated leathery grain: small polygonal cells about 0.5 to 1 mm with fine creases. Yours is noise that disappears below full size. Keep it lower and finer than the face's pebbles, but make it a real grain that reads.
- **The pouch is machined.** The spacing is right, but every pleat is identical, like corrugated card, and the skin between reads fibrous, like corduroy. Vary the pleats a little in width and depth, let a few merge or fork as the brief asked, and make the skin between them smooth, fine-grained skin.

Same sets, names and scales; update the report for this round. Before you stop, set each set dyed in its default colour and relit beside the concept's head at the concept's scale: a stranger should see the same face.
