# Leg study, round 1: sent back

The numbers are right (mean grey 0.500, neutral, normal against height 1.00, seams fine), the height maps are well built (plates down the middle, pebbles at the sides, wrapping round), and your foot preview was a good idea. But the surfaces do not yet read as the concept's legs, so please do all three sets again. The attached sheets are my review, and `concept_legs.png` is the concept's leg and foot enlarged.

- **The plates are the wrong shape, and that is partly my brief's fault.** I described long transverse plates each wrapping a third of the way round. The concept shows something else, and the concept wins: down the front of the shank, large rounded polygonal plates, irregular in size (about 6 to 10 mm), staggered like cobbles in two or three columns across the front, getting smaller toward the sides, where they break into small pebbly scales, and smaller again round the ankle joint. The toes carry a row of rounded plates along their tops, two or so across, with pebbles down their sides. See `concept_legs.png`.
- **The surfaces are crumbly.** Relit, the plates read as dried mud or bark: lumpy, noisy, cracked. Each plate in the concept is a firm, slightly domed piece of keratin with a fine leathery grain on its face and a soft rounded rim, separated from its neighbours by a clean dark crease. The pebbles are small smooth domes. Firm and clean, not crumbly.
- **The base colour is flat.** Read the new section "Where the drawing lives" in `tex/studies/COMMON.md`. In the concept every crease between plates is darker (dirt and pigment) and the plates' faces carry fine light and dark grain. Draw that in the base, restrained and with no light direction, mean 0.50, no hue.
- **The claw is near black and reads as brushed wood.** Its base averages about sRGB 35. The concept's claws are a mid-dark grey-brown horn, roughly sRGB 90 to 110, a little lighter and warmer toward the worn tip, with a polished sheen. The vertical streaking is too strong and regular; growth lines on a claw are faint and few. It is undyed, so paint it in that colour.

Same sets, names and scales; update the report for this round. Before you stop, set the shank and toe dyed ochre and relit beside the concept's leg and foot at the concept's size: a stranger should see the same cobbled plates.
