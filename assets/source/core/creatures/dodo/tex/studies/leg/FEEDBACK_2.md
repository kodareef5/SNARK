# Leg study, round 2: closer, sent back once more

Better: the plates are now firm, slightly domed pieces with rims and a fine grain, the creases are darker in the base, and the claw is no longer black. Up close (the attached sheet, row two) a single plate is right. But set your foot preview (`checks/foot_ochre.png`) beside the concept's legs (`concept_legs.png`, attached again) and it reads as snakeskin, not as this bird's leg:

- **A honeycomb of identical hexagons.** Every plate is the same regular hexagon on a lattice, so the pattern reads as chicken wire or snakeskin. The concept's plates are irregular polygons of many shapes and sizes: some broad and wider than tall, some nearly square, some five-sided, small wedge-shaped plates filling the gaps, staggered with no lattice. No two neighbours alike.
- **The plates cover too little of the leg.** You plated a narrow strip about a fifth of the way round, two small plates wide, and filled the rest with fine dots. In the concept the whole front half of the shank is plated, with plates of 6 to 10 mm across the front grading to 3 to 5 mm round the sides; only the back of the leg has the fine pebbles. At the bird's size the leg must read as a plated leg, not a striped one. The same goes for the toes: plated over their tops and sides, as in the concept.
- **The back pebbles are uniform dots.** Make them small irregular scales, 1.5 to 3 mm, with creases between, growing into the side plates without a line.
- **The claw has gone blank.** The base is one even grey-brown and the height is noise. On the bird the claw is small, so what it needs is a clear value run: dark brown-grey at the base (about sRGB 70) to a paler, worn grey horn at the tip (about sRGB 130), a soft sheen, and a few faint growth lines along it that show only close up. Smooth relief, not noise.
