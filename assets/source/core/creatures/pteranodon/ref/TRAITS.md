# Pteranodon: trait sheet

The few traits that make players recognise ARK's pteranodon, written for the designer (PA1), the modeller (PA2), the
animators (PA3) and the texture maker (PA4). It describes ARK's pteranodon as players know it, not the real animal; the
real animal is a check on how a wing folds and how a body leaves the ground, at the end.

**Evidence.** Official pictures on the ARK wiki: the dossier painting, the six region renders of each game's model, the
gallery's in-game screenshots (from below in flight, gliding, banking, on the ground beside a survivor, saddled and
ridden, first person from the saddle), the saddle's item icon; the official Steam screenshots of both games (75 looked
at, four in full); FAN_ART_SURVEY. Every source is in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). Pictures were looked at in a temporary
folder outside the repo and deleted; nothing here is traced or measured from a game.

**Ratios are by eye,** from screenshots taken with a wide third-person camera, which stretches whatever is nearest the
lens. Treat every number as "about".

**ASE and ASA.** ARK: Survival Evolved's model is the base. ARK: Survival Ascended's keeps the layout, the six regions,
the twin crest sails and the stripes, and adds deep wrinkles and pebbling, crisper cells in the sails, modelled teeth,
grooves along the snout, a heavier brow over a smaller eye, knobbly wrists and a fibrous membrane. No ASA picture of it on
the ground was found.

## What matters, most important first

1. **The arrowhead head.** A long, straight beak tapering to a needle point, balanced behind by a crest sweeping up and
   back about as far: the head is the largest single part, longer than neck and torso together. ARK's crest is a rod
   or spike with a dark sail filling the angle above the skull, a second low fin along the top of the snout, and a
   V-notch between them: a "double-finned" head.
2. **Enormous wings on a tiny body.** The span is about 2.5 to 3 times the length from beak to feet. A short arm rises
   to a high wrist, then the long wing finger runs out and a little down to a pointed tip swept back like a scythe. A
   small three-clawed hand sits at the wrist on the leading edge. From below the outline is a shallow gull's M.
3. **Contrast between body and membrane.** A pale body against a coloured membrane, and the beak and crest as a "cap"
   of another colour from the face, the line just in front of the eye. In game the colours vary widely; the contrast
   stays.
4. **A long slim neck held up in an S,** the head held level, on the ground and in flight.
5. **Sitting up on the ground.** A very upright quadruped: torso tilted about 50 to 60 degrees nose up, neck nearly
   upright, head high. Each arm is one near-upright strut to a clawed hand on the ground in front; the folded wing
   finger runs back along the ground and kinks up into a tip behind the body at about shoulder height, so the folded tips
   point up and back. The membrane stays spread at the sides like a cape.
6. **Legs that hang in flight,** thin, with curved grasping claws, swinging forward to grab.
7. **A rider sitting upright on the shoulders,** just behind the base of the neck, with reins to a halter on the snout.

The first three say "pteranodon" to anyone. The twin sails with their notch, the stripes and the halter say "ARK's
pteranodon"; the twin sails are ARK's own invention, which makes them the detail most likely to read as copied.

## Silhouette and stance

- **From below, in flight.** A tiny body, the head and crest a long arrow in front, the wings a shallow M: broad at the
  root, the membrane running from the shoulder down to the ankle, narrowing outboard to a point. The trailing edge is
  two concave scallops: a long shallow curve from the tip to a small point behind the wrist, then a deeper one down to
  the ankle. No membrane joins the legs to each other. The belly shows pale cross-bands.
- **From the side, in flight.** The arrowhead head on a slim neck rising forward at about 30 to 45 degrees, the head held
  level; a small body; legs hanging down and a little forward; the huge wings.
- **From behind, the upstroke** is a tall V (a rider seen from behind).
- **On the ground.** The sitting-up quadruped of item 5. The far wing's tip pokes up behind the back.
- **Gliding,** the wings are flat with the tips a little drooped. **Diving,** the head goes down and the wings sweep up
  and back with the legs trailing (ASA renders).

## Colouring and where colour sits

The game has six regions, the same names in both games. The wiki shows each in red on an albino.

| Region | Name | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Patterning | The stripes: bands across the neck, back and flanks, bars on the crest spike, bars radiating over the cheek, a ring round the eye, the belly's bands; in ASA thin lines along the wing's bones |
| 1 | Wing Base | A faint wash on the inner membrane near the body and armpit, and the folded far wing's tip (ASE) |
| 2 | Face, Crest and Hands | The beak, both crest sails, the claws of hands and feet |
| 3 | Inner Crest | The cells inside the sails |
| 4 | Wing Membrane | The whole membrane |
| 5 | Body | The skin between the stripes, the crest spike, the leading edge of the wing bones |

- **The dossier painting:** an off-white to pale grey body with crimson zebra stripes over neck, body, cheek and crest
  spike; the membrane deep crimson above, darkening to charcoal at the tip, red-brown below; the sails teal to slate
  with white cells; a charcoal beak; pale wing bones and claws. Stripes on the body only, nothing on the wing.
- **In game:** brown-red wings on a grey-green body; a pale body with grey wings and orange crest cells; a green body with
  pink-red wings; orange wings. Body colour carries nothing; the contrast does.

## How it reads near, far and tiny

- **Close (a few metres, and from the saddle).** The beak's point and the cap line, the crest and its pattern, the eye
  under the brow, the skin, the hand at the wrist, the halter and reins, the membrane's fibres.
- **Middle (20 to 100 m, in the air).** The arrowhead head, the span, the body-membrane contrast, the hanging legs. From
  below, the M against the sky, dark against light.
- **Far, or a few pixels.** A thin M with a point at the front. Against the sky the outline is all there is: the span,
  the swept tips and the forward point of the head.
- **On the ground, far.** An upright stalk with a big head, two pointed tips behind.
- **The lowest level** keeps the span, the swept tips, the head's arrow and the crest: a flat M of a few dozen triangles.
  It is mostly seen from below.

## The quality bar in official pictures

- **ASE.** Wrinkled to finely pebbled bare skin, painted stripes, the membrane with lines radiating from the body,
  cobbled cells in the sails. Realistic surfaces on a stylised shape.
- **ASA.** Deep wrinkles and pebbling, crisp sail cells, modelled teeth, snout grooves, a fibrous membrane. A clearly
  higher bar.
- **The ASE level is the comparable target**: skin that reads up close, a membrane that reads as skin and not as cloth or
  plastic from below, the head's colours readable at middle distance.

## What does not matter

- Exact colours; the sail cells' pattern; the skin's wrinkles.
- Teeth, invisible at play distance.
- The exact scallop of the trailing edge.
- The tail: none shows in any picture.
- The number of toes, the eye's detail.
- The differences between ASE's and ASA's detail.
- The saddle's bag and buckles.
- Sex: no picture or page shows a difference.

## Where ours can differ

Options for the designer, not decisions. None costs recognition while the ranked traits stay.

- **The crest.** A single solid blade sweeping back, with or without a low ridge along the snout, in place of ARK's twin
  sails and notch. Still an arrowhead. This is the clearest single way to look like our own.
- **The beak.** Toothless, as the real animal's name says, with the upper jaw a little longer and turned up at the tip.
  The needle silhouette stays.
- **The pattern.** Our own in place of zebra stripes: a seabird's countershading, a dark wingtip, a band of our own on
  the crest. Keep pale against coloured between body and membrane.
- **The skin.** A short coat of fine fuzz on head, neck and torso, as all pterosaurs had, with the membrane bare. The
  outline does not change; the reptile look does.
- **The wing.** By degree narrower than ARK's bat wing, toward the real animal's long narrow wing, still reaching the
  ankle. The wing carries recognition, so change it a little.
- **The ground stance.** Weight on the three small fingers with the wing finger folded tighter up the flank, but still
  sitting up with the tips pointing up and back.
- **The saddle.** Our own: a low pad with a chest harness and our own halter; the rider still upright on the shoulders
  behind the neck.
- **The colour set.** Our own natural colours per region, under ARK's six region names and rough places (FUNCTIONS §14).

## Real anatomy, as a check

For how the wing folds, how it stands and how it leaves the ground. Not the subject: ARK's pteranodon is its own.

- **The wing's bones.** A short, very strong upper arm; a longer forearm; a very long wing metacarpal, as long as the
  forearm or longer; three small clawed fingers at the knuckle where the wing finger starts; a wing finger of four bones,
  half the wing's length or more, the last curving back to the tip. The pteroid braces the front membrane from wrist to
  shoulder.
- **The membrane** runs from the wing finger to the lower leg or ankle (Elgin, Hone and Frey 2011, every completely
  preserved specimen). No broad membrane between the legs. The real wing is narrow and long, about 9 to 1.
- **Folding and standing.** Standing, it rests on the metacarpals and the three fingers, the elbow bent, the wing finger
  folded back by extension through up to 175 degrees, tight along the flank, the membrane drawn in. Trackways show an
  upright four-legged walk, three-toed hands and flat four-toed feet.
- **Taking off.** On all fours: crouch, the hind legs kick, the arms vault it up and forward, the wings snap open on the
  upstroke, then the first downstroke (Habib 2008; Witton and Habib 2010).
- **Landing.** Stall, touch down lightly on the heels, skid forward, both feet down, then both hands, a short step, and
  away on all fours (Mazin, Billon-Bruyat and Padian 2009).
- **Head and size.** Males had the long swept crest, females a small rounded one; males spanned 5.6 to 7.6 m, females
  about 3.8; the body was small and light. Toothless; a tail about 3.5% of the span.
- **Where ARK departs and still reads:** teeth (low weight), the twin sails (high: they are ARK's), the broad bat wing
  (medium), bare scaly skin (medium), the membrane spread like a cape on the ground (medium), raptor-like grasping feet
  (they are the grab), a body large enough to ride (the ride).
