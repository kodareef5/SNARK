Three animal-reference cautions matter here:

- **Heron strikes are not invariably closed-beak spearing.** Grasping prey between the mandibles is common; your closure at contact is a sound reference choice. Borrow the directed acceleration and quick retrieval, while respecting this animal’s shorter neck. [Heron behaviour terminology](https://heronconservation.org/conservation/terminology/terminology-in-herons/)
- **Foot carrying is a deliberate game invention for this Pteranodon.** Recent comparative work interprets its feet as strongly adapted for terrestrial use, offering little support for an eagle-like prey-carrying specialization. Keep the mechanic, but make its grip and load response convincing. [Comparative hand and foot study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2024.09.014)
- **A big head needs controlled stopping, not constant wobbling.** Across the bites, call and startle, one small delayed settling movement will communicate its inertia better than extra neck oscillations. The skittish character should come chiefly from fast withdrawal, interrupted effort and alert holds.
