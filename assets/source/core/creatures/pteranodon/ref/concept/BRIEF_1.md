You are painting the concept design of a game creature: a pteranodon, the first creature in the game that flies and the first that is ridden with a saddle. Work only in the current folder.

## What the pictures are for

A survival game needs its pteranodon: a fast, skittish flying reptile with an enormous wingspan on a tiny body, a long straight beak balanced by a crest sweeping back, that flies over beaches, lands now and then to rest, is knocked out and tamed, and is then ridden by a player sitting upright on its shoulders. A player sees it mostly from below and from the side against a bright sky, at 20 to 400 metres, often banking; and up close when taming it on a beach, climbing onto it, and riding it with the saddle and themselves on its back.

Your pictures are the design. After you, a modeller builds the 3D mesh straight from your orthographic views, three animators work on a skeleton fitted to those views, and a texture painter paints from your colours and markings. So the views must be exact enough to model from, consistent enough that every view describes one animal, and beautiful enough to put on a studio wall.

It is a stylised game pteranodon, not a museum reconstruction, and it must be our own design. It must not copy any existing game's pteranodon. Do not search the web and do not look at pictures of other pteranodons or pterosaurs. Work from this description. In your image prompts, describe the animal in your own words and do not name any game, studio or franchise.

## The example to beat

The attached pictures are the game's first creature, a dodo, painted in an earlier round of this same work: its side view, its three-quarter view and its head close-up. That is the hand and the finish: realistic matte surfaces on a slightly stylised shape, even neutral light, a plain warm grey ground, a crisp confident silhouette, and surface detail a texture painter can follow. Paint the pteranodon in the same hand, so the two sit together in one game. Beat it.

## The animal, in words

**What makes it a pteranodon at a glance (keep all of these):**

1. **The arrowhead head.** A long, straight, tapering beak to a needle point, balanced behind by a crest sweeping up and back about as far. Beak and crest together are the largest single part of the animal, longer than neck and torso together.
2. **Enormous wings on a tiny body.** The span is about 2.6 times the length from beak tip to feet. A short arm rises to a high wrist, where a small three-clawed hand sits on the leading edge; from there one immensely long finger runs out and a little down to a pointed tip swept back like a scythe. From below and from the front, the spread wings make a shallow gull's M.
3. **Contrast.** A pale body against a darker, coloured wing membrane; and the beak and crest as a horn "cap" of another colour from the face, the line between them just in front of the eye.
4. **A long slim neck held in an S**, the head held level, in flight and on the ground.
5. **Sitting up on the ground.** A very upright four-footed stance: the torso tilted about 50 degrees nose up, the neck nearly upright, the head high. Each arm is one near-upright strut down to the small clawed hand on the ground in front. The folded wing finger runs back from the hand along the flank and ends in a tip that points up and back behind the body at about shoulder height. The folded membrane hangs at the sides.
6. **Legs that hang or trail in flight**: thin, with curved grasping claws, made for snatching things up.
7. **A rider sits upright on its shoulders**, just behind the base of the neck, legs either side in front of the wing roots.

**Our own choices (paint these exactly; they are what make it ours):**

- **Crest.** One single solid blade, flat from side to side like a sabre, rising from the back of the skull and sweeping up and back at about 50 degrees from the beak's line, its upper edge gently convex and its lower edge gently concave, tapering to a rounded point. No second fin, no sail, no notch, no cells. A low, sharp ridge (a keel, not a fin) runs along the top of the beak from the forehead to near the tip. The crest carries **one broad colour band** across it, about a third of the way down from its tip, crossing the blade cleanly on both faces.
- **Beak.** Toothless, smooth horn with faint fine growth lines running along it. The upper jaw is a little longer than the lower, and its tip turns very slightly up, so the point reads as a needle with a faint upward flick. The gape runs straight back to below the front of the eye. A small slit nostril sits on the side of the upper beak near its base. The horn continues from the beak over the top of the skull into the crest as one cap; the line where horn meets the face's fuzz runs down just in front of the eye.
- **Colouring: a seabird's**, not stripes. Pale ivory below and on the head, neck and body; a dark charcoal mantle over the back from the shoulders to the hips, narrowing into a dark line up the back of the neck to the nape; a dark charcoal streak through the eye, from just in front of it back to the nape line; dark charcoal wingtips (the outer fifth of the wing, on both faces, ending cleanly). The beak, crest cap and feet are burnt-orange horn; the crest band charcoal. The membrane is a slate blue-grey, darker on its upper face, a little paler and warmer on its under face, with a faint warm ash wash on the inner membrane near the body. No zebra stripes, no bars, no spots, no red-on-white.
- **Skin.** A short, fine, velvety fuzz (like the down on a moth or a short-haired mammal's coat) over the head behind the horn, the neck, the torso and the upper limbs; it softens the outline a little and lies smoothly, following the body's form. The membrane is bare, matte, thin leathery skin with a few fine branching veins and a very faint fibrous striation running toward the trailing edge; never cloth, rubber or plastic. Taut when spread.
- **Wing shape.** A little narrower than a broad bat wing, still reaching the ankle: the membrane is about 1.0 m deep at the body (from the shoulder back to the ankle), about 0.75 m at the wrist, about 0.45 m halfway along the finger, then to the point. The trailing edge is **one long, smooth, concave curve** from the wingtip all the way to the ankle, not two scallops. A narrow front membrane fills the angle between the shoulder and the wrist in front of the arm. No membrane between the legs.
- **Eye.** Small, dark amber with a black pupil, under a slight brow, set in the charcoal eye streak.
- **Hands and feet.** Three small curved claws at the wrist on the leading edge; on the ground, the animal stands on these. Feet with four long toes and long, strongly curved, dark claws: grasping feet. A tiny stub of a tail, barely visible.

## The saddle and its rider

**The saddle is ours**: a low, close-fitting pad of layered, stitched hide over the shoulders just behind the neck, with a raised back (a curved cantle of bent wood stiffened by a pale plate of chitin) that holds the rider's hips; a chest harness of broad hide straps and plaited fibre rope that crosses in front of the chest and girths round the body **behind** the wing roots, clear of the arm's movement; two hand grips, wrapped in rope, standing at the front of the pad either side of the base of the neck; two foot rests (rope loops or short wooden pegs) on the harness, low at the sides of the chest in front of the wing roots; and our own halter on the beak's base: a thin strap round the snout just in front of the eye and one round the back of the skull, with two reins running back to the grips. Natural materials only: hide, plaited fibre, bent wood, chitin. No metal, no buckles to speak of, no bags, no flags, no torch, nothing on the wings. The straps sit snugly into the fuzz, as if they belong there.

**The rider** is an ordinary human survivor in a plain hide tunic and trousers and soft boots, an adult **1.75 m** tall standing with hips at 0.90 m, thighs 0.41 m, shins 0.43 m, shoulders 0.40 m apart, arms 0.55 m from shoulder to palm. Seated: upright, the sit bones on the pad over the shoulder joints (about z = -0.08, y = 0.88 in the spread pose), thighs running forward and down either side of the base of the neck, knees about z = +0.28, lower legs hanging with the feet in the foot rests, both hands on the grips, head about 1.8 m up and clear of the crest. Generic and unnamed: no face to speak of, no logo, no weapon.

## What must hold

- **The views agree.** Measure them: wingtips, wrists, shoulders, ankles, beak tip, eye and crest tip must sit at the same places in every view where they show (within 2% of the span in the spread views, within 2% of the standing height in the folded ones). The span in the top, underside and front views must match. Every marking (mantle, nape line, eye streak, wingtips, crest band) must be the same in every view where it shows. Draw guide lines across the views side by side and look at them. If a view disagrees, fix it or make it again; do not stretch a drawing to fit.
- **The wing reads as taut skin on a long finger**, from below above all, and folds flat along the arm on the ground.
- **Flat neutral light.** Even, soft, frontal light with only gentle form shading. No strong key light, no rim light, no cast shadows, no ground shadow, no reflections. A texture painter must be able to read true colours from it.
- **A plain background.** One flat even colour, light warm grey (RGB 214, 212, 206), the same in every picture. No ground plane, no sky, no props, no scenery, no vignette.
- **No text** in any picture: no labels, numbers, signatures or watermarks. Check sheets are the only exception.
- **Symmetric.** The animal is the same on both sides.
- **No added parts.** No teeth, second crest, sail, frills, spines, feathers, armour or accessories beyond what is described.

## What excellent looks like

Concept art a studio would put on its wall. A silhouette that says "pteranodon" in one glance even as a black shape a few pixels wide against the sky: the thin M of the wings with a forward point for the head. Up close, a design with its own clear language: the needle beak with its faint upturn and keel, the single banded sabre crest, the orange horn cap meeting the ivory fuzz in front of the eye, the charcoal eye streak, mantle and wingtips, the slate membrane stretched taut on its long finger, the grasping feet. Realistic matte surfaces on a slightly stylised shape, the same hand as the dodo. Someone who knows game pteranodons should recognise the creature immediately and, on a second look, see that it is its own animal. And a rider on its shoulders should look as if the saddle was made for this animal.
