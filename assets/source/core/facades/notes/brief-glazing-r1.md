## This job: the glass and what hangs in it

Seventeen elements, each in its own folder. Sizes are width by height in pixels, then in metres; 256 pixels a metre.

### Windows and doors with glass (alpha 0 where glass)

- `window-0`, `window-1`, `window-2`: 368 by 512 (1.4 m by 2.0 m). A flat's or a hotel room's window, its whole opening. A frame about 6 to 7 cm wide all round, running to the picture's edges; a horizontal sliding window: two sashes overlapping at a meeting stile in the middle (the left sash in front), with a fixed transom light across the top 0.45 m. The bottom member is a little deeper (a sub-sill), since the wall below carries a concrete sill of its own. Variants: 0 white powder-coated aluminium gone chalky and pitted; 1 bronze anodised aluminium, dulled and streaked; 2 painted timber, white paint flaking to grey wood. Glass: a light salt film, heavier in the lower corners and in a few drip runs from the top rail.
- `slider-0`, `slider-1`: 240 by 576 (0.9 m by 2.2 m). A glazed door from a flat onto its balcony: a tall single glass leaf in a frame about 7 cm wide, a deeper bottom rail (12 cm) and a threshold track at the foot, a slim pull handle on one stile at about 1 m. Variants: 0 white aluminium; 1 bronze aluminium.
- `casement-0`, `casement-1`: 368 by 368 (1.4 m by 1.4 m). A flat's window onto its balcony, sill at 0.9 m: two casements side by side with a central mullion, frame about 6 cm. Variants: 0 white aluminium with a jalousie of horizontal glass louvres in the left half (typical of the region; the louvres are glass, alpha 0, with their thin metal clips solid); 1 bronze aluminium, plain.
- `strip-0`: 1744 by 208 (6.8 m by 0.8 m). A high ribbon window lighting a lobby: a row of narrow panes, frame members every 0.85 m, top and bottom rails 6 cm, white aluminium. Glass film light.
- `curtain-0`, `curtain-1`: 512 by 672 (2.0 m by 2.6 m). One panel of an office tower's curtain wall between two vertical mullions that the game models as their own boxes (so do not paint the vertical mullions at the left and right edges; leave a 2 cm glazing bead there only). A thin glazing bead round the edge, a horizontal transom at 0.9 m from the bottom (the panel below it is a spandrel of opaque back-painted glass, alpha 255, dark grey-green or dark bronze), vision glass above (alpha 0). Variants: 0 grey-green glass and silver frame; 1 bronze glass and dark frame. The opaque spandrel glass is smooth (roughness about 0.1) with salt streaks; the vision glass has a light film.
- `display-0`, `display-1`: 1280 by 624 (5.0 m by 2.4 m). Variants: 0 silver aluminium, its lower bead scuffed and dented; 1 dark bronze aluminium with remains of an old window decal (a faded rectangular frame line of peeled vinyl in the middle light, no lettering). Heavier film and dust on the glass's lower third.

### What hangs inside or is fixed over the glass (alpha is coverage)

These are laid over a window's glass, stretched to it. Alpha 255 where the element covers the glass, 0 where the glass (or the room) shows through.
- `blind-0`: 256 by 256 (1.0 m by 1.0 m), tiling in both directions. A venetian blind seen from outside through glass: horizontal slats 5 cm apart, slightly tilted so a thin dark gap shows between them (alpha 0 in the gap), off-white and yellowed, a few slats bent or snapped (alpha 0 where a slat is missing). Two thin ladder cords. The game shows it pulled part way down, its bottom rail drawn by the game.
- `drape-0`, `drape-1`: 256 by 512 (1.0 m by 2.0 m), tiling left to right. A curtain hanging inside the glass: soft vertical folds, faded fabric (0 a sun-bleached floral print gone almost to beige, no recognisable motif at 15 m; 1 a plain faded blue-grey), its hem ragged and a little uneven at the bottom (alpha 0 below the hem), a tear or two. - `boards-0`, `boards-1`: 320 by 624 (1.2 m by 2.4 m). Boards fixed over a window from outside before a storm: 0 two sheets of weathered plywood, grey and delaminating at the edges, a painted number or a cross sprayed in faded red, nail heads with rust runs; 1 three horizontal planks nailed across with gaps between (alpha 0 in the gaps), grey weathered timber. - `shards-0`, `shards-1`: 384 by 384 (1.5 m by 1.5 m). A pane broken out: what is left of the glass, stuck in the frame round the edges as jagged shards, alpha 255 on a shard and 0 in the hole. Shards are bigger at the corners, a few long ones along the bottom; the middle is empty. The game stretches this over any window's glass, so keep shards within about 25% of the size from each edge. Base colour of a shard: its film, grey-green at broken edges. Roughness low on shards (0.05 to 0.2).

### Notes for this job

- Every glass element (window, slider, casement, strip, curtain, display): the frame runs to every edge of the picture. Members are straight, square and of even width. A frame's relief is its profile: the outer face highest, a step down to the glazing bead, the glass lowest (the glass about 40 mm below the frame's face). `relief_mm` about 60.
- Aluminium: chalky, pitted, a little bare metal at corners and scuffs (metal 255 there, 0 on the coating); roughness 0.45 to 0.7 on the coating, 0.3 on bare metal. Timber: roughness 0.85. Glass: roughness 0.04 clean, up to 0.5 in a drip or crust; metal 0.
- The base colour inside the glass is the film's colour (a pale warm grey), not black and not a reflection.
