# Miami residential interiors

The eight rooms contain 32 base-colour maps. Each map is an 8-bit RGB PNG with an embedded sRGB profile. I composed the surfaces at 176 pixels per metre, then resampled each finished face to 512 × 512. I used the built-in image generator for the furniture elevations, fittings and material swatches. The layouts, surface wear, floor patterns and joins were painted in metric coordinates with Pillow and NumPy.

The cross images use one scale of 90 pixels per metre. Both back-wall corner columns share the side wall’s rear edge texel for texel. The flood mark, skirting, two-tone paint and corridor rails have the same height on all three walls.

The left panel of each window check is the centred view. The right panel moves the eye 3 m left and turns it toward the same opening. Both use the same lens, an eye height of 1.7 m and a 3 m perpendicular distance to the façade. Each ray samples the first box face behind the opening. The previews show the source colours directly so that dirt and material values can be judged before the game adds reflections and depth shading. In the 2.9 m rooms the ceiling intersects the top 10 cm of the nominal window. That strip takes the façade colour.

## Room 00 · Living room

I set the sagging fabric sofa beside an open hall, carried the pale yellow paint over exposed grey plaster, and laid a worn woven rug slightly askew on square tile. A muted painted closet door gives the side view a scale cue. The centred view keeps the cushion breaks, armrests and dark hall legible at quarter size. The cross shows the lone blade and corroded fan motor clearly. Seen edge-on through the glass, the fan reads mostly as its hub and a short stroke. [Cross](checks/cross-room-00.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-00.png)

## Room 01 · Bedroom

I brought the bare mattress and headboard inside the window’s sightline and set the open wardrobe alongside them. The blue plaster carries a damp bloom across the ceiling junction. The floor uses staggered wood-print vinyl strips with three lifted ends. In the centred view the stained mattress top and dark wardrobe opening read separately. The side view catches a second patch of mould at ceiling height. The small empty light rose loses its socket detail at quarter size. [Cross](checks/cross-room-01.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-01.png)

## Room 02 · Kitchen

I fitted the cabinet run, sink and damaged tilework to the rear elevation. A cleaner wall patch and an old outlet mark the refrigerator bay. The chequered vinyl and the two-tone washable wall finish continue past it. The crooked upper door is legible against its dark opening in the centred view. A sliver of chequerboard shows below the plinth. The refrigerator gap is easiest to identify in the cross, where its full width is visible. [Cross](checks/cross-room-02.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-02.png)

## Room 03 · Stripped flat

I cut patches through the plaster to reveal courses of rough concrete block. The loose wires continue from a ceiling junction onto the rear wall. I folded one broken chair across the wall-floor joint, with its slatted back leaning on the plaster and its seat and snapped legs on the slab. The cross shows the two halves line up. The frontal window now catches the backrest, and the side view catches the exposed masonry. The chair’s broken legs still fall below the sill. [Cross](checks/cross-room-03.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-03.png)

## Room 04 · Flooded flat

I ran a brown sediment line at 1.1 m, varied its edge by a few centimetres, and laid vertical mineral streaks below it. The dark dresser sits just under that line. Broad silt deposits and drying cracks cover the floor, with separate overlapping leak rings overhead. The line stays level through both folded corners and remains visible in the offset view at quarter size. At one-sixteenth size the dresser and lower wall begin to merge into one dark band. [Cross](checks/cross-room-04.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-04.png)

## Room 05 · Empty flat

I set the oak bookshelf into a painted casing, left two slim books together on one shelf, and placed cleaner picture rectangles with small fixing holes on the plaster. The grey parquet alternates the grain direction in five-finger blocks, with several fingers missing down to the adhesive. The centred view retains the shelf divisions and the offset view retains one picture ghost. The two books merge into one small mark on reduction. [Cross](checks/cross-room-05.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-05.png)

## Room 13 · Apartment lobby

I moved the mailbox bank and the double stair doors into a tighter group after the first window check cropped the door pair. The walls have two green paint finishes. The terrazzo has a border, divider strips, branching cracks and one broken ceiling panel beneath an open grid bay. Both stair-door leaves and several open mailbox cavities show in the frontal check. The ceiling grid and painted rail carry the depth into the offset view. Individual mailbox doors become indistinct at quarter size. [Cross](checks/cross-room-13.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-13.png)

## Room 14 · Residential corridor

I placed two unnumbered veneer doors on the long side wall and centred the green stair door at the far end. The faded runner and three unlit square diffuser fittings establish the 8 m depth. The frontal view shows the ceiling fittings receding toward the end door. At the offset angle a nearer room door fills the opening. The runner’s woven guard stripes are clearest in the cross and become a single narrow edge at distance. [Cross](checks/cross-room-14.png) · [Window views](checks/view-room-14.png)
