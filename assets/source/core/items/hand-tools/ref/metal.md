# Forged hand tools

- **Hatchet.** The Cobb Broad Hatchet suggests a short wood haft and a wide hewing bit. SNARK uses a squared poll, a low swept heel, and a silver band that runs the full height of the bit. The continuous cutting edge and exposed eye distinguish it from the lashed flint hatchet. [Cully Cobb Forest Resources Tool Collection, C_FR_CC_2010_0032](https://scholarsjunction.msstate.edu/msugm-cobb-cobbtoolcoll/69/)
- **Pick.** A double-headed sinking pick from the Victorian goldfields suggests the long transverse head and distinct swelling around the haft. SNARK pairs a long curved point with a shorter chisel and brightens both tips. The second end and the clear gap under each arm distinguish it from the lashed stone pick. [Museums Victoria, ST 19661](https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/items/404639)
- **Sickle.** The Ward reaping hook suggests a short wooden handle and a sweeping forged blade. SNARK opens the crescent around a large gap and uses a bright inner bevel and an iron ferrule. The empty crescent remains visible when the texture drops away. [Powerhouse Collection, H7783](https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/249189)

The models use original geometry. The museum records were consulted for visual reference.
