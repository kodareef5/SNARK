You are painting the concept design of four small weapons for a game: a grenade, a bola, an arrow and a tranquilizer dart. Work only in the current folder. Do not run git commands that write.

## What the pictures are for

A survival game starts every player with nothing. They make stone tools, then a bow and its **arrows** (a flint head on a wooden shaft); a **bola** of three stones on cords that they throw to tangle a running animal's legs; later, once they forge metal and make gunpowder, an iron **grenade**, and **tranquilizer darts** fired from a rifle to put a wild animal to sleep. The grenade and the bola are held in first person at the bottom of the screen, closer than anything else in the game; the arrow and the dart are seen flying, and stuck in what they hit. All are small pictures in the inventory, 64 pixels a side.

Your pictures are the design. After you, a modeller builds the low-poly 3D meshes straight from your orthographic views, and the materials (already made, or being made now) are laid on them. So the views must be exact enough to model from, consistent enough that the views describe one object, and beautiful enough to put on a studio wall.

They are our own design. Do not search the web, and do not look at any pictures of weapons from games. Work from this description. In your image prompts describe the weapons in your own words and do not name any game, studio or franchise.

## The look

The game's style is readable, chunky low-poly with realistic matte surfaces: forms built from big, clean, flat facets you could count, carrying fine natural detail in their surfaces. A little playful, never cartoonish. Think of well-made hand-crafted objects, slightly exaggerated so they read at a glance. The game's first tools (a stone pick, hatchet and spear) are already made in this style: pale honey-tan wood, tan plant-fibre cord in thick tight bindings with cut tails, knapped flint with a pale fresh edge, grey stone.

**What the four share:** each is made of at most three materials from this set: honey-tan **wood**, straw-tan fibre **cord**, dark knapped **flint** and grey **stone**, brown **hide** leather, and dark **steel** with **brass** fittings. Chunky: about 15 to 20% fatter than the real thing.

**Grenade (ours; paint it exactly):** a hand-made iron bomb. A chunky **egg of dark cast iron** cut into **eight broad flat facets** round its middle (an octagon seen end-on) that taper to a rounded base; a **brass fuse cap** on its narrow top; a **steel pull ring** through a pin in the cap; a **steel lever** (a spoon) that runs from the cap down one side, close against the body; a **band of brown hide** round its waist, laced with cord, where the hand grips it. Steel and brass, hide, cord: no paint, no text.

**Bola (ours):** three fist-sized round **grey stones each sewn into a pouch of brown hide**, its mouth gathered and tied tight with cord where the cord enters; three **fibre cords** about 0.42 m long, joined at one **thick knot** with a short loop to hold. It is shown two ways: as it lies on the ground, the cords spread in a slack Y; and as it is held by the knot ready to throw, the cords gathered and running forward from the hand, fanning a little, the three weights bunched together at their ends.

**Arrow (ours):** a straight **wooden shaft**, a **leaf-shaped knapped flint head** with a pale fresh edge set into the split end of the shaft and bound with a **thong of hide**, three **cut-leather vanes** for fletching (no feathers), and a plain carved nock.

**Tranquilizer dart (ours):** a stubby hand-made syringe: a **steel needle**, a fat **brass barrel** that holds the drug, a steel collar and end cap, and a **tuft of hide fringe** at its tail as a flight.

## What must hold

- **The views agree.** For each piece, the views describe one object: every length in one view matches the others within 1% of the piece's length, and widths and heights within 1 mm. Each band, tie, cord, vane and fitting is the same in every view where it shows. Measure it, draw guide lines across the views side by side and look. If a view disagrees, fix it or make it again; do not stretch a drawing to fit.
- **The sizes hold.** Measure the extents and the parts in pixels and convert to metres: within 3% of the numbers above.
- **Nothing added** beyond what is described.

## What excellent looks like

Prop concept art a studio would pin to its wall: four objects you instantly believe someone made by hand, and that read in one glance even as black shapes 32 pixels across: the grenade a faceted iron egg with its ring and lever, the bola three round weights on a Y of cord, the arrow a long line with a leaf and vanes, the dart a stubby brass syringe with a tuft. Up close, dark oiled steel with bright worn edges, warm brass, supple hide with its gathered ties, knapped flint with a pale edge, tight twisted cord, honest grained wood. Someone who has played survival games should recognise each immediately, and on a second look see it is its own design.

Look at every picture you make, compare the views against one another and against this description, and keep going until they agree and you would stand behind them.
