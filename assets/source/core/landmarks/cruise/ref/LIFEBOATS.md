# Lifeboat references

The two original photographs by **Concord**, taken 11 February 2025, show Icon of the Seas' actual PALFINGER boats. They are retained unmodified under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/): [photograph 01](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Icon_of_the_Seas_Palfinger_01.jpg), [photograph 03](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Icon_of_the_Seas_Palfinger_03.jpg). Exact file hashes, source URLs and credits are in `lifeboat-sources.json`.

The original modeled fitting uses the yellow upper canopy, white lower hull, paired fabric entry doors with small windows, rounded cockpit, perimeter rubrail and hanging grab lines visible in these photographs. It is original geometry; no photograph pixels enter production textures.

The archived [PALFINGER Boats and Davits catalogue](https://assets.palfinger.com/cache-buster-1574941498/importdata/marine/events/marintec/palfinger-marine-boats-and-davits-brochure.pdf), printed pages 16–17 (PDF page 9), lists the **MPC 49** family at **15 × 5.5 × 4.2 metres** and 450 persons. This is the chosen modeling envelope, rather than a dimension inferred from photographic perspective. The photographed boat itself displays 450 persons. The catalogue is manufacturer reference material, not an open-licensed production asset.

A separately indexed manufacturer product page lists 5.52 m beam. Its live URL returned 404 when checked on 16 November 2026. We retain the directly retrievable, archived catalogue's 5.5 m baseline and record that small published discrepancy here. The family catalogue does not establish every custom fitting or the exact hanging height on Icon. Those details are original interpretations of the actual photographs. The small open rescue craft in photograph 03 is a separate boat and should not be counted as another full-size lifeboat.

The older Swedish catalogue URL redirected to a homepage during verification. Use the archived manufacturer PDF above for the dimensional evidence. A 2025 manufacturer release describing eighteen boats for a later Icon-class ship is not evidence of the original ship's exact installed arrangement.
