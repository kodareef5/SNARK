# Berry bushes: concept art, round 1

You are the concept artist for the berry bushes of a survival game. The game plays like a well-known dinosaur survival game: a player wakes on a shore with nothing, and the first thing they do is strip bushes by hand for berries and fibre. So these bushes are the first thing every new player looks for, and they must be found at a glance among everything else that is green. There are several kinds of berry, told apart only by colour (red, deep blue, violet, yellow-orange, near black, and white), and they all grow on the same kind of bush. Anyone who has played that game should know at once what these are for; the bushes themselves are ours.

The world is a city by a warm bay, centuries after it was abandoned: pale limestone and concrete ruins, broken roads, palms, fig trees with hanging roots, mangrove at the water, and an undergrowth of palmetto, fern, sea grape and vines taking it all back. A painted map of the place is in `../../../../../../maps/miami/tiles/z0/0_0.png` (it is a map, not a style for these pictures). The berry bush grows in that undergrowth: in clearings, along old streets, at the foot of ruined walls.

A 3D artist will build exactly what you paint, so paint plants that can be built. The bush will be built from a few dozen flat cut-out pictures of leafy twigs, each a spray of leaves on one or two stems, set on a short woody frame. So the leaves must come in **clear sprays and clusters with readable outlines**, layered into a rounded mass with real gaps and shadowed hollows between them: not a ball of green noise, not a hedge clipped smooth, not individually modelled leaves by the thousand. The berries sit **on the outside** of that mass, where a hand would reach them, in clusters at the tips of the sprays and along them, visible from every side and from above.

## Sizes

0.9 to 1.3 m tall, 1.0 to 1.6 m across: about waist high on a 1.8 m person, a little wider than tall. Leaves 3 to 8 cm long. A berry 0.8 to 1.5 cm, in clusters of 5 to 30.

## What excellent looks like

- **Found at a glance.** At 15 m, among grass, ferns, palmetto and rubble, the eye goes straight to it. Its shape is distinct from the plants around it (rounder, denser, a darker and cooler green, glossy), and its berries are a scatter of bright points across its whole outside.
- **Berries read by colour.** The berries are the only colour on it that is not green or brown. Each berry is a little sphere with its own highlight; clusters hang a little under their own weight. The leaves are a green that red, blue, violet, yellow, black and white berries all stand out against.
- **A real plant.** Woody stems from a short base, branching as a real shrub branches; sprays that lean outward and up to the light; inner leaves darker and fewer; a few yellowed or bitten leaves; the odd dead twig. Not symmetrical.
- **Picked clean, it looks picked.** The same bush after a survivor has stripped it: no berries, leaves torn off and fewer, the outer sprays snapped and hanging, bare twig ends showing where clusters were, a few leaves and a broken spray on the ground. Still alive, plainly raided.

What we do not want: a cartoon, a lollipop or topiary shape, berries buried inside the leaves, berries so large they look like fruit, a uniform texture of leaves, anything copied from an existing game.

**Style.** A still from a finished, high-end modern survival game at maximum settings: realistic plants, materials and light, with shapes chunky and readable enough for a game where hundreds are in view. Colours natural, warm light, the hot humid coast.

**Light.** Warm afternoon sun at an angle, soft sky light in the shadows, so the mass of the bush and its hollows show.

## This round

1. **Three design directions** for the bush, each a different idea of what this shrub is: its leaf shape and gloss, its branching habit, how the berries are carried (for example: small glossy leaves on stiff twigs with berries in clusters at the tips; looser arching stems with berries in tight bunches all along them; broad leathery leaves with berries in hanging clusters among them). Draw on the real shrubs of a subtropical coast for inspiration, but make each our own. For each direction:
   - the bush alone, a three-quarter view from where a player stands, about 2.5 m away at eye height (1.7 m), with red berries, on a plain sandy-grassy ground;
   - the same bush at 15 m, in a clearing among ruins and other undergrowth, to test whether it is found at a glance.
2. Say which direction is strongest and why, then for that one:
   - a row of six of the bush, one in each berry colour (red, deep blue, violet, yellow-orange, near black, white), the same view;
   - three different individuals of it (a young compact one, a mature full one, an old sprawling one), so a 3D artist can see how they differ;
   - the bush picked clean, the same view as the first;
   - the bush seen from above at a steep angle (a player standing over it);
   - one leafy twig with its berry cluster, laid flat and seen straight on against a plain neutral grey background, as a 3D artist will cut it out: the spray of leaves, the stem it grows from, and its berries. And the same twig with its berries picked off.
