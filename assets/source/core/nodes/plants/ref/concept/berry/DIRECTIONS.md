# Berry bushes — round 1

**Selection: A, Forkleaf.** All three are original fictional coastal shrubs developed from the written brief. The names describe design directions, not berry types; all six berry colours belong on the same chosen species.

## A — Forkleaf

[Close study](direction_a.png) · [Distance study](direction_a_far.png)

Small, smooth elliptical leaves with short points and waxy gloss sit on stiff, outward-and-upward forked twigs. Several woody stems branch close to the ground; overlapping sprays form an uneven rounded crown with open hollows. Loose red clusters project from tips and outer axils, including the crown. At 2.5 m, the branching, leaf groups and individually highlighted berries are readable. In the intended 15 m view, the dark rounded body and scattered red points remain a coherent harvest cue. Its weakness is the fine pedicel structure: it will disappear before the larger foliage shapes do, and near-black fruit has less colour contrast against the shaded leaves.

## B — Tidebow

[Close study](direction_b.png) · [Distance study](direction_b_far.png)

Narrower, satin-gloss leaves follow loose arching stems from a low woody base. Small hanging fruit groups repeat along exposed outer nodes and ends, leaving leaf-only intervals between them. At 2.5 m, the bows make the weight of the fruit and the reach of each spray particularly clear. At distance, the separated arches and narrow leaves begin to merge with grasses and fine undergrowth; the red dots do more of the identification work. Its weakness is a less solid rounded silhouette, and fruit beneath the arches is more vulnerable to occlusion when viewed from above. It also demands more attention to twig-card orientation along each curve.

## C — Shellleaf

[Close study](direction_c.png) · [Distance study](direction_c_far.png)

Broad, rounded spoon leaves form leathery, glossy fans on crooked stocky branches. The fans project as irregular shelves, with short pendant berry clusters beyond their lips and additional fruit above the crown. At 2.5 m, the broad blades, hanging clusters and deep inter-shelf openings are easy to understand and translate into cards. The chunky leaf groups also survive the distance view well. Its weakness is the conspicuous tiered rhythm: it risks looking cultivated or bonsai-like, and its broad foliage competes with nearby sea grape. Repeating the same fan too regularly would exaggerate that ornamental quality.

## Why Forkleaf wins

A gives the strongest overall balance of a natural shrub, a familiar hand-harvest cue and a practical card assembly. Its fruit is distributed across the whole outer mass rather than depending mainly on hanging below leaf shelves. Its medium-sized leaves separate it from both fine ferns and broad sea grape without making it exotic. C has particularly strong individual foliage shapes, but A has the more useful continuous rounded silhouette and fewer directional fruit-visibility problems. Keep the open hollows and uneven shoulders: filling those gaps would remove much of its identity.

Build targets for Forkleaf: mature height 1.1 m, spread 1.4 m; young about 0.9 × 1.05 m; old about 1.05 × 1.6 m. Leaves 4–6 cm, berries 0.8–1.2 cm, generally 8–18 berries per cluster. Use roughly thirty one- or two-stem leafy sprays around a short branching woody frame, with fewer, darker inner leaves. The flat spray establishes the relative leaf/fruit scale; retain small spheres rather than enlarging berries for visibility.

The distance plates are concept studies aimed at a 15 m standing-player view, not measured engine captures. They share the same clearing and lighting for comparison, with small generative differences in framing and surroundings. The all-colour sheet tests the close view; only red was requested and tested in the distant environment.

## Files and review record

Eighteen images were generated and visually reviewed; twelve were selected and six superseded images are preserved in [round1](round1/README.md). [prompts.md](prompts.md) contains every exact prompt, its input references and output mapping. The local map informed the setting only; no external images were searched, downloaded or supplied as references.

All PNGs are the full native files returned by the built-in image tool, without resizing, upscaling or repainting: the six direction plates, picked and above are **1536 × 1024**; colours and individuals are **2172 × 724**; the twig pair is **1254 × 1254**. Every prompt requested the largest available native output. These are concept references, including grey-backed twig studies, rather than alpha-masked production textures.
