# Sea grape and creeper: concept art, round 1

You are the concept artist for the low plants of a survival game. The game plays like a well-known dinosaur survival game: players harvest the world by hand and with tools (fibre and thatch from plants, wood from trees, stone from rocks), and the plants are what fills the ground between everything else. They are the smallest and most numerous things in the world: a player stands among hundreds of them, walks through them, picks them, and sees them carpet the ground to the horizon.

The world is a city by a warm bay, centuries after it was abandoned: pale limestone and concrete ruins, broken roads, palms, fig trees with hanging roots, mangrove at the water, and an undergrowth of palmetto, fern, berry bushes, sea grape and vines taking it all back. A painted map of the place is in `../../../../../../maps/miami/tiles/z0/0_0.png` (it is a map, not a style for these pictures). This job is two of that undergrowth's plants, and one picture of all of it together:

- **Sea grape.** A sprawling coastal shrub of the dunes and the beach edge: big round leathery leaves 12 to 25 cm across, flat or a little cupped, with pale or red veins, on short stalks along crooked smooth grey-brown branches; some old leaves turn red and orange; hanging clusters of round green-to-purple fruit like loose bunches of grapes. Shrubs 1.2 to 2.5 m tall and 2 to 3 m across, low and wind-shaped on the shore.
- **Creeper.** A mass of climbing and trailing vine that smothers whatever it grows over: rubble, a dead shrub, the foot of a wall. Heart-shaped or lobed leaves 5 to 15 cm on long runners, the runners spilling over the edges of the mass and hanging in loose curtains, a few trumpet flowers. It must look right standing alone in the open (a vine-smothered mound) and also heaped against the foot of a ruined wall. Masses 0.6 to 2 m tall and 1.5 to 3 m across.

A 3D artist will build exactly what you paint, so paint plants that can be built. Each is built from a small number of flat cut-out pictures and simple stems: for the sea grape, single leaves and fruit clusters set on a branching frame; for the creeper, sprays of leaves on runners laid over a mound, and hanging strands. So **leaves must come singly (sea grape) or in clear sprays (creeper), with readable outlines**, and the plant's beauty must come from how they are arranged. Not a mass of texture.

## What excellent looks like

- **The silhouette says what it is** from 2 m to 100 m: the sea grape a low, wide, open shrub of big round leaves on twisting branches; the creeper a lumpy blanket of leaves with ragged trailing edges.
- **A real plant.** Leaves of different ages and sizes, facing the light, a few red or holed; the twisting branches of the sea grape showing between its leaves; the runners of the creeper reaching out over the ground at its edge. Not symmetrical.
- **At home in the ruins.** Sea grape at the sandy edge of the bay among broken concrete; creeper over rubble and up the foot of limestone walls.

What we do not want: a cartoon, a uniform texture of leaves, plastic gloss, anything copied from an existing game.

**Style.** A still from a finished, high-end modern survival game at maximum settings: realistic plants, materials and light, with shapes chunky and readable enough for a game where hundreds are in view. Colours natural, warm light, the hot humid coast.

**Light.** Warm afternoon sun at an angle, soft sky light in the shadows.

## This round

For each of the two plants:

- the plant alone, a three-quarter view from about 3.5 m at eye height (1.7 m), on sandy ground with a little rubble;
- three different individuals side by side (young, mature, old or sprawling), so a 3D artist can see how they differ;
- the plant after a survivor has harvested it: the sea grape stripped of most of its leaves and fruit, broken twigs; the creeper torn back, runners ripped and hanging, leaves on the ground;
- the elements a 3D artist will cut out, laid flat and seen straight on against a plain neutral grey background: for the sea grape a few single leaves (young green, mature, red-veined, an old red-orange one, a holed one) and a fruit cluster; for the creeper a few leafy sprays on their runners and a hanging strand.

And one picture of the whole undergrowth together: the foot of a ruined pale limestone wall beside a cracked street at the edge of the bay, seen from about 8 m at eye height, with creeper heaped against the wall, sea grape toward the water, saw palmetto, a fern clump in the wall's shade, grasses, and a berry bush (a rounded shrub of small dark glossy leaves with clusters of red berries on its outside). This is the picture the rest of the work will be measured against: make it a place a player would want to explore.
