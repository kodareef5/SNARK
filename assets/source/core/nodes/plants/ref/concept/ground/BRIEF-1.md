# Saw palmetto and fern: concept art, round 1

You are the concept artist for the low plants of a survival game. The game plays like a well-known dinosaur survival game: players harvest the world by hand and with tools (fibre and thatch from plants, wood from trees, stone from rocks), and the plants are what fills the ground between everything else. They are the smallest and most numerous things in the world: a player stands among hundreds of them, walks through them, picks them, and sees them carpet the ground to the horizon.

The world is a city by a warm bay, centuries after it was abandoned: pale limestone and concrete ruins, broken roads, palms, fig trees with hanging roots, mangrove at the water, and an undergrowth taking it all back. A painted map of the place is in `../../../../../../maps/miami/tiles/z0/0_0.png` (it is a map, not a style for these pictures). This job is two of that undergrowth's plants:

- **Saw palmetto.** A low clumping fan palm: stiff fan-shaped leaves, each split into many narrow pointed segments, on long thin stalks edged with small teeth, rising from fibrous trunks that creep along the ground; old leaves turn orange-brown and hang down at the base. Yellow-green to silvery blue-green. Clumps 0.9 to 1.6 m tall and 1.5 to 2.5 m across; a fan 0.5 to 0.9 m wide on a stalk 0.4 to 1 m long.
- **Fern clump.** A clump of arching sword-shaped fronds, each a long stalk with many small leaflets either side, rising from a crown at the ground; young fronds uncurling at the centre, old ones browned and lying at the edge. Fresh bright green. Clumps 0.5 to 1.0 m tall, 0.8 to 1.5 m across; fronds 0.5 to 1.1 m long.

A 3D artist will build exactly what you paint, so paint plants that can be built. Each is built from a small number of flat cut-out pictures (one palmetto fan, one fern frond) bent into shape and set on simple stems. So **every fan and every frond must be clear and separate, with a readable outline**, and the clump's beauty must come from how a dozen or two of them are arranged: their angles, lengths, ages and overlaps. Not a mass of texture.

## What excellent looks like

- **The silhouette says what it is** from 2 m to 100 m: the palmetto a spiky fountain of fans; the fern a soft rosette of arching blades.
- **A real plant.** Fans and fronds of different ages and sizes, leaning out and up to the light, some broken or browned, the inside of the clump darker; the creeping trunks and the old leaf bases of the palmetto; the fiddleheads and the dead fronds of the fern. Not symmetrical, never a perfect circle.
- **At home in the ruins.** They grow from cracked pavement, at the foot of walls, among rubble and sand.

What we do not want: a cartoon, a perfect radial pattern, a uniform texture of leaves, plastic gloss, anything copied from an existing game.

**Style.** A still from a finished, high-end modern survival game at maximum settings: realistic plants, materials and light, with shapes chunky and readable enough for a game where hundreds are in view. Colours natural, warm light, the hot humid coast.

**Light.** Warm afternoon sun at an angle, soft sky light in the shadows.

## This round

For each of the two plants:

- the clump alone, a three-quarter view from about 3 m at eye height (1.7 m), on sandy ground with a little rubble;
- three different individuals side by side (young, mature, old or sprawling), so a 3D artist can see how they differ;
- the clump after a survivor has harvested it: the palmetto with its fans cut away to a few stubs and the youngest spear, cut fans on the ground; the fern torn back to its crown with a few broken fronds;
- a group of them in their place: palmetto in a sunny lot among ruins, ferns in the shade at the foot of a ruined wall, seen from about 15 m;
- the element a 3D artist will cut out, laid flat and seen straight on against a plain neutral grey background: one palmetto fan on its toothed stalk (and a dead brown one beside it); one fern frond (and a young uncurling one and a dead brown one beside it).
