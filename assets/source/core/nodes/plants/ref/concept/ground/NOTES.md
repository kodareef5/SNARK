# Low plants — round 1

## Saw palmetto

Build each live or dead fan as one cutout blade with a shallow pleated bend, attached to a slender toothed petiole; use simple geometry for one or two creeping fibrous trunks, old leaf bases and the closed spear. The mature clump targets 14–18 live fans, two drooping dead fans and one spear, within roughly 1.25 m height and 2 m spread. Stagger attachment points and rotate fans through broadside, oblique and near-edge-on views; keep the rear higher, one side spreading low, and openings through the front. Young plants use 8–10 smaller, yellower fans; old plants widen around offset crowns on exposed horizontal trunks, with more damaged tips and brown leaves. The stiff pointed fans, long visible stalks and rusty basal skirt give the immediate spiky-fountain read. Harvesting retains the base, a few cut petioles and the spear, with detached fans on the ground. Weaknesses: overlapping rear fans still compress into a dense centre, and the fine points and stalk teeth risk shimmering or vanishing at distance; preserve the large gaps when building lower-detail versions.

## Fern

Build each sword-shaped frond as one cutout strip carrying its central rachis and simple leaflet rows, with several bends along its length to make a continuous upward-and-outward arch. Add plain slender lower stalks, a small ground-hugging crown, and curled young elements. The mature target is about 16 green fronds, two fiddleheads and two brown ground-level fronds, approximately 0.75 m high and 1.2 m across. Place shorter new blades inside, longer arches outside, unequal heights and an open front wedge; avoid equal angular spacing. Young individuals use roughly nine fresh upright fronds; old ones spread lower and wider, with worn outer tips and extra dead material. Long tapering arcs and the soft, uneven rosette distinguish it immediately from the palmetto. The harvested version exposes thin torn stalks and two broken frond remnants. Weaknesses: the leaflet rhythm remains fairly regular, and the narrow gaps between leaflets will be fragile at distance; retain whole-frond outlines and the open spacing between arches as detail falls away.
