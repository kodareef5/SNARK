**Ship decision: hold this set for another modelling pass.** The five plant families are present, and several individual leaves are convincing. The assembly is much weaker than the artwork: the bush exposes its card construction, the palmetto resembles a bouquet of flat fans, the fern lacks a soft arching crown, the sea grape has shelves of leaves on sticks, and the creeper resembles a solid object upholstered with foliage. The creeper's harvested state and the fern's low setting are particularly clear failures of meaning.

I reviewed all nine game images, the six principal concepts, the concept construction notes, and the relevant fruit, individual, and harvested studies. The visual benchmark I would take from foliage in games such as Uncharted 4 and The Last of Us Part II is coherent growth: branches support leaves, overlapping groups make volume, and a few characteristic shapes survive at distance. This renderer can achieve those qualities. I am not grading the test ground, sky, missing shadows, or the absence of the concepts' cinematic lighting.

The counts and dimensions below are **proposed construction targets**, informed by the concept notes, not measurements of the current meshes. Redistribute and bend existing geometry before assuming that more cards are needed. Still images establish the visible problems; they do not establish whether normals, materials, alpha handling, or geometry caused every one of them.

**Berry bush: highest priority, because it carries the food-finding interaction.** Compare `game_bush_four_sides.png` with `berry/direction_a.png` and `berry/twig.png`.

1. **Replace the hollow shell with a branching, overlapping crown.** All four views expose large holes through the middle, detached-looking outer sprays, and thin dark blades where whole sprays turn edge-on. The concept has deliberate hollows within a connected shrub; these views repeatedly reveal the assembly. Start around **30–34 leafy sprays** on **3–5 basal stems** with actual secondary forks: roughly **22–26 outer sprays and 6–8 smaller inner sprays**. Place the inner sprays at different depths across the largest through-holes. Keep two or three irregular branch windows rather than closing everything into a ball. Aim at the concept's mature **1.1 m height and 1.4 m spread**, with one higher shoulder and a low lateral reach.
2. **Make each spray grow from a branch.** Use roughly **20–35 cm spray units**, preserving **4–6 cm leaves**. The repeated large planar patches currently become the visible units of the bush. Give a spray a shallow bend or two offset planes; vary neighbouring planes by roughly **15–40 degrees**, with some facing upward and others outward. Stagger attachments along forked twigs instead of arranging a ring of outward-facing cards. Trace every conspicuous peripheral spray back to supporting wood. Check the dark edge-on patches from all four sides; adding a second identical crossed card everywhere would just create a different repeated construction.
3. **Rebuild the fruit presentation.** At 3 m, the fruit reads as flat dusty-pink flower clusters distributed over leafy panels. It does not give the concept's exposed, round, red berries. Start with **16–24 distinct fruit clusters**, each containing about **8–18 small berries**, at outer tips, upper shoulders, and a few crown-centre openings. Keep berries near the concept's **0.8–1.2 cm** size; improve exposure and cluster grouping before enlarging individual fruit. Separate clusters from the surrounding leaf plane and give the important ones depth, so they survive an oblique view. Preserve saturated body colour over most of each berry; a small highlight must not bleach the whole cluster pink. More tiny pink marks throughout the canopy will increase noise.
4. **Keep the picked bush recognisable as the same plant.** Removing fruit in `game_row_bush.png` already changes the colour signal, which is useful. Keep the same woody frame and most of the green crown; expose empty fruit stalks at the previously visible cluster sites. If following the rough-harvest concept, use **two or three damaged outer sprays**, not a generally skeletal bush. Fruit removal should remain the main state change. The existing open construction makes the picked bush look underbuilt even before damage is added.

Keep the low multi-stem habit, small pointed leaves, uneven overall outline, and fruit distributed around multiple sides. The concept's young, mature, and old plants differ in branching and lateral reach; preserve that distinction instead of solving all individuals with the same spherical shell at different scales.

**Saw palmetto: immediately identifiable on medium, but too flat and too silvery.** Compare `game_row_palmetto.png`, the central plant in `game_patch.png`, and `ground/palmetto.png`.

1. **Give the fans folded surfaces and a connected clump.** The current fans read as separate, nearly flat discs on thin spokes. Their fine repeated teeth are stronger than the broad leaf mass. Use **14–18 live fans on a mature clump**, **8–10 on a young one**, with **one closed spear and about two dead fans**. A mature target is **1.25 m high by 2 m wide**, with principal fans roughly **40–65 cm across**. Form each fan from several shallow radial folds, keeping a joined basal area and broad fingers. Bend some tips out of the main plane; do not make every fan a perfectly circular comb.
2. **Arrange a fountain with age and gravity.** Put young narrow fans near the crown, the taller mature fans behind and above it, and older fans spreading low on one side. Across the clump, mix broadside fans with **20–50 degree oblique turns** and a few edge-on fans. Avoid a line of equally upright presentation discs. Use **one or two offset crowns** on a short creeping trunk. The exposed brown forms currently read rather like loose tapered logs; old leaf bases and a compact fibrous junction should visibly connect trunk, petioles, and fans.
3. **Pull the palette toward living foliage.** The dominant pale blue-grey is substantially chalkier than the concept's olive and muted blue-green. Keep the cool identity, but lower the pale surface value and make most mature fans greener. Reserve yellow-green for a few emerging fans and rust brown for the basal skirt. Some leaves turn very dark while neighbours are pale; inspect their orientation and front/back shading before compensating by brightening the atlas.
4. **Make the cut crown carry the harvested state.** The rear examples read largely as scattered fan shapes around logs and a spear. Retain a compact base with **5–8 obvious cut petioles**, unequal heights, small light cut ends, the central spear, and **one or two attached dead fans**. Use only **2–3 detached fans** as secondary evidence, resting against the ground with their stalks attached. The recognisable object should be the cut plant, not its debris field.

Keep the fan silhouette, long petioles, creeping habit, contrasting new growth, and rusty dead leaves. Those already separate it well from the fern. It needs volume and a better colour balance more urgently than additional fine teeth.

**Fern: the strongest close leaf artwork, weakened by stiff construction.** `game_inside.png` shows good leaflet and fiddlehead detail; `game_row_fern.png` shows how much the whole-plant shape still needs work.

1. **Turn the radial star into an arching crown.** Many fronds project as straight swords or bend only near the end. Several plants have a bare centre and widely separated spokes. Build a mature plant around **14–18 green fronds**, with **two fiddleheads and one or two old brown fronds**; use **8–10 green fronds** for a genuinely young plant. Aim around **0.75 m high and 1.2 m across**. Group short upright growth inside, medium arches above it, and longer outward arches around the outside. Vary angular gaps; keep one open wedge rather than equal spacing all around.
2. **Spend geometry on the curve.** Give principal fronds **4–6 sections along their length**, rising near the crown and turning progressively outward and down. Outer tips should fall below their highest point. As a starting range, use principal fronds around **55–80 cm long and 10–18 cm wide**, with shorter inner growth. Add a slight fold along the midrib and modest twist between fronds. Their soft tapering arcs must remain distinct from the palmetto's stiff fans. Adding more straight cards would make a denser star.
3. **Unify the living and dead colours.** Keep warm yellow-green young fronds and deeper green outer fronds, but reduce the abrupt light/dark jumps between adjacent sheets. The conspicuous pale grey dead fronds look like bleached cutouts; bring them toward the concept's muted brown, make them droop to the ground, and keep their count low. Preserve the good leaflet shape and central rib. Fine texture detail already exceeds what the overall silhouette supports.
4. **Give harvesting a visible rooted remainder.** The cut examples nearly disappear into detached scraps and tiny centres. Retain a small fibrous crown, **5–7 green cut or torn stalks**, **one or two damaged partial fronds**, and a fiddlehead. Stalk remnants can reach roughly **15–30 cm** with unequal ends. One or two fallen pieces are sufficient. Avoid a woody stump: the concept's cut fern is recognisable through thin green stalks and surviving curl, not a miniature tree base.

Keep the feathered leaflet rhythm, taper, fresh growth, and visible fiddlehead. The fern is already convincing in parts at close range; bending and layering those parts will improve it more than repainting them.

**Sea grape: good leaf identity, weak branching and canopy depth.** Compare `game_row_seagrape.png` and the foreground of `game_inside.png` with `coast/seagrape.png`.

1. **Build the crooked spreading frame first.** The current limbs read as a few clean poles and simple forks with leaf stacks at their ends. The concept's identity includes branching along low, wandering limbs. Target **5–6 basal limbs with 10–16 smaller forks**, approximately **1.45 m high by 2.8 m wide** for the mature individual. Give limbs bends, taper, unequal fork heights, and an obvious long lateral branch on the old plant. Keep several windows through the canopy, but make each leaf group visibly belong to this continuous frame.
2. **Break the horizontal leaf shelves.** In the inside view, overlapping leaves become stacks of dark flat plates. Use roughly **100–150 individual leaf cards**, **12–25 cm wide**, distributed along the forks rather than concentrated in a few terminal piles. Place loose groups of **3–7 leaves at staggered nodes**, with neighbouring leaves tilted roughly **15–45 degrees apart**, and include upright terminal growth. Give large foreground leaves a shallow cup or midrib bend. Preserve their round outlines from several directions; a dense stack viewed edge-on loses the species' strongest feature.
3. **Recover the warm coastal palette.** The current mature leaves tend toward dark, cool green, with less of the concept's yellow-green canopy variation. Use medium olive-green as the main mass, brighter young leaves near selected tips, and a small minority of orange-red old leaves. Keep those warm leaves sparse and recognisably leaf-sized so they do not become competing berry signals. Preserve the veins. Retain about **four hanging fruit racemes** on a mature plant, outside leaf stacks; the inside view already shows that this detail can work.
4. **The stripped state has the right idea; improve its frame.** The mostly bare rear plants are meaningfully different from the live ones. Keep the full crooked branch structure, remove all fruit and roughly **85–95% of leaves**, and leave a few recognisable round leaves at unequal heights. Add a few exposed broken tips if matching the concept. Fixing the live branch network will also stop the harvested version resembling disconnected sticks. Fallen leaves should be incidental, not the primary state cue.

Keep the large round leaves, visible veins, orange ageing leaves, pale wood, pendant fruit, and low spreading proportions. These are strong ingredients. The main need is a more convincing relationship between branches and leaf groups.

**Creeper: largest mismatch between material and form, and the weakest harvest read.** Compare `game_row_creeper.png` with `coast/creeper.png` and `coast/creeper_cut.png`.

1. **Make a draped vine rather than a green solid.** The dominant visible form is a smooth, steep-sided mound with leaf texture wrapped over it. On the taller left specimen, the texture stretches into a vertical curtain without enough actual loose leaves. Scattered surface cards do not conceal that continuous shell. Start from **three unequal support shoulders**, with a mature spread around **2.3–2.6 m** and maximum height **1.0–1.15 m**. Drape **24–30 leafy sprays**, each carrying roughly **5–7 main leaves**, over those shoulders. Overlap them down the slope, turn adjacent sprays differently, and pull their tips away from the support. Keep a few substantial openings beneath overhangs. Any opaque interior used to fill the mass must sit behind the leaf fringe rather than define a smooth outer wall.
2. **The harvested heap must visibly expose its support.** The rear row still looks like smaller, less decorated green mounds. It does not communicate vines torn away from rubble. Preserve the same support profile and footprint, but expose mostly pale stone or rubble with brown connecting stems; leave only **3–5 small foliage patches** and a few torn strands. Aim for roughly **70–85% of the visible support surface exposed**. Do not shrink a fully green mound to indicate depletion. This state needs a material and coverage change as well as fewer loose cards.
3. **Give the plant a ragged perimeter and connected runners.** Add **5–7 hanging strands** and about **four ground runners**, connected through visible curved stems. Let runners reach roughly **0.5–1 m beyond the mound** on selected sides, with successive leaves changing size toward the growing tip. Current scattered flecks around the heap do not consistently read as a vine path. Build a few readable chains, including one longer escape, instead of evenly sprinkling detached leaf marks. The base should end in leaves and strands at different distances.
4. **Separate leaf layers by value and scale.** The surface is too uniformly dark olive and texturally busy. Keep a darker interior, medium green broad leaves over it, and occasional lighter young tips. Preserve the pointed, heart-like leaf shapes at the perimeter; do not let fine repeated texture become the only description of foliage. A few pale lavender trumpets, roughly **2–4 on the mature mound**, would restore a secondary concept cue, but flowers will not solve the form. Keep them much sparser than the berry clusters.

Keep the unequal mound heights, low footprint, and existing attempts at trailing growth. A dense creeper should have the fullest mass of these five plants. That density needs to read as overlapping leaves following support, with a loose edge.

**Would a new player find the berry bushes and recognise the fruit colour? Not reliably enough on medium.** In `game_patch.png`, the repeated rounded shrubs and pink marks can be found once the viewer knows what to seek. In `game_inside.png`, the central bush is conspicuous enough to notice, but its marks suggest blossoms as readily as food. Being the only pink flowering-looking shrub is a learnable cue; it is weaker than an immediately legible fruit-bearing shrub. The four-side view at 3 m does not resolve that ambiguity convincingly.

The concept's red fruit reads as dusty pink in these medium captures. On low, it becomes strongly saturated red angular pieces. That discrepancy matters: changing graphics quality changes the apparent colour category and makes the food signal stronger on the simpler setting. Establish the colour in the game's rendered output, then bring both representations into agreement. Concentrate colour into exposed clusters and keep a subdued, coherent foliage mass behind it.

`berry/colours.png` specifies **red, deep blue, violet, yellow-orange, near-black, and white** on the same foliage. Only the reddish variant is demonstrated in the game images, so the other five cannot be signed off. Blue/violet will need clear hue separation; near-black will need exposed placement and small readable highlights; white must remain distinct from general bright leaf highlights. Yellow-orange must not be confused with the sea grape's large ageing leaves. Keep foliage colour consistent between berry variants. Do not solve visibility by bleaching every fruit type toward the same pale highlight colour.

For the next review, show all six colours, live and picked, with the same camera at **3, 8, and 15 m**, from several sides, on both settings. At 3 m, fruit and its intended colour should be unmistakable; at 8 m, the player should distinguish fruit-bearing from picked bushes; at 15 m, the bush family and exposed fruit groups should remain findable. Those are proposed acceptance checks, not results established by these captures. If colour distinguishes gameplay properties, it also needs a redundant identification cue for players who cannot distinguish the hues.

Flat colour is a viable style for this set. The failure is removing the geometric feature that names the plant. Each low version should preserve the same broad outline, growth habit, fruit cue, and harvest meaning as medium, even with far fewer internal elements.

**The three changes with the greatest return, in order, are:**

1. **Rebuild the relationship between supporting structure and leaf mass.** Close the bush's accidental through-holes with offset inner sprays; curve and layer fern fronds; fold and stagger palmetto fans; distribute sea-grape leaves along crooked forks; drape the creeper over a support that becomes visible when harvested. Use the existing concept counts as starting budgets. This addresses the common problem that the construction is more visible than the plant, and gives each harvested asset a believable persistent base.
2. **Make the berry interaction read, and calibrate the palette around it.** Present small round fruit in exposed groups, restore distinct rendered berry colours, and align medium with low. Keep berry foliage comparatively dark and coherent, fern warm green, palmetto muted blue-green, sea grape olive with sparse orange leaves, and creeper a layered middle green. Review these together under the existing sun. Colour should reinforce the differing forms without making every leaf equally bright or every fruit cluster pastel.
3. **Author low meshes around species-defining outlines.** Prioritise feathered fern fronds, joined palmetto fans, round sea-grape leaves, connected creeper runners, and compact berry clusters. Recheck each beside its medium and harvested versions. The player's ability to identify and harvest a plant must survive the graphics setting change. More atlas detail will not repair the current low silhouettes.
