# Clam shells

The fluted giant clam (*Tridacna squamosa*) gives the first shell its broad
folds and heavy lips. The Atlantic calico scallop (*Argopecten gibbus*) gives
the second shell its rounded fan, radiating ribs and small hinge ears.
[Western Australian Museum](https://museum.wa.gov.au/online-collections/names/Tridacna-squamosa/)
[Bailey-Matthews National Shell Museum](https://shells.shellmuseum.org/shell/argopecten-gibbus/)

The giant clam sits upright with a gap between the valves. The scallop rests
on its deeper valve and lifts its upper fan diagonally over the hinge. Both
shells are sized for gathering by hand. The live forms show the pearls above
the front lip. After gathering, the two valves splay into low, empty bowls.
The giant clam keeps its wavy rim and the scallop keeps its fan and ears.

The live giant clam uses 892, 444, 224 and 96 triangles. Its empty shell uses
784, 384, 192 and 80. The live scallop uses 892, 444, 208 and 96 triangles.
Its empty shell uses 784, 384, 192 and 80. The first two levels have metre UVs.
The flat levels keep the shell and nacre colours and retain pearls on the
live forms.
