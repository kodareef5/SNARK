# Quartz outcrops

The 1.73 m fork takes its tall prisms and blocky host from the quartz and microcline
habit of the West Ossipee pegmatite. Two rising points open above a shorter front
crystal. The two main roots leave broad, jagged breaks after mining. [West Ossipee specimen CK1-1J8](https://www.mindat.org/photo-1228741.html)

The 1.10 m comb takes its staggered growth from the Berns Quartz at the Coleman Mine
in Arkansas. Three broad points run diagonally along the matrix, with shorter growth
on both sides. The mined surface keeps that diagonal as a row of low fractures. [Smithsonian Berns Quartz](https://naturalhistory.si.edu/explore/collections/geogallery/10026927)

Both outcrops use the mineral drop's pale quartz texture at 0.18 m per tile and its
limestone texture at 0.21 m per tile for the host. The two flat levels keep a pale
quartz finish and a warm grey matrix. The authored levels drop smaller growths first,
then simplify the matrix and prism sections.
