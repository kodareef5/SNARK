# Rocks of the ruined bay city

Nine final paintings are in this folder. The accepted sheets establish the individuals, and subsequent paintings use those sheets as image references.

The nine paintings are the **built-in imagegen tool**'s originals, unresized and byte-for-byte. [prompts.md](prompts.md) contains every exact submitted prompt, including corrections. [manifest.json](manifest.json) records input references, decisions, native dimensions, original tool locations and SHA-256 hashes of all 14 images the tool returned; the five superseded passes are not kept. Only the two supplied references and images generated for this brief were used; no outside images were searched for or downloaded. The supplied `BRIEF.md` is unchanged.

**shore.png.** Eye-height beach hero with the L1 wedge, L3 sloping slab, L5 low ledge, L7 buried shoreline rock and L8 fig-gripped rock. Sea grape, palmetto, palms, turquoise shallows and distant ruined buildings place the material in this world. A small survivor supplies scale; the small pink rubble remnant connects this shore to the city. Preserve the smooth sand-line band and roots entering the ground. **If painted again:** give the rear outcrop a slightly larger sunlit opening so its bedding is as legible as the foreground wedge.

**hillside.png.** The broad clearing looks toward scattered limestone and three small dark ore rocks: O1 left, O2 uphill right of centre, O3 farther right and lower down. Pink-faced rubble sits near the right path. The first pass exaggerated rock size; the accepted correction makes the masses much smaller and lower than the nearby person. All three dark shapes remain findable at the reduced size of a contact sheet, where vein detail mostly disappears. **If painted again:** add a companion view with one ore rock partly in vegetation shadow. The roughly 80 m distance is a painting target, not a calibrated camera measurement; check the eventual meshes at 80 and 100 m using the game's actual field of view.

**ruins.png.** R1 sits nearest on the left, R2 farther down the left side and R3 on the right. Broken concrete frames, faded stucco, fig roots and forest growth surround an open route. Heap identities, aggregate-bearing breaks and slender bent steel carry over from the sheet. **If painted again:** vary the distant building collapse heights more; keep the three quarryable heaps unobstructed.

**limestone.png.** Eight individuals on sand under one light. Read left to right: L1–L4 across the top, L5–L8 across the bottom. The wedge, cleft block, long slab and twin shoulder have different silhouettes; the ledges emerge from the ground, while burial and roots supply distinct contact conditions. **If painted again:** add a reverse three-quarter view of L2 and L6 to specify their unseen backs and cross-bedding.

**ore.png.** O1 upper left, O2 upper right, O3 lower left; L1 lower right is the material comparison. The broad dark faces, compact angular breaks and sparse ore exposure distinguish these from the limestone. The correction breaks up the golden stripes and gives O2 a different vein location. **If painted again:** show O2 from its unmineralized side to establish a useful orientation that relies entirely on mass and colour.

**rubble.png.** R1 upper left, R2 upper right, R3 below. Pink slab, mint/yellow wall and hollow blocks, and aqua/yellow column-drum heap provide three different architectural silhouettes. Rebar was reduced from hose-like forms to slender rods. **If painted again:** add a small section through a slab showing concrete cover around the steel; retain the current exterior shapes.

**used_up.png.** Three rows: L1, O1, R1. Whole on the left, depleted on the right, at matching orientation and approximately matching footprint. The limestone retains its low pitted wall beneath a fresh cream break. Ore keeps dark skin beneath a lighter cool grey-green fracture. Rubble retains low pink fragments and right-hand base blocks. **If painted again:** add top-down paired outlines to specify exact footprint registration; do not shrink an entire whole mesh to make its remnant.

**surfaces.png.** Clockwise from upper left: weathered limestone; fresh limestone fracture; broken concrete, pink stucco and rebar; ore with a seam and downward rust stain. Each is intended to cover about one metre. Fossils are embedded inclusions; steel emerges from inside concrete. The correction reduces intact fossil motifs, projecting gold and steel thickness. **If painted again:** show another fresh break with mostly oblique shell fragments, preventing the single coral cross-section from becoming a repeated texture stamp.

**flat.png.** Shore above, hillside below, simplified into untextured polygonal masses. The dark ore shapes remain separate from pale limestone; rubble retains cut grey planes and pink paint. Dark limestone crust stays concentrated on a few upper faces. This is a visual LOD concept, not a render of finished game meshes. **If painted again:** include the ruined street as a third panel to compare all three rubble silhouettes at the same reduced detail.

## What the modeller must not miss

- Build the large geological planes and distinct silhouettes first. Limestone bedding and cross-bedding belong in form; pitting follows it. Avoid uniformly stippled clay or perforated sponge.
- Keep broad, embedded ground contact. Sand meets the scoured band, roots grip and enter the soil, and rubble has packed support beneath its slabs. Use only small local overhangs; most tops must remain usable for climbing and standing.
- Limestone has a thin dark weathered skin above pale stone, varied fingertip-to-fist cavities, tiny sharp crest points and smoother lower faces. Fresh fossil debris is embedded in the fracture, not scattered on top like shells.
- Ore needs quiet dense faces and clean angular breaks. Its dark mass must identify it before the vein does. Veins are irregular, discontinuous and flush; rust runs downward. Use reflected light, never emission or crystal effects.
- Rubble has manufactured evidence: slab thickness, rectangular block cavities, human-cut coral faces, a cylindrical column drum, and paint on a thin stucco layer. Keep rebar around 16–22 mm diameter, with fine ribs and credible embedment; do not reproduce the discarded thick rods.
- Depletion removes upper mass while preserving the base, orientation and distinctive lower details. Fresh ore is a lighter cool grey-green, not cream limestone. Keep both remnant collision and scattered fragments low enough to step over or onto.
- Treat individuals as separate sculpts. Rotating one wedge cannot replace the eight limestone designs or the three ore and rubble designs.
- At reduced detail, preserve pale limestone bodies, limited dark crust, dark ore masses and cut rubble planes with restrained pastel faces. Do not depend on pit normals, rust streaks or metallic glitter for recognition.
