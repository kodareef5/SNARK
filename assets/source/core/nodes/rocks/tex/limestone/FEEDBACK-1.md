# Feedback on round one

Read `BRIEF.md` again, then your `REPORT.md`, then this. The concept's `NOTES.md` now exists in `../../ref/concept/`: read its colour table and "what the modeller must not miss", and look at the upper half of `surfaces.png` there: weathered limestone on the left, a fresh break on the right, each about a metre across. That is what these must look like.

- **The crust is good.** Its colour follows its relief: dark in the pits, the buff showing on the ridges. Keep it.
- **The clean weathered skin's colour says nothing of its relief.** The height has pores, vugs and sharp ridges, and the relit check shows them, but the base colour is a soft blotchy wash with no grain, so on a boulder lit by a sun that casts no shadow into the pits (the game's) it will read as a smooth painted surface. Real porous limestone shows its pores in its colour: the inside of every pore and vug a little darker and greyer with fine grime and sand, the ridges and points paler where they are scoured, a fine salt-and-pepper of grains (ooids, shell grit, a few dark specks) at one to three millimetres, and here and there a little ochre staining. My "faint darkening deep in pores" was too timid: let the pores read clearly, about a quarter darker than the ridges, as the crusted version already does in its own colours. Keep the mean near (196, 178, 140).
- **The fresh break is too smooth and too pale a wash.** A fresh break of oolite is crisp: sub-millimetre ooid grain you can see as texture, shell fragments in section (thin curved lines and arcs from a few millimetres to two centimetres, cream to white), now and then a piece of coral or a small gastropod, small open pores, and a hackly, faceted relief with sharp steps of 2 to 8 mm. Its colour pale cream, but with that grain and those fragments visible, not a soft cloud.
