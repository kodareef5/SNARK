# Figs, mangroves and hammock hardwoods: concept art

You are painting the concept art for the broadleaf trees of a survival game's world. Work only in the current folder. Do not run git commands that write. Do not look up, copy or feed any other game's pictures, or any photograph, to the image tool; this is our own design, painted from words.

## What it is for

The game is a survival game closely modelled on ARK: Survival Evolved for how things behave, with its own art. Its first real map is a ruined, overgrown subtropical city on a bay: a drowned and broken coastal city a few generations after people left, which the plants of a hot coast are taking back. Palms, strangler figs, mangroves and hardwood hammock trees grow through cracked roads, plazas and the shells of buildings.

Trees are what a player harvests for wood and thatch, by walking up to the trunk and striking it with a stone hatchet; a used-up tree leaves a stump until it grows back. They are also most of what fills the view: a player sees one tree at arm's length while chopping it and thousands at once as a hillside 500 m away. So a tree must be handsome at 3 m and must still say what it is as a few dozen pixels on a far slope.

A 3D modeller will build each tree by script, to your pictures, and a material artist will paint the bark and the leaves to the model. Paint what should be built, clearly enough to build it.

Three kinds, each needing several individuals, because a forest of one tree copied looks false:

- **The strangler fig (banyan).** A fig that began high on something built and sent its roots down round it until they fused into a trunk: a lattice and then a wall of grey, smooth, muscular roots, fused and braided, wrapped round what it swallowed. Each individual has swallowed something different of the old city: a concrete lamp post or utility pole, a column of a portico, the corner of a wall with a window in it, a rusted street sign. The swallowed thing shows through gaps in the roots. Above, heavy grey limbs spread wide, a dense dark green crown of leathery oval leaves 14 to 18 m tall and 18 to 24 m across, and from the limbs hang thin aerial roots, curtains of them, some reaching down toward the ground.
- **The mangrove.** A red mangrove at the water's edge, standing on its arching prop roots: a cage of curved, pale grey-brown roots that leave the trunk from 1 to 2 m up and arch down into the mud and the shallows, a short crooked trunk, and a low, rounded, glossy dark green crown 5 to 8 m tall and as wide. Some lean out over the water; some are young and mostly roots; old ones carry a few dangling aerial roots from their branches.
- **The broad hardwood of the hammock.** A live oak: a short, massive trunk with dark, deeply furrowed bark that splits within a few metres into great limbs reaching out more sideways than up, some sweeping low toward the ground and up again; a broad, dense crown of small dark green leaves 12 to 16 m tall and 18 to 24 m across, built of distinct clumps on the ends of the limbs; grey-green Spanish moss hanging in beards from the limbs, ferns along the tops of the big limbs.

Rules that come from the game:
- The trunk is what is struck, and the game treats it as a simple post for collision (a post 1 to 2 m across for the big ones). Nothing wide and solid may stand at the foot where a player would walk through it: buttresses and roots stay close to the trunk, within about a metre of it. The mangrove's root cage is the one exception: it is the tree, and the game will make it solid as a whole, so keep it compact, within about 1.5 m of the trunk.
- Thin things a player may pass through: aerial roots as thin as a finger or a wrist, moss, leaves. Thick hanging roots that reach the ground are posts in their own right; keep them close to the trunk.
- The tree must stand any rotation about its vertical and a range of sizes, so do not rely on one "front".
- Leaves are drawn as cut-out cards in the game, so a crown is made of distinct clusters of leaves with clear silhouettes and clear gaps of sky between them, not a ball of noise.

## What to paint

Painted concept art in the look of a finished, high-end survival game at its best settings: stylized realism, readable chunky shapes, rich materials, believable light. Not photographs, not cartoons. Warm late-afternoon sun, real shadows, sky light in the shade.

1. **`fig_lineup.png`**: three strangler figs side by side, each a different individual and each having swallowed something different, on the cracked ground of an old plaza, a person (1.8 m) for scale, seen from the side far enough off to show each whole tree.
2. **`fig_trunk.png`**: close up at eye height, the foot of one fig where its roots have closed round what it swallowed: how the roots braid and fuse, how they grip the ground and the broken concrete, where the old thing shows through.
3. **`mangrove_lineup.png`**: four mangroves side by side at the edge of the bay's shallows, at low water so the whole root cage shows, a person for scale.
4. **`oak_lineup.png`**: three live oaks side by side in an old park, a person for scale.
5. **`oak_trunk.png`**: close up, the trunk of a live oak where it splits into limbs, with moss and fern.
6. **`stumps.png`**: what is left when each kind is cut down by a player with a stone hatchet: a stump 0.5 to 1 m tall, the cut hacked and splintered with its wood showing (the fig's stump still holding what it swallowed; the mangrove's root cage left standing with the trunk cut above it; the oak's a broad stump with dark bark and pale wood).
7. **`hammock_near.png`**: a player's view at eye height (1.7 m) of a hammock forest with all three kinds and a few palms among ruins near the water: one tree 4 to 8 m away, more behind.
8. **`hammock_far.png`**: the same kind of country seen from about 400 m: a hillside of hammock forest, a mangrove shore, palms standing above, the ruins showing through. This is the picture that matters most: how each kind reads as a silhouette and as a few colours when it is a few dozen pixels tall.
9. **`hammock_far_flat.png`**: the same view drawn as a low-poly game draws it: every tree a few solid flat-coloured shapes (a trunk and limbs of a few faces, a crown of a few lumps of two or three greens), no textures at all, each kind still recognisable and still handsome.
10. **`leaves.png`**: a sheet of the leaves of each kind laid out on a plain ground, as a material artist would want them: a twig of fig leaves, a twig of mangrove leaves, a twig of oak leaves, a beard of Spanish moss, a tuft of aerial roots, each at a size you state in centimetres.

## What excellent looks like

- **Every tree is an individual,** with a life written in its shape: what it grew round, where it leaned for light, the limb it lost.
- **Real structure, not a ball of noise.** Limbs that branch as that kind branches (the fig's heavy horizontal limbs, the oak's great sweeping ones, the mangrove's crooked short ones), and a crown built of distinct clumps on the ends of the limbs, with sky between them and dark hollows inside.
- **Roots that grip.** The fig's roots clamp what they swallowed and flow into the ground; the mangrove stands on its roots in the water; the oak's foot flares into the soil.
- **Colour that is true and varied:** smooth pale grey fig bark, dark furrowed oak bark, grey-brown mangrove roots wet and darker at the water line; deep glossy greens with yellow-green new growth, a few brown leaves, the grey-green of moss.
- **It reads from far away,** as a silhouette and as a few colours: the fig as a broad dark dome on a pale trunk, the mangrove as a low dark band at the water, the oak as a wide dark spreading crown.
- **Its own design.** Someone who knows ARK's tropical island should feel at home, and nothing is copied from any game.

## Deliver

The pictures above as PNG in this folder, each as large as the image tool gives. Then `DESIGN.md`: for each kind, the shape of an individual and the range among individuals (heights, crown widths, trunk diameters, how the limbs split and how many, how many leaf clumps and how big, what each swallowed or stands in), the colours you intend for each part (sRGB), and what makes each read as itself at 3 m and at 500 m, so a modeller can build them from your notes and pictures. Keep everything you generate and do not keep in `source/`, and your exact prompts in `prompts.md`. Look hard at each picture, argue with it, and paint again until it is the best tree art you have seen in any game.
