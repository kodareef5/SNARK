# Mangrove leaves: sun leaves and shade leaves

Read your `REPORT.md` and the briefs beside it first. Your set is on the crowns in the game and the construction holds: look at `feedback/round1_game_20m.jpg` beside the concept `../../ref/concept/broadleaf/mangrove_lineup.png`. What separates them now is light. The concept's crowns read by **sunlit yellow-green tops against deep shade under and inside each billow**; in the game every part of the crown is the same mid olive, so a crown reads as one flat drab colour, and the engine has no cast shadows or bounced light to make the difference.

A tree really does make two kinds of leaf: **sun leaves** on the outside and top of the crown, smaller, thicker, lighter and yellower; and **shade leaves** inside and under it, larger, thinner, darker and bluer. Use that, which is botany and not baked light, to give the crown its value range.

The cards are laid by cell: **cells 0 and 2 lie on the tops of the billows**; **cells 1 and 3 stand round their rims and hang beneath them**; **the mass** is the shell under all of them, seen between and below the cards and through the gaps. So:

- **Cells 0 and 2: sun leaves.** Lighter and warmer: a mean near (104, 126, 62), the brightest leaf tips toward (160, 170, 72). Cell 2 keeps its fresh growth, now the brightest yellow-green in the atlas.
- **Cells 1 and 3: shade leaves.** A mean near (64, 86, 56), cooler, a little bluer, still plainly the same leaves at the same size.
- **The mass: the crown's interior.** A mean near (50, 68, 44), its upper leaves near the shade leaves' tone and its hollows deeper and bluer, down to about (24, 36, 30) in base colour and 0.35 in occlusion; more of its area in pooled hollows than now, so that between and under the cards the eye looks into shade.
