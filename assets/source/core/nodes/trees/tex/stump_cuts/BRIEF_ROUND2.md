# Stump cuts, round 2

Your four stump tops are built and checked well, and the torn ridge of fibres is right. As pictures of hacked wood they fall short of the brief and of `../../ref/concept/broadleaf/stumps.png`; look at them beside it:

- **No growth rings.** The oak, the fig and the mangrove all show a plain, even field of pale wood. Hacked wood shows its rings: fine concentric rings, a little irregular and off-centre, cut through by each facet so that a ring steps where two facets meet, and more visible across the oak's dense sapwood than across the soft fig. Paint them in the base colour (a few percent of value) and in the height (a fraction of a millimetre).
- **The oak's heartwood is a flat cut-out blob.** Heartwood follows the rings: a darker rounded core, its edge irregular but following a ring, with radial checks (fine cracks running out from the middle) through it.
- **The facets do not read.** A stone hatchet leaves broad wedge-shaped facets, each a slightly different plane, each with its own tone (a facet cut across the grain is smoother and paler than one where wood was torn out), the edges between facets clear in the height as steps of a few millimetres to a centimetre. In your height the cut is one shallow dish. Give each disc four to seven facets meeting at the torn ridge.
- **The bark rim is a clean circle.** Chip it: blows that landed near the edge broke the bark off in places, leaving bare wood in notches, and lifted it in others.
- The banyan's disc should show the seams where its fused roots grew together: a few curving lines across the wood where the grain changes direction, darker in the seam.

Keep the layout, the disc sizes, the colours of the woods and of the bark round each disc, and everything that passed. Replace the four layers, keep the new sources and scripts, and add to `REPORT.md` what changed.
