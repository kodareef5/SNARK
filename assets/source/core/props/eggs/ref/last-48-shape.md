# Last 48 egg shapes

The domestic chicken egg supplies the everyday ovoid. The peregrine falcon egg
shows the difference between a full blunt end and a narrower, rounded tip.
The shared game profile carries its broadest cross-section just below its
midpoint, with a fuller curve under it and a taper above. The blunt pole sits
at ground level. [Australian Museum egg specimens](https://australian.museum/blog/amri-news/oological-odyssey-the-wonders-of-bird-eggs/)

The five shells stand 0.11, 0.16, 0.23, 0.33 and 0.48 metres tall. Each is 1.36
times as tall as it is wide. All species use those five sizes, and the plain
and fertile variants share geometry within a size. The narrow band runs around
the upper shoulder, from about 71 to 75 percent of the height. Its faces
follow the curve of the shell. The instance palette makes the band blend into
plain eggs or show up chalk-coloured on fertile eggs.

Each size has 1,280, 624, 288 and 144 triangles. The first two levels have
metre UVs at a 0.22-metre repeat. The two flat levels keep the shoulder band
and use scattered groups of faces for blotches, speckles, streaks and dashes.
