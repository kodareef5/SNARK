# Shared shell for Last 48

- shell-1. One 256 × 256 tile repeats every 0.22 m across the shell. Fine pores and faint
  directional grain give the nonmetal shell its satin finish. The neutral grey carries light
  and dark variation, and the dye supplies the shell colour.
- shell-2. The first RGB mask carries the ground, irregular blotches, and fine freckles.
  The second carries wispy streaks, short dashes, and chalk. The blotches take their loose
  edges from Japanese quail, the freckles their scattered spacing from killdeer, the streaks
  their curves from common guillemots, and the dashes their short, scratch-like shape from
  ruddy turnstones. Each egg's palette can bring a different set of marks forward. The
  generator draws every mark. [Japanese quail study](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpsa1964/14/5/14_5_229/_article/-char/en),
  [NPS killdeer account](https://www.nps.gov/miss/learn/nature/birdskill.htm),
  [Common guillemot field account](https://d8.britishbirds.co.uk/journal/article/red-common-guillemot-egg-skomer),
  [Ruddy turnstone account](https://www.cyberlibrary.org/en/books/life-histories-of-north-american-shore-birds-part-2-of-2-77498/chapters/38/)
- shell-3. The upper band moves the ground's weight to the chalk channel. Plain eggs give
  chalk the ground colour. Fertile eggs give it a light chalk colour, with the marks
  continuing across the band edge.

Run `python3 -I paint.py` from the egg source directory to regenerate the eight PNGs.
