## Setting out

**Axes.** Face the building from the street. Local X runs right along the frontage, Y runs outward toward the street, and Z runs up. For an end wall, rotate X and Y together to follow that wall. Each piece has its own origin O. A table interval gives the two face coordinates in metres from O. The PNG cut lists give the bounding sizes in X × Y × Z order. The small umber cross on a measured view locates O. Plate 05 enlarges the downpipe plan sixfold. Its scale bar applies to the plan. The front is viewed from +Y, the side from −X, and the plan from above with the street at the bottom.

**Solids.** Fill each interval as a box unless the row specifies a profile. For a profile along X or Y, extrude the specified end section through the full remaining axis. A tapered cut with a flat base is a box and a triangular wedge, or a single wedge if the thin end closes to zero. A pitched strip with two sloping faces can be built from two triangular prisms sharing a diagonal. Fuse their internal faces. A wedge may terminate in an edge. The smallest independent strip or plate is 0.03 m. Levels evaluated on a continuous slope follow its plane through the grid points.

**Finish.** V is the unlit palette value, from 0 black to 100 white. Give each part its single base tint and named world-tiled finish. The swatches on the plates use these tints. Sun and sky supply the light face, dark return and contact shadow. The shutter rolls and the runoff stain both belong to their material. Mask or cut the host face wherever an insert replaces it.

**Distribution.** Place the required entry and drainage pieces at actual openings and low points. On an otherwise ordinary four-bay frontage, concentrate optional trim in one bay and leave three quiet. Canopy and surround may share a lobby socket. A shutter may share a bay with its separate door. A cabinet needs its own clear pocket. Add damage only where the host has the corresponding failure.
