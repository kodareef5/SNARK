# A second opinion: the walk to the water, before and after the first two steps

You drew the measured elevations of three ruined building kinds (flats, shops with rooms above, a parking structure) and
the slab-edge sheet for a browser survival game set in a real coastal city rebuilt as ruins, and painted over two of
its pictures as the street should look. Those sheets and paint-overs are in `ref/`. A builder has now made the first two
steps toward them, and the pictures in this folder are the game's own, from the same fixed cameras on a real GPU at
14:00, before (`before-*.png`) and after (`after-*.png`), on the high setting.

The two steps:

1. Each building's outer wall bays now wear modules of its kind, the same in every bay of a storey: flats get paired
   balconies over a coral recessed wall with dark openings, a darker solid ground floor and a roofed top; shops a pale
   sign band over a dark display and door, a shutter in one bay of four, two windows a bay above and a parapet;
   garages open decks behind darker spandrels, a clearance bar at the way in, a ramp behind every third front bay and
   poles on the top deck; offices spandrel bands and panes. Long blocks of flats have blank ends.
2. Every slab and overhang has a darker soffit under a lighter edge. Where a bay fell, the bays beside it end their
   slabs at the break (whole with a lip, spalled, or snapped with bars) with no wall, and one column of each failure
   stands with its broken head over the slab it held, fallen and broken along the same teeth.

Not yet done (later steps, do not judge them as missing): a curb, sidewalk and gutter; small kit pieces (door surround,
canopy, rails, drains); windows drawn in the wall's material; the far city's rhythm. The sky occlusion in the after set
is another builder's work that landed at the same time.
