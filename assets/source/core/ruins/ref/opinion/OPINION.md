# The walk to the water

## Across the road

**Shop.** The dark display under the old pink band on the right now reads as a shop, and the paired openings behind the left roof pick out the flats. The shutters blend into the displays, the upper rooms are hard to find, and no garage is identifiable across the alley.

**Apartments.** Paired dark openings in coral recesses above a solid base identify the flats where the lower block used to show through. Higher up, the blue-grey balcony fronts hide almost all the coral beneath the blank end wall, and neither shops nor parking decks are identifiable in this view.

**Garage.** Roof poles and a yellow clearance bar over the ramp identify the garage on the left, and the coral balcony bays pick out the flats behind the viaduct on the right. The heavier deck fronts hide much of the ramp beyond the entrance, and the pale band at far right is the only hint of shops.

**Third Avenue.** The paired coral recesses now identify the flats down the right side, while on the left several pale shop bands show through the same opening and it is hard to place the nearest display. The tilted slab beside the tree is easy to mistake for a ramp, though little else there suggests a garage.

**Corner.** The sign bands on the left and the balcony pairs on the tower ahead make the shops and flats easy to place from the junction. The near right corner reads as offices because blue-grey panes wrap both faces where the paint-over shows open garage decks.

**Water.** Rows of coral openings distinguish the flats just right of centre, the poles on the low deck behind the foreground palms suggest parking, and the trees hide the shopfronts. With the tower faces filled in, the slit of sky beneath the tall blank end at far right makes the upper block look suspended.

**Air.** Beside the river on the left, the coral rows identify flats behind a long open-sided block that suggests a garage, while individual shops are too small to pick out. The newly windowed pale block nearest the river looks perched on a stack of bare floors.

## Following a break

Third Avenue comes closest. A rust-coloured bar shows at the high end of the tilted slab just right of the tree. The low end disappears into the sill, leaving a thin triangle across its face. The next bay to the right keeps both posts and a level edge for its full width. The damage looks local, but the teeth on the stub and the fallen head cannot be matched from here.

In the shop paint-over a torn upper room leads down to a fallen piece beside the alley. In the game the roof ends square above the post, with a slit of sky under the red-and-grey parapet just to its left. The lighter deck edge over the dark soffit is visible on the garage, but whole, spalled and snapped ends are still hard to tell apart from the road.

## Five changes, most visible first

1. **Give the shop row its upper rooms.** Above each surviving 8 m shop bay, fill one 4 m storey with pale limestone and two dark openings 4 m apart. Seat the parapet on that wall. Carry one faded mint band 0.9 m high over the 5 m charcoal display and recessed door, with a lighter blue-grey shutter in every fourth bay.
2. **Open the garage face at the corner.** Use the parking module on the near right return shown in the corner paint-over. In each 4 m storey, put a 1 m weathered-grey spandrel below a 2.6 m clear opening and a 0.4 m pale deck edge. Show the pale ramp climbing 2 m in 16 m behind the posts. Use the same section on the garage beside the viaduct so its ramp stays visible.
3. **Lighten and lower the balcony fronts.** Give each 4 m half-bay a 1.05 m high limestone front. Use the apartment sheet’s values of 83 for limestone, 58 for the faded coral wall and 29 for the openings, on a scale from black at 0 to white at 100. Keep the wall 0.8 m behind the column line so the paired openings show above the fronts.
4. **Expose the two halves of the Third Avenue break.** Clear the sill across the failed bay and rest the low end of the 0.4 m slab on the ground beside the 0.8 m wide column stub. Keep the broken head on the slab with the same three teeth visible on both cuts. Use pale exposed concrete against the dark grey underside. End the wall at the last whole post.
5. **Give each exposed slab end a distinct outline.** On the 0.4 m slab, carry a pale concrete lip 0.2 m past a charcoal soffit with a 0.15 m undercut. Keep the whole end square. Take a shallow, darker aggregate pocket out of a spalled lip. At a snapped end, cut through the full depth and carry three bent rust-brown bars past the teeth. Keep the adjoining bay at its full width.
