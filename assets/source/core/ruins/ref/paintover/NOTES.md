# Street kit build notes

The building dimensions follow the four elevation sheets. Values below are the sheets’ painted lightness on a 0–100 scale, before sun and sky lighting. The street dimensions are proposed kit sizes. Both views use the same street section and the same 0.40 m concrete slab.

## Corner looking east

1. **Set the edges of the street.** Run a 2.4 m warm-grey concrete walk from each near corner along the existing entrance line. Give its street edge a 0.15 m upstand and a 0.20 m wide pale curb top. Turn the short curb pieces around the corner behind the wrecks. The lit lip should be visible again on the far side of each car.
2. **Open the parking decks on the right.** Between the existing columns, repeat an 8 m bay with a 0.40 m light concrete slab and a 1 m grey guard, leaving 2.60 m of open height. Use value 48 for the guard and about 36 for the concrete seen in shade. Carry the same low guard around the roof.
3. **Fill the left shop row.** Snap a two-storey module to the standing piers. Each 8 m bay gets a 5 m dark display and recessed door below a blank, 0.90 m faded-mint fascia. Put two room windows in the limestone wall above, 4 m apart. Step the parapet over the near bays so the roofline still reads from the corner.
4. **Break the garage bay just left of the white wreck.** Take a roughly 4 m piece out of the front of the lowest raised deck. Terminate the 0.40 m slab with a toothed face and short bent rust-coloured bars. Leave a jagged 0.80 m square column stump on its axis. Tip the matching slab directly below, with its light nose facing the street and its broken head on the ground inside the walk.
5. **Give the tower left of the street centre paired balconies.** Divide each existing 8 m bay into two 4 m cells. Project the pale balcony fronts 2 m from the column line, raise the guards 1.05 m and set the faded-coral wall 0.80 m behind the line. Dark window and door panels sit in that recess. Stack the cells up to the existing roof boxes.
6. **Show the garage ramp through the openings.** Put a light-grey inclined slab one column line inside the right façade. Let it rise 2 m over 16 m, with the upper half visible above the darker guard. Repeat the flight on the deck above so the diagonal is visible between successive horizontal lips.
7. **Mark the pedestrian entrance in the garage.** Enclose a 4 m half-bay by the tall palm in chalk-coloured concrete. Cut a small dark landing window at each 4 m level and set a roughly 1 m wide blue-grey door at pavement height. Lift the core cap about 2 m above the adjacent guard.
8. **Drop the curb at the garage entrance.** Use a 4 m wide apron with a 1 m tapered curb return at each side. Bring the roadward edge down the full 0.15 m and keep a level strip along the back for walkers. Give the apron the same warm concrete as the walk so the dark vehicle opening identifies its use.
9. **Seat the asphalt in a gutter.** Run a shallow 0.45 m wide channel against the curb toe, including through the apron. Make the bed a darker grey than the road and fall about 0.03 m toward the curb. Break the strip into 2 m lengths aligned with the sidewalk joints.
10. **Place deposits at their source.** Taper buff sand into the low gutter near the driveway returns in patches roughly 0.3–1.2 m long. Root low olive clumps in selected walk joints and at the garage base. A 0.10–0.25 m tuft is enough to mark a seam at this distance.

**Three-change cut.** Keep 1, 2 and 4. Together they give the walk a continuous edge and make the parking structure and its lost bay legible from the intersection. Cut 10 first, then 9, 7, 6, 8, 5 and 3.

## Across the street at the shops

1. **Build the two-storey shop bay.** End each frontage at the standing columns beside the passage. Repeat 8 m limestone bays, value 83, with a broad dark display and door opening at pavement height, rooms at +4 m and the roof at +8 m. Give each upper bay two punched windows, roughly 2 m wide on 4 m centres, with projecting concrete sills.
2. **Fit the storefront at walking height.** In each ground-floor bay, set a 5 m wide, 2.70 m high dark display about 0.20 m behind the piers. Put one mullion in it. Beside it, fold a roughly 1.95 m wide entrance 1.20 m back from the front line. Use value 29 for the glazing and a terracotta floor across the recess so the individual doors can be found from the street.
3. **Open the last room on the left block.** At the passage, remove the outer 4 m of upper wall and roof. Use the snapped 0.40 m edge with a few projecting rust bars and put the broken 0.80 m square column on the outer grid line. Tip the missing light concrete piece onto its darker broken head at the foot of that same bay. Its long edge points back toward the break and leaves the through-walk clear.
4. **Join the thresholds with a continuous walk.** Lay 2.4 m wide warm-grey concrete across both shop fronts, with transverse joints about every 2 m. Finish it with the 0.15 m raised curb and a 0.45 m darker gutter. The curb top catches the sun and its front face gives the asphalt a distinct lower level.
5. **Tie the shops together at the first floor.** Run the blank, 0.90 m high mint fascia under the slab nose, value 72. Use one length per 8 m bay with the joints over the piers. Carry it to the damaged end above the last surviving display. The dark shelf underneath should read across several storefronts.
6. **Cut the driveway at the central passage.** Centre a roughly 6 m vehicle crossing on the existing opening. Taper the two curb ends down over 1 m each. Use a shallow warm-grey apron up to the level path at the back and carry the gutter through at road height. The opening must still give a sightline into the block.
7. **Swap a display for a shutter.** Use a closed 5 m blue-grey panel, value 47, in the third visible left-hand bay and repeat it in the right run. Keep the adjacent recessed door. Broad horizontal ribs and a recessed top rail distinguish the shutter at street distance.
8. **Finish the roof with the shared slab edge.** Fit a low 0.80 m limestone parapet and one raised mint panel about a bay wide on each run. Give the 0.40 m cast edge its 0.20 m nose and darker soffit. A 0.04 m bevel on the top corner picks up the same narrow light as the projecting sills.
9. **Separate the two structures above the shop roofs.** On the left, pair 4 m pale balcony fronts against recessed coral wall panels. On the right, put 1 m mid-grey guards in front of open parking bays and show the light ramp rising behind them. Use the existing deck elevations and rooftop silhouettes for both.
10. **Catch sand at the ends of the apron.** Paint short buff wedges roughly 0.3–1 m long against the two lowered curb returns. Set sparse olive tufts, about 0.15 m high, in a few transverse sidewalk joints. Keep the centre of the crossing clear enough to read its slope.

**Three-change cut.** Keep 1, 3 and 4. The room rhythm, the missing end and the pedestrian edge carry across the full view. Cut 10 first, then 9, 8, 7, 6, 5 and 2.
