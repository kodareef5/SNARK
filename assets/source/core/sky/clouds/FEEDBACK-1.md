This is round two of the cloud layers in this folder. Read `BRIEF.md` and `REPORT.md` first; the brief and its bar are unchanged.

I have looked at your lit sheets, `tiled.jpg` and the originals. Round one is a real step: overhead the low layer reads as tropical cumulus with heaped lobes and torn edges, the cirrus lies along one wind, the wrap is invisible, there is no light in the data and the method (four native patches on a torus, no enlargement) is sound. Keep all of that. It is not yet at the bar, for four reasons.

1. **The low layer has too many small scraps.** Overhead they make the sky busy; from 5° to 20° above the horizon, where the layer is seen nearly edge on, every scrap becomes a thin horizontal lens and together they make a field of fish scales across the whole lower sky (`lit/noon.jpg`, both horizon views). Trade cumulus are distinct heaps with broad lanes of clear sky between them, a few shreds trailing off a heap's edge, and little else. Cut the loose scraps hard, keep the heaps compact and lumpy, and let the clear lanes be wide; aim for fewer, more distinct clouds at several sizes rather than more of them.
2. **The tile shows a lattice.** In the raw 3 by 3 the big complexes sit at nearly the same spot in each of the four patches, so the sky holds a grid of large heaps every 4 km. Break it: the large heaps and the empty lanes should fall unevenly within the tile, so that no spacing repeats inside 8 km.
3. **The high layer is too faint to do its job.** Mean cover 0.026 and nothing over 0.39 means the cirrus is a few hairlines. At sunset and dusk the high cloud is what catches the last colour long after the low clouds are grey, so it needs some presence: a few long bands and veils of denser fibres (cover up to about 0.7 in their cores) among the thin wisps and the open sky, still mostly empty.
4. **Judge the horizon.** I have changed `light.py`, so run it again before judging: the layers now fade into haze by the haze formula the engine will use (density falling with height, integrated along the ray), and a cloud seen low over the horizon shows its sunlit side rather than its grey base. The horizon views are where the layer must hold up now.

Keep the seams, the format, the no-light rule, the measures and everything else that passes. Put round one's pair and sheets in `rounds/` beside the earlier ones, and add to `REPORT.md` what changed and why.
