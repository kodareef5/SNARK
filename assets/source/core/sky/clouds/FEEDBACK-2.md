This is round three of the cloud layers in this folder. Read `BRIEF.md`, `FEEDBACK-1.md` and `REPORT.md` first; the brief and its bar are unchanged.

Round two fixed what I asked and went too far. The single heaps are now the best yet: overhead (`lit/noon.jpg`, straight up) they are compact, lumpy, soft-edged tropical cumulus, and the horizon is calmer. The cirrus has presence and takes the sunset's colour. Keep those heaps, the seams, the format and the no-light rule. Three things are short.

1. **The low layer is too sparse and too even.** At 0.116 mean cover the sky holds a few isolated heaps, and in `tiled.jpg` they are polka dots of much the same size at much the same spacing. The tropical sky players remember from ARK has more cloud than this, and it is organised: groups of heaps close together, a broken row along the wind here, a big heap with small ones round it there, and wide stretches of clear blue between the groups. Bring the mean cover back to about 0.22 to 0.28 by adding heaps, not scraps: a few large heaps (1 to 1.5 km, 250 to 400 pixels), many medium ones (400 to 800 m) and some small (150 to 300 m), clustered unevenly at the scale of 2 to 4 km, so that some quarters of the tile are busy and others nearly clear.
2. **One heap is a landmark.** The large boomerang-shaped complex is the most distinctive thing in the tile, so it is what the eye finds again every 8 km. No heap may stand out from its neighbours like that: break it up or replace it with heaps of ordinary shape.
3. **The cirrus is wallpaper.** In `tiled.jpg` the high layer is about six similar diagonal strokes at even spacing over a faint sprinkle of small hooks that covers the whole tile, and in the lit views the sprinkle reads as brush texture laid over the sky. Cirrus comes in a few long, irregular bands and isolated patches with the sky between them truly empty: two or three bands of different widths and lengths that wander and fray, a few patches of fine fibres or ripples, and nothing at all elsewhere. Inside a band the fibres are long and continuous and curve with the wind; hooks are at their ends, not everywhere.

Run `light.py` again and judge the views, overhead and at the horizon. Put round two's pair in `rounds/`, and add to `REPORT.md` what changed and why.
