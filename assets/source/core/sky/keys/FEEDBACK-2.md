This is round four of the mood keys in this folder. Read `BRIEF.md`, `BRIEF-2.md`, `FEEDBACK-1.md` and `REPORT.md` first.

Round three made the golden hour, sunset, dusk and night keys whose skies are at the bar, and dawn's nearly so: the sea now takes the sky, with a glitter path under the low sun; the afterglow, the rose return and the moonrise are lovely; the night is a readable blue. Keep their skies exactly as they are. This round changes the sun and moon in every key, dawn's sea, and the three day keys, partly because I steered you wrong.

1. **The sun and the moon are specks.** At their real sizes they are a few pixels in a player's view, and ARK's moon is a feature of its night: large, with a face, lighting the sea. Make the sun's disc 2° across with a soft limb and its glow round it, and the moon's 4° across (about 11 and 23 pixels tall in the panorama, wider near its top as the projection draws them). The moon's face is `../moon/moon.png` (our own painting; the moon team may still improve it, so have your build read that file and place it, so the keys can be built again with the final face): a full face, north up, lit by the moon's light colour, with a soft glow round it. The sea takes the moon's light: a silver path under it where it stands low (sunset and dusk, in the east), a broad silver sheen toward it at night.
2. **I asked for a deep ceiling at noon, and that was a mistake.** At noon the sun is 66.7° up, so the zenith is only 23° from it, inside its glow. In real light the deepest blue lies about 90° from the sun, which at noon is the sky 20° to 40° up on the side away from it: the `high` point. So the noon dome runs: deepest, most saturated blue at `high` and round that side of the sky; a little lighter and softer toward the zenith as it nears the sun; bright round the sun; pale at the horizon. Round three has it the other way round (zenith (29, 86, 161) under a `high` of (110, 194, 254)).
3. **The day blue is electric.** (110, 194, 254) is a synthetic cyan, and all three day keys have exactly that value at `high`, so it reads as one colour stamped three times. Paint a deeper cerulean to cobalt, the tropical blue you would want to look up into: about (60, 130, 215) at `high` at noon, a little lighter and cooler in the morning (about (75, 145, 225)), a little warmer and softer in the afternoon (about (85, 145, 210)), the zenith of each a touch lighter than its `high`. Noon must sit beside the golden hour as the same game on the same island.
4. **Morning and afternoon have a cream band round the whole horizon,** away from the sun as strongly as toward it, as bright as the sun's own glow. Keep haze low all round, but away from the sun it takes the sky's colour, a pale clear aqua-blue; the warm cream belongs only on the sun's side.
5. **Dawn's sea away from the sun is grey fog on a wall.** Give it the gradient the other keys have: it takes the sky above that side, lilac and pale blue, deeper and darker toward the shore.
6. **The measure is stricter.** Its grade could stretch to numbers no real grade would use (a tint of a seventh, the horizon lifted seven times), which is how round three's noon fitted. It now keeps the grade gentle: each tint within a factor of 4, saturation within 2, the horizon lifted at most 2.5 times or lowered to a third, and the glow round the light at most halved. Round three's noon now scores 11.2. Run it again; every key must be 6 or under, and keep the sun's and moon's glow off the four sky points.

Keep everything else that passes. Put round three's keys in `rounds/` and add to `REPORT.md` what changed and why, with the angular sizes of the sun and the moon, in plain sentences without dashes as punctuation.
