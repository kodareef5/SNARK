This is round two of the moon in this folder. Read `BRIEF.md` and `REPORT.md` first; the brief and its bar are unchanged.

I have looked at `check.png` at 100% and at the 512 disc. The format is right (the disc to the edges, a clean rim, opaque inside, no glow, the mean near grey) and the seas are roughly where they belong. As a moon it is not there yet, for four reasons. Paint it again, rebuild, recheck, and add to REPORT.md what changed and why.

1. **The face does not read at 32 and 48 pixels.** The seas are a mid grey only a little darker than the highlands, so the small discs are a pale blur with faint smudges. On the full Moon the seas are about half as bright as the highlands and are what everyone sees first: the dark, smooth, connected shapes of the face. Make the seas clearly darker and calmer, the highlands a clean bright ground, so that at 32 pixels a player sees the face at once.
2. **The seas' edges are torn lace.** Imbrium, Procellarum, Serenitatis and the rest have jagged, fragmented, paper-cut outlines with islands everywhere. Seas are lava plains: broad soft-edged pools that run into each other, smooth inside, with a few gentle wrinkles and the odd bright splash, and rounder where they fill a basin (Crisium, Serenitatis, Imbrium's curve).
3. **The rays are starbursts.** Tycho and Copernicus are drawn as ringed craters with straight spokes, like a sun icon. Under a full sun Tycho is a small bright spot in a slightly darker collar, with long, faint, uneven streaks running far across the south and into the seas; Copernicus a bright splash with a softer, shorter spray. Paint them as splashes of light, not lines.
4. **Craters are drawn as rings all over.** A ring is a crater's shadowed wall, and a full face has no shadows: at full moon craters show as bright spots and pale halos, or not at all. Keep only what light shows: a few bright young craters, the pale highlands' fine mottling, the seas' darker smooth floors.

Keep it a painting: a moon you would paint, a little warmer ivory in the highlands and a little cooler grey in the seas if it helps the read, never a photograph. Keep the mean near grey, the rim bright to the edge, no halo, and everything that passes now.
