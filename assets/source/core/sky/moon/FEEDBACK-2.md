This is round three of the moon in this folder. Read `BRIEF.md`, `FEEDBACK-1.md` and `REPORT.md` first; the brief and its bar are unchanged.

Round two fixed the read: at 32, 48 and 64 pixels the dark seas now give the face at a glance, the rings and spokes are gone and the highlands are a calm bright ground. Keep that. At 512 it is a cut-out, not yet a painting that rewards a look, for three reasons.

1. **The seas are flat grey fills with scalloped edges.** Every sea is one even grey with a lobed outline like a cartoon cloud. Real seas differ from each other and inside themselves: Serenitatis paler in the middle with a darker rim, Tranquillitatis a little deeper and cooler, Imbrium dark with lighter streaks crossing it, Procellarum a broad mottled plain that fades into the highlands at its west edge rather than stopping at a line. Some edges are crisp where a basin's rim holds them, most are soft and ragged. Give each sea its own tone and some gentle variation inside, and lose the scallops.
2. **Tycho has lost its rays.** It is the one feature of the full moon's south everyone sees: a small bright crater whose long, faint, uneven streaks run across the southern highlands and out over Nubium and beyond. Bring the rays back as soft streaks of light, fainter than the crater, a few of them long. Copernicus is a sharp sparkle now; make it a bright splash with a soft spray round it, like Kepler and Aristarchus smaller still if you want them.
3. **Keep the read.** None of this may cost the small sizes: the seas stay clearly darker than the highlands, and check at 32 and 48 pixels after every change.

Keep the format, the mean near grey, the rim bright to the edge, no halo. Add to `REPORT.md` what changed and why.
