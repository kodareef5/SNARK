# Camp bedding references

The hide sleeping bag follows George Murray Levick’s Antarctic sleeping bag, circa 1910.
The museum photograph shows a single low pouch, a rounded foot with a hand-sewn edge and a flap
turned back from the opening. The game bag tapers toward the foot and uses a pale lining and
an uneven rolled lip to mark the opening. [Royal Museums Greenwich, AAA4213](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-3968)

The simple bed follows the Genesee Country Village & Museum’s rope bed, circa 1800–1820.
The photograph shows four posts, square rails, a cut-corner headboard and cloth hanging over
the rails. The game bed uses shorter posts and a rough plank deck, with a folded hide blanket
and linen pillow. [Genesee Country Village & Museum, 71.408a-i](https://www.gcv.org/online-collections/entry/bed-18)
