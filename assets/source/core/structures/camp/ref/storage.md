# Camp storage

The collection records guided the construction and silhouettes. The game uses original meshes and shared camp materials.

- The small box borrows its low lid, broad board faces and nailed construction from a seventeenth-century New England box. A stepped lid and one central clasp keep its outline readable. [Box, 1675–1700, Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/912)
- The small box takes the contrast of dark iron against oak from a compact oak chest. The game's iron follows the lid seam and fastens the boards. [Iron bound wooden chest, sixteenth or seventeenth century, Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co155464/iron-bound-wooden-chest-chests)
- The large box uses the stiles, raised panels and feet of a Virginia blanket chest. The game simplifies the framing to two panels across the front and carries the stiles down into four feet. [Blanket Chest, 1795–1805, Colonial Williamsburg](https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/objects/34876/blanket-chest)
- An Alamance County chest shows how two boards and paired iron hinges form a lift top. The game gives each panel a separate pull and leaves a wider seam between them so they read at inventory size. [Blanket chest, 1765, attributed to George Tate, Colonial Williamsburg](https://emuseum.colonialwilliamsburg.org/objects/9709/blanket-chest)
