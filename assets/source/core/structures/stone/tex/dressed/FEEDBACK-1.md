I have looked at your dressed stone tiled, cut into blocks and up close. The layers agree and the seams are clean, but it does not yet read as stone. Two things, then rebuild, recheck and update REPORT.md (keep a short note of what changed and why).

1. The base colour reads as plaster or paint. A standard deviation of 3 levels is a painted wall. Limestone has variation at every scale: soft drift of a few levels between warmer and cooler areas over 0.3 to 1 m, faint bedding bands, fine mineral speckle you can see at arm's length, shell fragments and darker inclusions that are actually visible, a few faint iron or rust stains, and the grooves of the tooling holding a little grey dirt. Aim for a standard deviation of about 8 to 12 levels, keep the mean at (196, 195, 190), and keep nothing so strong that it marks the repeat. Use the real stone in your generated source much more; at the moment almost none of it survives.

2. The tooling reads as a procedural hatch: short dashes of one length laid on parallel lines, the same everywhere, like a pen pattern. Real batted tooling is a broad chisel driven across the face in strokes 40 to 80 mm long: each stroke leaves several continuous parallel grooves 2 to 4 mm apart, strokes overlap and wander a little, the rows are not evenly spaced, and the grooves fade in and out where the stone is harder. Punched work is a field of irregular pock marks. Make the relief real: tooling 0.8 to 2 mm deep, pits 2 to 3 mm, so that the height uses most of its 6 mm range. Lit from the side it should look cut by hand, not printed.
