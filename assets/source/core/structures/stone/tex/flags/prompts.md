# Round three source generation

The four atlases are new sources. Each of their four quadrants is used for at most one flag; the unused sixteenth quadrant is retained. All original image-tool outputs are kept unchanged in `source/`. Previous prompts and sources are in `round2/`.

## P01 — `source/limestone_atlas_01.png`

Create a material-source atlas, square image, exactly 2 by 2 equal square quadrants, four DIFFERENT independent large samples of ancient walked limestone flagstone FACE texture. Each quadrant fills its entire square: no surrounding background, no labels, no frames, no joints, no stone outlines, no bevels. All four are natural warm-grey limestone with restrained pale buff sediment in the matrix, the stone of a medieval stronghold floor worn by centuries of feet. Broad smooth worn plateaus with irregular subtle bedding, small dissolved fossil fragments and shell cross-sections, intermittent fine calcite strands, shallow worn pitting and softly mottled mineral colour. A few tiny ochre-grey inclusions. Faces look weighty and old, NOT gravel, concrete, granite, slate, marble, tiles, cobbles or modern garden paving. Texture changes inside the stone on the scale of 3 to 20 cm. Overall warm grey, not golden or brown, about sRGB 132 128 120. Dry, clean enough to walk on, no moss or puddles. Orthographic perfectly top-down colour/albedo scan; absolutely diffuse neutral illumination, NO directional light, NO cast shadows, NO gloss highlights, NO dark vignette, NO raised or shaded edge. No recognizable big dark blotch or hero fossil. The four quadrants must be individually invented, not copies, not rotations of one another. Every sample represents about 0.9 m square of a SINGLE stone, with broad quiet places between geological features. Very high quality realistic game texture, painterly clarity of large forms but photographic fine stone material. Deliver only the four-quadrant texture atlas. Quartet A: predominantly quiet grey-buff worn micritic limestone, faint irregular fossil traces, gently clouded buff strata, sparse eroded shallow pinholes.
