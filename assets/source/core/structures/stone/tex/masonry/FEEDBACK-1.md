I have looked at your wall tiled 3 by 3, relit and up close. The registration, the tiling, the mean and the layer agreement are right. As a wall it is close, but it still reads printed, for four reasons. Fix them, rebuild, recheck, and update REPORT.md with a short note of what changed and why.

1. The fine texture on the stone faces reads as felt or fur: long hairy streaks, the same on every stone, very visible in relit.jpg. It must read as stone: granular and crystalline, with cleavage facets, pitting, the odd vein, and on some stones the marks of a pitching tool or punch. Different stones should have different surfaces: some fine and close, some coarse and pitted, a few with a broken spalled corner.

2. The faces are too flat: they read as slabs. Rough-cut stone is rock-faced: the face bulges 5 to 12 mm toward its middle and falls away to a broken arris, with facets where it was pitched off. Use more of the 32 mm range for this. The geometry already gives each stone a vee at its joints; your relief makes it a stone.

3. The colour is a two-colour patchwork: clearly blue stones and clearly warm stones alternating, like a checkerboard from across a valley. Make it one limestone family on a continuum: mostly warm grey and pale buff, cooler stones fewer and only a hint of blue, and variation within each stone too (a darker bed, faint iron staining, a weathered crust that is paler on the upper face and darker toward the lower edge).

4. The mortar is a clean light band of nearly one width everywhere, which is what makes it look tiled. Lime mortar in an old wall is recessed, irregular, packed with grit, darkened with grime in the recess, here and there eroded back so the stone's broken arris overhangs it, here and there fuller. From a distance the joints should read slightly darker than the stones, not lighter.

Keep the registration to the layout, the mean colour, the tiling and everything that already passes.
