I have looked at your oak tiled, as beams, and on the lintels and door of the stone house. It reads as wood and the beams work, but from a few metres it reads as brown plastic with a grain print, not old hewn oak. Three things, then rebuild, recheck, and update REPORT.md with a short note of what changed and why.

1. The drying checks are nearly invisible. Old oak beams are split: several long checks per beam face, 2 to 6 mm wide, 0.2 to 1 m long, deep enough to hold real shadow, opening and narrowing along their length, now and then branching. They should be the darkest thing in the picture, and the deepest in the height.

2. The colour is too even: one milk-chocolate brown everywhere. Weathered oak has the surface fibres bleached toward a warm silver-grey, especially on the raised grain and the adze ridges, with darker brown left in the pores, the checks and the hollows, and long tonal streaks along the grain that change over 0.2 to 1 m. Aim for about twice the tonal variation you have now and keep the mean at (118, 92, 66).

3. Let the adze work read: shallow scalloped facets across the grain every 8 to 15 cm, each with a slightly sharper edge where the blade stopped, irregular in spacing and depth, lit from the side.

Keep the tiling, the horizontal grain, the strip test and everything that passes.
