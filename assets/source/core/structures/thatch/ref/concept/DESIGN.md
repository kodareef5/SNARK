# Thatch tier — direction A, Long Skirts
Round 2 modelling sheet

Build the compact square house in [house_hero.png](house_hero.png), using the isolated pieces for construction. The identity is **two long, full reed skirts**, their irregular pale feet separated by one deep broken overlap shadow, on a sparse lashed pole frame. The roof is a flat compressed reed mattress with a soft perimeter fringe. The foundation is an earth-retaining crib. The door is a separate light reed-filled frame.

These are perspective concept paintings, not measured orthographic drawings. The dimensions below are the construction targets used for the set; use the numerical datums for the blockout and the pictures for shape, assembly and finish. The isolated pieces resolve small construction details that are less legible in the house views. In particular, use the corrected foundation's flush stake heads and the detail sheet's connected hinge lashings.

Keep exact mating planes beneath organic surfaces. In a joined wall run, end-post treatments resolve into **one shared post** at each panel junction, not two adjacent posts. The ceiling top is horizontal. A rising near corner in a perspective image is perspective, not a roof ridge. [house_high.png](house_high.png) is the authority for the level four-slab roof and square footprint. No upper-storey floor would need to bridge a mound, seam ridge or change in level.

## Solid wall — outside and inside

Best references: [piece_wall.png](piece_wall.png) for volume and outer bindings; [piece_wall_inside.png](piece_wall_inside.png) for the reverse construction; [details.png](details.png), upper-right, for the post/rod joint.

A sparse pole frame carries the same reed packs through the wall thickness. Hang the lower row first, then the upper row over its heads. The outside presents generous shallow broom bellies and pale free tips. The inside shows the calmer, compressed backs, smaller loose ends and the retaining switches. Do not build a second independent thick thatch wall on the inside.

The exposed poles stand proud of the compressed reed face while remaining part of the wall envelope. Local gaps between clumps are shaded hollows backed by reed, not holes through the building.

Best references: [piece_door.png](piece_door.png), left view exterior and right view interior; [details.png](details.png), lower-right, for the installed hinges.

The 1.40 × 2.30 × 0.08 m leaf uses a light pole perimeter holding fine, vertical reed infill. Its construction is much thinner and quieter than the wall skirts.

| --- | --- |

| Infill | Approximately 2–3 cm compressed vertical reeds, tied between retaining members. No wall-style two-tier skirt. |
| Crossbars | 3–4 cm diameter, at approximately 0.40 and 1.90 m above leaf bottom. |

| Leaf lashings | 5–8 mm cord at corners, bars and brace contacts. |

The loop ears on the isolated leaf are its unattached state. In the installed detail, cord visibly crosses the gap, wraps behind the jamb, and returns around the stile. Do not model floating rope handles that never touch the jamb. Preserve flexible loop geometry through the quarter-turn swing. The opening and leaf share the specified nominal dimensions; any tiny fitting clearance belongs at the organic edges, not in a larger opening.

## Foundation

Best reference: [piece_foundation.png](piece_foundation.png); skirt-to-crib relationship in the upper-left of [details.png](details.png).

A low retaining crib holds packed earth. A compressed reed wearing mat covers the fill and finishes at the exact floor datum. This is a ground-supported platform, with no stilts or hollow deck underneath.

| Part | Size / assembly |
| --- | --- |
| Overall | 3.00 × 3.00 × 0.375 m. |
| Horizontal logs | Two rough 9–11 cm logs per edge, lower near ground and upper immediately below the floor. |
| Earth band | Approximately 12–16 cm visibly exposed between logs; compact but irregular, with small clods and recesses. |
| Stakes | 6–8 cm diameter, roughly 65–85 cm spacing. Exposed height about 34–35 cm, ending below the wearing surface. |
| Floor mat | 2–3 cm compressed horizontal reeds over compact earth. Sparse near-flush stitches; no loose wall bundles on top. |
| Lashings | 8–12 mm cord at stake/log intersections. |

All four foundation tops meet at Z = 0.375 m. Stake heads, upper log crowns and knots must not stand above that plane. Internal joins have no doubled raised rail, step or gap. Keep edge members inside the footprint so foundations can meet directly. The final isolated picture corrects the proud stake caps from the rejected first pass.

## Ceiling / flat roof

Best references: [piece_ceiling.png](piece_ceiling.png) above, [piece_ceiling_below.png](piece_ceiling_below.png) below, and the lower-left of [details.png](details.png) at the eave corner.

A thin branch frame carries dense horizontal reed packs and a compact walking skin. The soft eave comes from the outer reed tails. It is not a cap of wall bundles, and it introduces no slope to the top.

| Part | Size / assembly |
| --- | --- |
| Core module | 3.00 × 3.00 × 0.25 m including joists, reed mattress and top skin. |
| Joists | Seven per isolated module, 7–9 cm diameter, approximately 50 cm centres: two boundary members and five between. |
| Perimeter rails | 7–9 cm diameter, across the joist ends; lash the crossings and seat them within the 25 cm total depth. Do not add a full extra beam layer beneath. |
| Reed mattress | Roughly 14 cm compacted pack above/between the upper parts of the frame, with 2–3 cm compressed wearing skin. Fit frame, pack and skin to the 25 cm envelope rather than summing full crossing-member heights. |
| Underside stems | Run across the joists, bound into roughly 30–50 cm packs. |
| Joist bindings | Pale 6–10 mm cord, spaced about 35–50 cm along the joists, plus bindings at perimeter joints. |
| Top retaining switches | Approximately 1–2 cm, seated low in the compressed skin; near-flush stitches. |
| Exposed eave projection | 20–28 cm from wall, never over 30 cm. |
| Fringe droop | Approximately 12–22 cm below the top plane, with stray tips near/below the joist underside. |
| Eave corner | Rounded reed mass, roughly 15–25 cm corner radius in plan; unequal pale tips, shaded hollow, visible round endgrain underneath. |

The isolated ceiling displays all four exposed edges. On the assembled 2 × 2 roof, keep fringe **only on the external perimeter**. Remove the fringe and projecting joist-end treatment at internal joins; butt the core surfaces flush. Adjacent boundary members can share their join treatment. Preserve one continuous level 6 × 6 m walking plane with two shallow seam traces crossing at its centre.

[house_inside.png](house_inside.png) shows the calm horizontal reed underside, small branch joists and slab-boundary rails in context. Keep the ceiling dark under doorway-only illumination; the lighter isolated underside is for construction visibility.

## Low-detail shape and colour

[house_flat.png](house_flat.png) is the untextured flat-shaded **visual target**, not an exported mesh. Every bundle remains a separate irregular faceted broom volume with a narrow head, shallow belly and uneven pronged foot. Preserve the two tiers, upper overlap shadow, pale eave silhouette, exposed poles and low earth crib before retaining individual stems.

Give neighbouring bundles different body colours; vary their pale end zones independently. Hollow faces use darker olive-brown material. Poles, rods, lashings, tip zones and earth remain distinct geometry/material groups. Use flat face normals and solid face colours; no straw, bark or cord textures are needed for this study. Reduce the number of small tips before flattening the larger bundles into rectangular siding.

[house_far.png](house_far.png) is the distant field read: the golden mass, pale irregular eave, dark skirt overlap and frame remain identifiable after fine stem detail becomes small. Its roughly 50 m intent is a perspective concept camera, not a surveyed render.
