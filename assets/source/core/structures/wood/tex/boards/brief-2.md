The engineering is excellent: exact lines, exact nails, seamless, normals rebuilt exactly. Keep all of that. The look is not there yet. Looked at full size, it reads as a modern oak laminate floor, not as weatherboards a settler sawed and nailed to a timber frame:

1. **Too clean and too smooth.** The faces are a smooth, even, factory finish with cathedral veneer figure. Weatherboards are rough-sawn or roughly planed softwood: faint straight saw kerf marks across the grain in places (pit-saw or frame-saw, slightly irregular in spacing and angle), raised and torn grain, the soft earlywood weathered back so the latewood lines stand proud, small splits at board ends and round nails, a few pitch pockets. Less figure, more surface.
2. **Almost no relief.** In the lit previews the boards look flat; the normal map is nearly uniform. Give the wood real surface: latewood ridges and eroded earlywood (about 0.5 to 1 mm), saw marks (0.2 to 0.4 mm), splits and checks with depth, slightly cupped boards (a board face 1 to 2 mm lower in its middle than at its edges, varying board to board). A raking light should make the surface obvious.
3. **The nails are flat black squares.** Make them forged iron: a slightly domed, hammer-faceted head, base colour of dark iron (around sRGB 70 to 85, not black), a rim of rust, a small hammer bruise dented into the wood round some of them, and a thin dark rust streak running down the grain below a few, where rain carried it.
4. **Weathering in the right places.** Water runs down a weatherboard wall and drips off each lapped lower edge: the bottom couple of centimetres of each board slightly darker and greyer, a few drip stains, the top edge (sheltered under the board above) a little fresher. Some boards more sun-bleached than others. Keep it subtle: the overall read stays warm honey to mid-brown, mean colour still roughly sRGB (150, 108, 72).
5. **Fine detail must be crisp at 1:1.** In the native crops the fine grain is soft. At 2,048 a pixel is 1.5 mm, so fibres, kerf marks and splits should be sharp at that scale.
6. The tonal rhythm between boards is good. Keep it, perhaps a touch less orange and a touch more variety in grain character (a couple of boards with tighter straight grain, one or two with a knot cluster, one with a long shallow split).
