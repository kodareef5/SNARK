This is close. The colour, the board rhythm, the staggered joints and the gaps are right, and the engineering is excellent. Keep all of that. What would take it from a good texture to floorboards that have been walked on for years:

1. **Relief.** Under the lit previews the boards still look nearly flat. Weathered deck boards have the soft earlywood worn down so the latewood ridges stand up (0.5 to 1.2 mm), board edges rounded over by feet (the last 1 to 1.5 cm of each board falling 2 to 4 mm into the gap), slight cupping (board middles 1 to 2 mm low, varying), and the checks and splits with real depth. Use the 12 mm range.
2. **Nails.** They are flat grey squares. Make them forged iron heads, flush or a hair sunk, with a slightly irregular hammered outline, dark iron base colour (sRGB 60 to 80), a rusty ring and a dark stain bled into the grain round them, the wood round a few dented by the hammer.
3. **Gaps and joints.** Dirt and grit packed into the gaps and butt joints, not just a clean dark line: the gap edges ragged in places, a little lighter dust at the bottom of some, the board ends at joints more worn, darker and split.
4. **Wear.** Feet polish and lighten the raised grain; rain and dirt darken the low grain. Add that contrast within each board at the fibre scale (it is what makes old floorboards read as old). A few boot scuffs and darker water marks, subtle and not repeating obviously at 3 m.
5. **Fine detail crisp at 1:1.** A pixel is 1.5 mm. Grain, splits and dirt should be sharp at that scale.
