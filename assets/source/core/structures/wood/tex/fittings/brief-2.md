The engineering is excellent and the rivets and nail heads are good. I have seen it on the modelled pieces (straps across timber joints, door hinges, corner brackets). Two things to fix:

1. **The rust reads as camouflage.** Large saturated orange blotches over a flat grey read as a printed pattern. Old blackened wrought iron is mostly dark (about 80 to 85 percent of the area): blue-black to brown-black forge scale, a little variation, hammer facets visible in the base colour too, and a slightly lighter worn sheen on the high points and edges. Rust belongs where water sits: along the lower edge of each strap, round rivet and nail heads, in hammer pits and pitting, as a thin bloom in places, and a few small streaks. The last plate band may be rustier, still mostly iron. Iron base colour a touch darker, around sRGB 55 to 70. Rust a deeper brown-orange, less saturated.
2. **The end grain looks drawn.** The rings are too regular and too concentric, the colour too even. Real sawn ends of old squared timber: uneven ring widths, the pith off-centre and the rings flattened on one side, darker latewood lines, medullary rays in the oak-like ones, radial checks opening from the pith and from the edges, faint saw marks across the face, the whole face oxidised darker than fresh wood with a lighter band where the arris is worn. Darker overall, around sRGB (95, 66, 44), so it sits with the dark timber frame.
3. Make each strap band's edges read: a thin brighter worn line where the bevel catches light, a dark line where two bands meet.
