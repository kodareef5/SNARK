The engineering is excellent: exact bands, seamless wraps, normals rebuilt exactly, a reproducible build. Keep all of that. The look is not there yet. Full size, it reads as smooth stained oak veneer, a laminate floor, not as heavy squared timbers that hold up a house:

1. **No hewing.** The bands called "hewn" look like the sawn ones. A hewn timber face has broad shallow scallops from the adze or broad-axe, each about 8 to 20 cm long along the grain and the full width of the face, overlapping like fish scales, 2 to 5 mm deep at their edges, the grain and colour breaking a little at each scallop edge, with a slightly sheared, torn texture where the axe split fibres. It must be obvious under a raking light and visible in the base colour as subtle tone shifts at the scallop edges.
2. **Sawn faces need saw marks.** Pit-sawn and frame-sawn timbers show straight parallel kerf marks across the grain, slightly irregular in spacing (5 to 15 mm) and angle, 0.3 to 0.6 mm deep, catching light.
3. **Checks need to be real.** Drying checks in heavy timber are long dark cracks along the grain, 3 to 8 mm wide at their widest, tapering out at both ends, several mm deep, with fibre texture on their walls. Two or three bands should have one you can see from a few metres. In the current maps they are faint lines.
4. **Too smooth and too uniform.** It is old structural wood: raised weathered grain, a slightly furry dry surface on the weathered bands, the odd bruise, a few dark iron stains where a nail or strap was, slight waney edges on one or two bands (bark-side rounding at the arris, a bit darker). The surface relief, which the 16 mm range allows, should actually be used: the relief-only lit previews currently show almost nothing.
5. **Fine detail crisp at 1:1.** A pixel is 1.5 mm; fibres, kerf marks and check walls should be sharp at that scale, not soft.
6. **Colour.** The darkness is right. Make it a little less red-orange and a little more of an aged brown with some greying on the weathered bands, still a rich warm dark brown overall, mean roughly sRGB (104, 72, 48). Some band-to-band variety in tone as now.
