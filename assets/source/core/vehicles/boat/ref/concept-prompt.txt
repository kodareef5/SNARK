Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: original production design study for SNARK, a carefully made stylized survival game.
Primary request: design a rugged, unarmed 6.8 metre open hard-hull utility motorboat, 2.6 metre beam, one original outboard engine, four real independent forward-facing upholstered seats in two rows of two. Seats and feet need generous room. The helm is at the front left from the pilot's perspective, with a low dark instrument bridge, a small steering wheel and throttle, a broad clear two-pane windscreen. There is NO cabin roof or canopy. Hull has a strong continuous warped V keel, sloping bow and welded chines, raised gunwales with a continuous black rubber rub rail, compact handrails, a bow cargo locker with a latching lid, aft open work deck, a stern-side swim ladder safely offset from the engine, and proper engine well and rigging. An expensive working craft, maintained and functional, with subtle scuffs and salt exposure.
Composition: one coherent landscape design sheet on warm light grey paper. Main image large three-quarter view from elevated front-left showing bow, four seats, interior, helm, motor and ladder. Small inset true side profile, small inset top view showing clear four-seat arrangement, and small original rescue and police colourway swatches of this same hull. Main craft neutral warm ivory paint, muted teal stripe, dark gunwales, grey-green upholstery, brushed dull metal rails and charcoal outboard. Rescue swatch muted orange-white, police dark navy-white. Only small UTILITY / RESCUE / POLICE lettering, no real brands or agency marks. Balanced useful industrial detailing, no excessive greebles.
Style: polished original 3D game concept, readable soft faceting and bevels, believable construction and material separation, restrained painterly surface detail, excellent silhouette at distance. Soft studio lighting, no dramatic glow. A broad practical bow, never an inflatable tube boat. All parts mechanically connected. No weapons, gun mounts, people, water scene, speed streaks, trailer, duplicate engines, cabin roof, logos or copied manufacturer design. The sheet is visual design evidence; do not print dimensions or dense technical annotations.
