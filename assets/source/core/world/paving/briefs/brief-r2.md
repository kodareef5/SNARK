# Round two

I have looked at your set in my own sheets (a 3 by 3 tiling, a half-offset, 12 and 40 by 40 fields, and 1:1 crops lit from the height by a low and a high sun). It is technically right: it tiles, nothing repeats, the mean is on target and the layers agree. Keep all of that.

It is not yet believable at arm's length, which is the bar: put a 1:1 crop of your base colour beside a photograph of the real surface at 2 m, and they should be hard to tell apart; the same for a low-sun render. What falls short is below.

Use the image tool for what a photograph has and a procedure lacks: generate photographic sources of the real material under flat overcast light, top-down, and build the tile from them (crop, quilt and blend several generations, or lay generated elements into the 2048 master), keeping the homogeneity you have now. Height must still come from shape: separate what you paint into its parts (stones and binder, blades and soil, flakes and render, slabs and joints) and model the relief of each part, rather than reading brightness as height. Write the new maps over the old ones, redo your checks, and add a short "Round two" section to REPORT.md saying what changed.

## Paving

The slabs are identical grey squares with thin dark lines; at 3 by 3 the field reads as graph paper. Give each slab its own subtle tone (a few percent warmer or cooler, lighter or darker), visible fine aggregate in the faces, worn and chipped arrises on a few corners, joints 8 to 10 mm with darker grit and paler sand filling them to an irregular level, a bevel that catches a low sun, and a faint even dark grime along the joints. Keep the 500 mm grid exact and the module [0.25, 0.25].
