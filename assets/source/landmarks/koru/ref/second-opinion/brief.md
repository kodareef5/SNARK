# A second opinion: can the two pictures of each pair be told apart?

`pairs-blind.png` holds fourteen pairs of crops from a web game's renderer (WebGL 2, four samples a pixel) of a 125 m
three-masted schooner seen abeam at 14:00, the first eight on its high setting and the last six on medium. The two
pictures of a pair were taken from two distances a few per cent apart, either side of a distance where the renderer
changes how it draws part of the ship: its hull and decks, or its standing rigging, the wires from the masts. The nearer
picture has been made smaller, its pixels averaged as light, to the scale of the farther one, so that both show the ship
at one size; both are then enlarged three times, each pixel a block. Which of A and B is the nearer was chosen by a
coin's toss for each pair.

Each pair has four crops: A sky and B sky, the upper rigging against the sky, and A land and B land, the forestays'
feet against the green land behind the ship, and its bow.

The bar: someone looking closely cannot say which of a pair is which from how the ship is drawn. The wires are the same
wires in both, as thick and as strong, and continuous lines, not dots or dashes; nothing appears, vanishes, thickens,
darkens or pales between them; the masts and the hull are alike. Differences that come only from the averaging (a
picture a little softer), from clouds that moved between the two shots, or from the camera standing a few centimetres
higher, do not count.

For each pair, please give:

1. Every difference you see between A and B in the rigging, the masts or the hull beyond those allowed above, and where
   it is.
2. Your guess at which of A and B is the farther, how sure you are (50% for a coin's toss, up to 100%), and what you base
   it on.
3. Any wire broken into dots or dashes in either picture.

Then, over all of them: are the hand-overs invisible? And does anything about the rigging, at any of these distances,
look wrong for a real ship's wire rigging seen in daylight?

Work from the picture alone; there is nothing else here to read. Change no file but your report. Write the report as
plain text, a short section for each pair and one at the end.
