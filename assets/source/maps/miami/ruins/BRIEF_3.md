## What round one left, most visible first

1. **Bridges, causeways, the elevated railways and the piers.** New data: `assets/source/maps/miami/blocks/ways.json` holds the full shape of every bridge, viaduct, pier and breakwater from OpenStreetMap, in the map's metres east and north: 422 road bridges (61 km), 113 rail bridges including the Metromover and the Metrorail (46 km), 243 piers (15 km), 29 breakwaters and 86 bridge outlines, each with its name, class, lanes, layer and length where tagged. Round one had a point for each and built none. Build them: decks on piers with the water running beneath, so the bay is one water again; the causeways' earth banks cut from the ground where a span crosses water (that is the ground builder's part, `tools/mapworld/ground.ts`, yours to change); the four authored broken spans still broken; the Metromover's loop through downtown and the Metrorail as a ruined viaduct a player can walk and fall from; piers and breakwaters reaching into the bay. Deck heights are not in the data: settle them from the class, the layer tag, the span and the ground at each end, keep boats' passages in mind for play, and record what you settled. A bridge is a chokepoint and a landmark in this kind of game; treat it as one.
2. **Compound footprints.** Your worst picture: a 38,453 m2 campus built as one flat stack. Split the largest merged footprints into wings with their own heights, cores and entrances. More than one core and more than one way in for anything large.
3. **Districts that read apart at a glance.** In the game the city is one concrete grey. The ground already colours districts; the buildings should too, by finish and by form: pastel stucco and low brows on the beach, pale stone and glass-blue infill downtown and in Brickell, terracotta and painted block in Little Havana and Overtown, painted warehouse walls in Wynwood, rust and steel at the port, white on the islands. Enough solid wall that a block has colour, not only a grey frame.
4. **Play.** The two stair routes that start under water (decide in the generator: do not begin a stair below the sea, or give it a dry way in). Ways in from the street that a player finds without hunting. A way off a roof other than the stair. Fewer trees where they hide low buildings and the port's yard from the water; the wood should grow through the city, not bury it.
5. **Heights that conflict.** The 73 height against storey conflicts and the tall named places built low (the SLS at 21 m is the known one): review them against the labels and the pictures you can open, correct the label data where you can show why, and list each correction.
