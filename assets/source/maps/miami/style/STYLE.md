# The drowned atlas — original Miami map style

An explorer's carefully observed field atlas, on pale warm rag paper. Quiet, luminous washes and crisp economical pen strokes; no sepia fog, heavy vignette or ornamental frame. Geometry is planar, north up. Tiny ruin symbols stay inside their real footprints.

## Symbols and scale

Sea has no invented islands or decorative compass. Shallows are derived from the true coast (a visual depth convention, not surveyed bathymetry). Beaches follow mapped low bare ground. Mangroves occupy actual vegetation/derived succession polygons. Swamp is sparse reed ticks, never dense fuzz. Building marks are clipped to true footprints: roofless limestone shells, courtyard voids, directional wall hatching. Roads are exact centerline corridors; causeways retain the real alignment. Any fictional break must also be recorded in a world-change layer.

Zoom 0: districts, bay and islands read immediately; main avenues and large ruin clusters. Zoom 1: blocks, causeways, canals and beach ribbons. Zoom 2: individual structures and grove edges. Zoom 3: roofless walls, small alleys, bridge spans, roots, sand ripples and submerged debris. Use the same pigment family and apparent ink weight at every zoom, with richer symbols at closer scales.

Never put lettering, a grid, compass, neatline, legend or page edge into a game tile. The separate style sheet may have sample captions. No mountainous relief: Miami is low and flat. No gratuitous noise or repeated decorative stamps.

## Round two: scale and paper take priority

The first finish lost the paper and forced all 11,919 footprints into every scale. The new overview and district originals use the same original style sheet with these stronger decisions:

- **z0:** main avenues/causeways only; districts described by ivory intervals, loose wash and directional hatching. A small pictorial skyline, port sheds/cranes, courtyard ruins and beach hotels are representative district symbols. Their projected heights are illustrative; do not read a symbol's drawn silhouette as a surveyed building footprint.
- **z1:** spaced source-building anchors and groups of ruins, more canals and park detail, still generous unpainted ground. Do not bring back the complete minor-road or house mesh.
- **z2/z3:** the actual footprint fabric returns. Keep independently painted roofless rooms and vegetation; differentiate warehouse trusses, pale beach galleries, darker downtown masonry and low residential shells.
- **Substrate:** exposed ivory, approximately #EDE1C6, with original rag tooth. The earlier land-wash anchor is a transparent pigment, never an opaque fill across all land. Quiet teal water may show paper tooth too.
- **Coast:** a geographically constrained hand, with variable pressure, short pen lifts, doubled quay strokes, almost no outline across a beach, and coast-following jade shallows. Do not encode all materials into rigid RGB categories merely to obtain perfect colour-classification scores.
- **Narrative:** actual saved missing spans and flooded streets stay in the rendering. Symbols communicate the drowned district at far scales; exact corridors and open rooms take over close up. Never add cranes to residential islands or invented wrecks to empty water.

`paper-study.png` is the first round-two art direction study, retained because it was an input to the accepted overview. Its geometry is not authoritative. Its elastic finishing experiment was rejected for warping architecture. The production log records the subsequent corrections.

## Round three: the bay has a geography of its own

Replace the shore-distance glow with hydrographic wash guided by NOAA's coastal
DEM: slate-blue port cuts and turning areas, the narrow western waterway,
irregular jade southern flats and submerged eastern bands. A quay may meet
deep water directly. Avoid an identical soft rim around each island. Keep
marker-readable midtones, real brush edges and rag tooth, with fewer, larger
water strokes at distant scales. Each closer view gets its own marks.

Preserve accepted district paint. Selected far landmarks have distinct
silhouettes: banyan and circular arcade, unequal drowned towers with a broken
link, reed-filled oval bowl, stepped Deco gallery, and three mangrove-swallowed
tanks. They are generalized future-district pictures, not new surveyed
footprints. Native room plans remain authoritative at z2/z3. The four saved
wrecks have individual interiors without changing their positions.

Mainland quays keep their real alignments with a living pressure line, bounded
subpixel deviations and pen lifts. No mechanical parallel dash. Union water
polygons before tracing: database record boundaries are not coastlines.
