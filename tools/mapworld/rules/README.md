# Shared ruin rules

Every map inherits `default.json`. Its `ruins/vocabulary.json` extends objects by key, including individual forms, assemblies and damage profiles. Lists with an `id` extend by id in their original order; new ids append. An override with `enabled: false` removes an inherited id. Other lists replace, so a city's district palette, an assembly's parts and a choice's allowed kinds remain explicit.

A fourth city supplies its categories, recipes, districts, budget, labels and places. It does not copy `forms`, `choices`, `assemblies`, `street`, `damage`, `interiors`, `growth` or `harbours`, which stocks its marinas and piers with vessels and wrecks (`tools/mapworld/README.md`, "Boats"). Named compositions remain in each city's vocabulary. For a city made from public data, `tools/mapdata/vocabulary.py` writes such a vocabulary, and drops the sections a vocabulary written before this library had copied. `event.travel` is an engine-space vector towards which the surge travels, not a compass bearing. Engine x points west and z north.

A form chooses shell openings, interior, front assembly, roof, roof marker, plan and optional terrace depth in floors. A choice matches label kind, recipe, size, height, neighbours, shoreline distance, trades, era, district or name. It selects a form and finish. The event field chooses the failure family by exposure band and shared spatial cell, with measured buildings upstream providing shelter. Height still comes from labels. This is an authored event, not a hydrodynamic reconstruction.

An assembly lists `[shape, u, y, v, finish, optional yaw]` in metres and degrees. `finish` uses the district finish. Dimensions and material properties live in `content/ruins.ts`. Add a large part there only if an existing part cannot express it economically. Roof markers and front assemblies use the same library as street remnants. `passage` lists local `[u,v]` waypoints through a street assembly; placement rejects obstacles along that passage, reserves it from later props and plants, and the room walk verifies it in the packed map. A part `["car", u, 0, v, family, yaw]` is one of the compact's wrecks standing on its own ground, its nose along `v` turned by `yaw` (90 puts it along `u`); `street.cars.looks[family]` weighs its looks, and `street.cars` says the rest (its `about`). A street assembly with `kerb` is set that far in from the kerb.

`growth.zones` overrides the named resource zones. Candidates are frozen as listed resource nodes only after their actual listed collision form clears structures, movement paths and spawn points. `growth.canopy` supplies estimated visual radius and height, scaled with the node's form, to keep leaves out of tested routes as well. `growth.lowAroundPlace` selects low vegetation within a radius of a public place kind, such as a crane yard. Roof planters reuse the game's existing low creeper mesh as statics: the map format gives resource nodes no elevation. `sightlines` in the city vocabulary reserve views towards named compositions against props and tall growth. They do not demolish existing footprint buildings.

Assemblies with role `roof identity` stay together when route clearance removes a part. A clock or sign must not retain a floating cap after its support is cut away. This is an assembly rule, not a structural solver for the entire building.

Sloped parts are declared in `content/ruins.ts`, using the engine's `stairFlight`, `pitchedRoof`, `leaning` and `slopedSlab` helpers. Their holding bounds are derived from the same convex shape used for collision. `damage.<mode>.plates` chooses the dropped-floor palette and `damage.facade.front` chooses the fallen window frame. Pitched roofs merge complete floor-grid rectangles before choosing a 4, 8 or 16 m part. A flight rises 4 m over 5.5 m, at 36.03 degrees; its landing starts exactly at its top edge. Rail access uses 4 m ramp modules whose rises are quarter metres, never steeper than 36.87 degrees.

`circulation` chooses ordinary, tower and tall-tower floor limits and their height thresholds. The city's budget remains the upper limit. A core above eight floors needs a neighbouring second stair, with a refuge circuit connecting both; a narrower footprint keeps its lower core. Both stairs and both directions of the alternate route are walked in the packed city. `growth.canopyBase` gives the fraction of estimated crown height below which only trunk collision is checked. Above it, the estimated crown must also clear building volumes, including roofs. Roof gardens use low shrubs so an unmeasured full-size mesh cannot grow through the next floor.

The generated `remnants.json` records each street assembly's stable placement id, assembly id, origin and the facing of its frame. This inventory is regenerated, not committed; `parked.json`, the drivable cars, is.

Circulation clearance cuts obstructing large floors into smaller tiles around a stair opening, retaining the surrounding support. Approaches beside other footprints are paved, and hill approaches use shallow ramps. Terrain grading and street passages respect each other's floor levels. `verify.ts` checks the packed file's chunk cap as well as movement; `chunks.json` records the busiest chunk's pieces by kind. `slopes.json` lists reachable heights, refuges and the sloped parts actually used.

To add a rule, add one choice with a stable id and narrow conditions, or extend one form or assembly. To add a place, add its footprint id to the map's `ruins/places.json` and its composition to that map's vocabulary. Build with `bun tools/mapworld/build.ts <map>`, then `bun tools/mapworld/verify.ts var/maps/<map>.snmap`. `proof-views.ts`, `views.ts` and `ruin-report.ts` produce the gallery and records. All selections and spatial decisions are seeded; no wall clock is used in the map bytes.

A form's `facade` gives a building its kind from across the road: the module an 8 m bay's outer wall wears on the ground floor, on the floors and on the top storey, and four storeys at once above the playable floors, the same in every bay of a storey so the bay and the storey repeat through every whole floor. Flats (`apartment`, `hotel`) wear paired balconies over a darker solid base under a roofed top; shops a sign band over a dark display and a door, a shutter down in one bay of four, windows to the rooms above and a parapet; garages open decks behind weathered spandrels, a clearance bar where cars go in, a ramp seen through every third front bay and light poles on the top deck; offices spandrel bands and panes. With `ends` a long block's short ends are blank. The modules are content/ruins.ts `RUIN_FACADES`, each one piece walked as a wall and drawn with its parts in their own colours. A wall onto a fallen bay keeps no module: its slabs end at the break, whole with a bevelled lip, spalled to the aggregate or snapped with bent bars, and one column of the failure stands with its broken head over the slab it held, broken along the same teeth (`RUIN_BREAKS`). Which kind a footprint is comes from its labels' use and height as before; which bays fail, which end each slab has and where the shutter is are seeded by the footprint, so a build makes the same building every time.

`street.walks` gives a city's streets their shape: along each road of the listed classes, on each side, an 8 m walk at curb height (0.15 m, 2.4 m deep, its curb's top pale) where the ground beside the road is open and level and nothing stands, clear of junctions, with a gutter on one 32 m run in `gutterEvery`, a storm drain where a gutter runs lowest and a utility box on one walk in `boxEvery`. A way in to a building keeps its corridor, so the curb is cut where a driveway or a door's path meets the road, and a run ends there and at a junction in a curb ramp (1.8 m, 1 in 12) where the ramp stands off the road it meets; elsewhere its curb stays full height. No walk, ramp or drain stands on a bridge's way on (its lanes and a metre beside them, 24 m out over the approach) or on the road ground graded to one, so the driving courses there meet no curb. The curb is solid: a body steps up it and a wheel rolls over it. A crossing over the road cuts the curb as a way in does. `street.walks.returns` rounds the corners where two streets of its `classes` meet, one of them `walked` (`corners.ts`): the radius at the curb's face by the wider street's half width (`radius`, the first `[most half width, radius]` it is within), shrunk where the streets leave less room down to `least`, at corner angles within `angles`; the ground is painted street outside the arc and sidewalk behind it. Within `within` degrees of a right angle a curb return of 4, 6 or 8 m (`content/curbs.ts`, its ramp at the middle of its arc) stands there where its ground is open and level and nothing stands on its arc, and the runs on its two kerbs begin at its ends; a run between two returns leaves the metres it does not fill at its first break, or cuts its middle slot with a ramp each side. The kit beside the facades is placed by the layout itself: a lobby's frame and canopy at its way in, a railed balcony beside a failure, a downpipe on a low building's end wall, a rubble wedge under a snapped first floor.

A ruin's outline is broken where it broke. Where the storey above a wall broke away (a bay that failed, or a building that lost its top) the wall's top is one of four broken crowns, stepping, sloping and notched down to the slab with bars standing out of it; a whole top has lost a stretch of its parapet in one bay of four. A tall building (eight storeys or more) has a top by its kind, chosen by its seed: its edge storeys set back one or two, a lift overrun and stair head over its cores, or a corbelled cornice round its roof; where it failed, its top storeys are bitten out round the failure's corner with broken tops and sagging slabs. Its cores keep their height and a tile by a core keeps its playable storeys, so no route inside changes. A bay's slab ends at a break whole, spalled, snapped or sagging into the gap. What fell lies heaped against the wall it fell from, and along the foot of a standing wall in one bay of four (one of two beside a failure, never at a way in), with weeds at its toe; a walk runs under its toe. Beside a failure a panel of the wall hangs from one fixing and a column of a bay that fell stands out of plumb; vines hang from broken slab ends and from balconies, and a tuft roots on one broken roof in ten. Each of these is a piece of content/ruins.ts `RUIN_BREAKS`, its convex parts walked as drawn, chosen by the footprint's seed.
