# Sound: hand-off

Branch `r9/sound`. The work moves from an M5 Mac with 24 GB to a Linux machine with an i9, 64 GB and an RTX 4070
(12 GB). Nothing here is merged; nothing merges until the owner has listened.

The bar: a player with eyes shut knows a dodo is near, which way, and whether it is calm or frightened, and nothing
sounds like a placeholder.

## Built and working

The engine path is done and tested headless (`planning/architecture/sound.md` describes it):

- `shared/pack/sound.ts`, `shared/pack/rice.ts`: a sound is `<owner>/sound/<name>` (`core:dodo/sound/call`), takes
  coded losslessly with FLAC's fixed predictors and Rice residuals, written here.
- `tools/pack/sound.ts`: `bun run build` reads each asset's `sound/sounds.json`, cuts, levels, resamples to 32 kHz and
  codes every take. The dodo's 49 takes come to 38.9 s in 1.27 MB (51% of 16-bit), coded in 0.2 s.
- `client/core/sound/`: the library (fetched beside meshes, decoded a take a frame, 48 MB budget), what creatures are
  heard doing from the state they already replicate (startle, landing, pain, peck, going down, the call idle,
  footfalls by gait phase and ground, death), and 32 voices with limits by kind and sound.
- `client/sound/`: the browser's part (Web Audio, HRTF panner, shared with voice) and the listening page.
- `spawn <species> [level] [count]` puts a flock ahead. `bun run check` passes; `bun run bench` has a Sound section.

How to hear it, from a checkout of the branch:

```
bun install --frozen-lockfile
PORT=8790 bun tools/dev.ts         # builds client/dist first: several minutes for the textures
```

- http://localhost:8790/sounds.html, the sound board: every owner, then every occasion it has, with what sets it
  off in play and how this build hears it (its own takes, a stand-in, a shared sound, or no take yet), each take
  playable with its file, length and recipe, and links to each owner's brief and recipes. It is an admin's page:
  open on a `--dev` server (as `tools/dev.ts` runs), elsewhere only with `ServerAdminPassword` at the browser's
  sign-in. The "not all its own" box lists what is still to make.
- http://localhost:8790/: click in, open the console with the backquote or Tab, type `enablecheats`, then
  `spawn dodo 1 40`, walk among them and strike one. `sound` lists the sounds, `sound call` plays one three metres
  ahead, `sound from moss` (or `stable`, `all`) chooses whose takes the game plays. The settings (O) have
  "Sound volume". The plaza is stone, the open ground grass, the pool's west rim and the south shore sand.

Not yet done with these takes: nobody has opened either page in a browser, and `bun run probe` has not run (its
self-check asks for the call and fails unless it is heard). Run the probe first.

## The method so far

Sounds are generated by local audio models; trimming, levelling and variation are our own tools.

| | Stable Audio 3.0 Small SFX | MOSS-SoundEffect v2.0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Run | MLX port in `Stability-AI/stable-audio-3` at 790793b8, weights `stabilityai/stable-audio-3-optimized` at 30cb2644; DiT fp16, 8 steps, cfg 1 | MLX `moss-sfx-mlx` (xocialize) at 4e8e3e36; weights `mlx-community/MOSS-SoundEffect-v2.0-bf16`, DiT mostly 4-bit (`-4bit`); upstream `OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-SoundEffect-v2.0` |
| Memory on the Mac | 1.8 GB peak | stock pipeline 14 GB; staged (encoder, DiT, VAE one at a time) 4.5 GB, so clips of 9 s at most |
| Time a take | about 1 s | 190 to 260 s |
| Licence | Stability AI Community: free under US$1M revenue with registration; outputs ours | Apache 2.0 |
| Takes kept | call 5, startled 4, hurt 4, peck 5, land 4, 6 steps a ground | call 5, startled 4 |

By measurement (spectrograms in `ref/sound/`, `voices.png` for calm against frightened): kept calls sit at 90 to
490 Hz, kept squawks at 1 to 2 kHz, so calm and frightened are far apart. MOSS gave the cleaner, more animal calls
and squawks; Stable gave clean, fast foley and steps but little variety between seeds, clipped noise at some
lengths (1.2, 1.5, 2.5 s; whole seconds were clean), and broadband noise for any prompt with "animal vocalisation".
The pick so far: MOSS for voices, Stable for foley, steps and pain; the owner decides the voices by ear. Prompts
that worked are in `recipes.json` (Stable: "TrackType: SFX, ..." in whole seconds; MOSS: 8 s asking for a few
calls with pauses). Prompts that missed: "mumble" (rumble from Stable, a human-sounding mumble from MOSS),
"chirrup" (small-bird chirps), a squawk with wing flaps (thumps only), and MOSS "panic" (buried in wing noise).

## v1: sound for the whole game (2026-11-15)

The design and catalogue are in `planning/reference/round9-sound-design.md`, the engine as built in
`planning/architecture/sound.md`, and ARK's practice in `planning/reference/round9-ark-audio-research.md`.

**In this branch.**
- The catalogue: `shared/sound/cues.ts`.
- The engine and its producers: `client/core/sound/`.
- The sound board: `sounds.html`.
- `bun run sounds`.
- Every brief: one per owner under `assets/source/core/**/sound/brief.json`. They hold 255 slots, all prompted.

**Sounds accepted.**
- 13 creatures: parasaur, raptor, rex, trike, stego, pteranodon, megalodon, sabertooth, direwolf, compy, dilophosaur,
  spider and mammoth, besides the dodo. Their content definitions do not exist yet; their sounds are found by id
  the day they do.
- The survivor's voice, male and female.
- `core:body` (water, thumps).
- `core:node` (felled, crumbled, picked).

**Still to slot in.** These are generating now, from the committed briefs, and need no code:
- `core:surface` (steps, landings and impacts by ground and material);
- `core:weapon`;
- `core:structure`;
- `core:ui`;
- `core:world` (beds, shore, wind, water).

**Making a brief's sounds.** These commands run from the worktree root:
```
tools/sound/.venv/bin/python -W ignore tools/sound/generate.py <brief.json>... --model moss
tools/sound/.venv/bin/python -W ignore tools/sound/review.py <brief.json> --out tools/sound/.work/<asset>/review
tools/sound/.venv/bin/python -W ignore tools/sound/pick.py <brief.json>
tools/sound/.venv/bin/python -W ignore tools/sound/accept.py <brief.json> tools/sound/.work/<asset>/picks.json --replace
bun run sounds && bun run build
```

**How takes are picked and stored.**
- `pick.py` picks by measure:
  - the loudest clean events, one per file first;
  - a seamless stretch for beds;
  - the whole file for stingers.
- `accept.py` stores each pick as a cut, mono 24-bit, with its recipe.

**Nobody has listened to the v1 takes yet; the owner listens before merging.** The probe passes when its page boots
within 30 s. On this machine under load it boots in 30 to 60 s; it passed with a longer wait.

## On the Linux machine (2026-11-15)

The machine has an RTX 4070 Laptop GPU with 8 GB, not 12 GB, and 62 GB of memory shared with other agents' jobs.

- `r9/sound` is merged with main locally (bfb3a0d). The only conflict was `tools/bench.ts`, which keeps both
  sections. `bun run check` passes on Linux. Before the merge it failed the two `Math.hypot` tests that main fixed.
- `bun run probe` passes, and the self-check hears the call (peak 0.24). It is flaky here: headless Chrome has no
  GPU and falls back to SwiftShader, and the page sometimes boots after the probe's 30 s limit. One run of two
  timed out.
- `tools/sound/` runs on Linux with `SOUND_BACKEND=torch` (the default off a Mac). Only MOSS is ported:
  `tools/sound/setup.sh`, then `generate.py <brief> --model moss`.
  - It runs upstream's own code in three stages: text encoder, DiT, VAE. The maths is the same as
    `MossSoundEffectPipeline`, and so is the full 30 s decode.
  - On this GPU a take takes 32 s after a first one that compiles (about 100 s). GPU peak 5.8 GB, process peak
    7.2 GB.
  - A seed matches upstream's own `pipe()` at correlation 0.99995. The compiled DiT is not bit-exact from one
    process to the next (max sample difference 0.02), so `remake.py` reports these takes as close, not identical.
  - Upstream's CPU offload works too, but it holds 17 GB of memory and pushed other jobs into swap. Don't use it.
  - Upstream ships the DiT in fp32 under bf16 autocast. Eager mode runs out of 8 GB, because autocast keeps a bf16
    copy of every weight. Leave `torch.compile` on.
- Takes from torch are named `moss-pt-<seed>`, so they never replace an MLX `moss-<seed>`. Briefs may set
  `settings` and `takes_<model>-pt` for them under `moss-pt`.
- Candidates made here, none listened to yet. Sheets: `review.py <brief> --out tools/sound/.work/core/creatures/dodo/review-pt`.
  Without `--out`, review would overwrite the Mac's committed sheets in `ref/sound/` with only this machine's
  candidates.
  - hurt `yelps-m` 501 to 504: 3 or 4 separate squawks at 1 to 3 kHz in each. These are the first MOSS pain takes
    that are not noise.
  - land `drops-m` 501 to 504: 3 or 4 separate thumps in each.
  - peck `wood-m` 601 to 604: clean, separate knocks. Peck `pecks-m`: 502 and 504 are clean; 501 and 503 have a
    noisy floor and many small events.
  - steps `walk` 1 to 4 on each ground: weak, as on the Mac. Stone 0001 has a regular stride. Sand is mostly a noise
    bed, and sand 0003 has hard silences. The Stable steps already accepted remain the better ones.
  - call `coo` 1 and 2: a clear harmonic coo over a low rumble.
- Stable Audio 3 is not ported. `stabilityai/stable-audio-3-small-sfx` and `-medium` are gated, and this machine's
  Hugging Face token has no access until the owner accepts their terms. Stable's PyTorch package pins torch 2.7.1,
  transformers 5.8 or newer and numpy 2.2 or newer. MOSS's pins conflict with those, so Stable needs its own venv
  (`.venv-stable`, already git-ignored).
- The owner listened to the CUDA candidates and liked them. Eighteen events from them are accepted. The picks were
  measured, not hand-picked: one event per file first, loudest first, within a length for the kind of sound. Files
  flagged with a noisy floor were skipped, and so were impacts over a steady tone. The picks file is
  `tools/sound/.work/core/creatures/dodo/picks-pt.json` (not committed). The dodo now has 67 takes: call 12, and
  every other sound 8, except sand steps at 7. Three of the four sand candidates had a noisy floor.
- Accepted takes are stored as cuts (`tools/sound/cut.py`): the event plus 50 ms each side, as mono 24-bit PCM at the
  model's rate. This is the same mono the build makes. From 16-bit sources the build reads exactly the same samples
  (checked on all 49 older takes); from float it is within 2^-24. The recipe keeps the source's sha256 and the
  cut in frames. `remake.py` remakes each source once, cuts it and compares. Takes went from 19.3 MB (32 whole files)
  to 7.9 MB (67 cuts). The game's pack holds 51.2 s in 1.62 MB.
- `accept.py` now adds picks to the takes already accepted (`--replace` starts a slot afresh), and `--compact` cuts
  any take still stored whole.

## What the new machine changes (the plan written on the Mac)

With 12 GB of GPU and 64 GB of memory the full models are open again. Try first:

1. MOSS-SoundEffect v2.0 in PyTorch on CUDA, bf16, from OpenMOSS's own package (`moss_soundeffect_v2` in
   `OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS`, installed with its CUDA extra), with the full 30 s decode; free the text encoder after
   encoding if 12 GB is tight; batch seeds. Make the missing peck, landing and step takes, and more pain.
2. Stable Audio 3 in PyTorch (`stable_audio_3`) on CUDA: retest the lengths that broke on MLX, then Medium (needs
   Flash Attention 2) and the base checkpoints with cfg and negative prompts for more variety. The gated repositories
   need the owner to accept their terms on a Hugging Face account.
3. Port `tools/sound/` to Linux: a CUDA `requirements.txt`, `flock` for the render lock, `time -v`. Takes remade on
   CUDA will be close to the MLX ones, not identical; keep each take's real model, revision and settings in its recipe.

## Rules that still hold

- Every accepted take keeps its prompt, seed, model, repository and revision, settings and licence beside it
  (`recipes.json`), with a script that makes it again. Accepted takes are committed; candidates are not.
- No outside encoder in `bun run build`: everything after the model is our own code. Tools on the side (ffmpeg, the
  models) are for making art only.
- No personal details in any prompt or request, and no game's audio as an input or reference.
- One heavy job at a time under the render lock, and no process holding a model between jobs.
- Nothing merges until the owner has listened.
